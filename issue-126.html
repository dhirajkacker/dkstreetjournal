El Niño Rain Lifted Klabin's Cost Per Tonne 12% as Suzano Cut Its Own and Raised Prices

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Two Brazilian eucalyptus producers reported three business days apart, and the wood in the ground separated them. Klabin's total cash cost per tonne sold rose to R$3,483 as El Niño rainfall disrupted forestry and forced purchases of standing timber, and its adjusted EBITDA margin narrowed six points to 33%. Suzano's pulp production cash cost fell 2% sequentially to R$825 a tonne while realized pulp fetched $591.

The two cost measures are not identical and their levels do not compare, but their slopes do, and the shares followed: Suzano up 8% in dollars over the past month, Klabin down 10% in reais, against an Ibovespa that moved 0.5%. The awkward part is that Klabin's price gain came from export pulp rather than from the contracted Brazilian packaging book meant to insulate it.

Klabin and Suzano both grow eucalyptus in Brazil, and both told investors this month what a tonne of it now costs them. The answers moved in opposite directions. Klabin's total cash cost per tonne sold, including scheduled maintenance downtime, rose 12% year on year to R$3,483, of which the company attributed about R$111 to extraordinary weather and geopolitical pressure. Suzano's pulp production cash cost ex-downtime fell 2% sequentially to R$825. The two numbers measure different things — one an integrated pulp, paper and conversion book, the other a pulp mill — so the level does not compare. The slope does.

Until these results the market treated both as hard-currency assets grown in Brazil. On the 26 October session when the Ibovespa fell 13.88%, Suzano's American depositary shares rose 2.1% and Klabin rose 3.2%, both on roughly four times normal volume. What the quarters showed is that only one of them is being paid for fibre on improving terms, and that Klabin's insulation from the pulp cycle works in both directions.

Rain in the plantations, not weakness in the boxes

Klabin's revenue rose 3% to R$5.57bn, yet adjusted EBITDA fell 12% to R$1.856bn and the margin dropped to 33% from 39%. The cause was physical: rainfall tied to El Niño disrupted forestry operations and forced higher wood inventories and purchases of standing timber, with chief executive Cristiano Teixeira saying the extra costs should mostly phase out by the second quarter of 2027.

Where the revenue came from matters more. Klabin's pulp line grew 6% to R$1.51bn on an 18% rise in dollar short-fibre prices, while corrugated-box revenue of R$1.7bn was flat because implemented price increases exactly offset a 2% volume decline to 245 thousand tonnes. More than 70% of the packaging area is contracted with brands including JBS, Nestlé, Unilever and Natura, and Brazilian corrugated shipments are forecast up only 1.6% to 2.0% this year. A contracted book in a 2% market defends price and delivers no growth.

It also carries R$24bn of net debt at 3.3x adjusted EBITDA, financed into a policy rate the central bank held at 13.75% on 4 November after five consecutive cuts. Below-the-line financing costs consumed most of the quarter's operating profit, and management says the end of the investment cycle should support deleveraging from 2027. On 6 August, a quarter before the cost miss, Teixeira told investors: "for the next quarters, you're going to see a significant free cash flow generation in the company." The trailing free-cash-flow yield is 2.5%, and the shares carry 6.09x trailing EV/EBITDA and 2.15x book.

Suzano announced a price and collected it

Suzano's pulp segment earned R$4.8bn of EBITDA at a 50% margin on 3.19m tonnes at $591 a tonne. At the post-election rate near R$5.60 to the dollar that realization is roughly R$3,310 a tonne against R$825 of production cash cost. Two $20-a-tonne Asian increases landed, the second effective 11 November, after consultancies counted more than 900,000 tonnes of bleached chemical pulp pulled from the market mid-quarter.

"We confirm that during October, our price increase announcements were fully implemented with no concessions," pulp vice-president Leonardo Grimaldi said on 6 November, adding that Chinese order intake showed "no decrease at all."

The counterweights are real. Chinese hardwood spot in Shandong averaged 4,613.33 RMB a tonne on 20 November, up 0.51% in five days and still near the lows even as imports rose 23% month on month. Suzano lifted full-year cash cost guidance from R$800 to R$825 a tonne because Brent ran $91 to $98 against a planning assumption of $84. Buying Kimberly-Clark's stake in the Arbex consumer business pushed net debt to R$76.3bn, US$14.7bn, and leverage to 3.7x, while Arbex's 16% margin helped compress consolidated gross margin to 23.7% from 30.4%. Suzano guides net debt below US$14bn by year-end on seasonal fourth-quarter cash flow, and chief executive João Fernandez de Abreu said on 6 November that after Arbex, "nothing else is on our agenda at the moment."

What the split actually rests on

Cerrado is no longer the variable: the 2.55m-tonne mill hit nameplate within months of its 2024 start-up and should run near 2.57m tonnes this year. What moved in Suzano's favor was other producers' downtime and a competitor's Indonesian delay, conditions it does not control, met by a cost line it does. Klabin's position is the mirror image: a price book that worked and a forest that did not.

That accounts for most of the 18-point gap between the two shares over the past month, and it is why the cheaper of the two on every currency-consistent anchor is the one that rose. Suzano trades at 5.39x trailing EV/EBITDA and a 9.1% free-cash-flow yield against Klabin's 6.09x and 2.5%. Forward earnings multiples on both are unusable, dividing dollar or five-share unit prices by reais per-share estimates.

Klabin expects the rain premium out of its cost line by the second quarter of 2027. Later the same year Arauco's Sucuriú mill in Mato Grosso do Sul is guided to start up, carrying more new short-fibre capacity in a single plant than Suzano's Cerrado added, and returning both Brazilian producers to the question neither can settle on its own: what a tonne is worth in China.

Walmart de México Added No New Shoppers Last Quarter; Its Growth Came Entirely From Price

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Mexico's formal discount shelf has stopped adding shoppers. Walmart de México's Mexican same-store sales rose 1.7% last quarter on ticket alone, with traffic flat for the first time in nine quarters and Bodega Aurrerá, the format serving its poorest households, missing its own internal plan on smaller baskets.

The money behind those baskets is shrinking: October remittances fell 1.7% year on year, a seventh straight monthly decline, and a Banco de México board member put the loss in their real peso purchasing power at more than 20% over eighteen months.

The damage is not evenly spread. Walmex operating income has fallen for three straight quarters and the shares are down 12.1% over twelve months while the Mexican index sits at a record; FEMSA's Oxxo grew same-store sales 9.2% and earns a fee paying out the remittance flow, and Banorte's bank margin came in at the high end of guidance.

Ticket grew, traffic did not

Walmart de México's Mexican same-store sales rose 1.7% in the third quarter, and all of it came from the price on the receipt. Ticket was up 1.6% and customer traffic was flat for the first time in nine quarters. The format that gave way is the one serving the lowest-income shoppers: Bodega Aurrerá, the 2,198-store discount chain, came in below Walmex's internal expectations on lower traffic and smaller shopping baskets, with weakness concentrated in larger stores in central Mexico, while Sam's Club led growth.

Mexican consumer staples are owned as the defensive corner of a market that has been working. The S&P/BMV IPC set an all-time high of 72,937 on 11 November, up about 11.3% on the year. Walmex closed at MXN53.19 on 27 November, down 12.1% over twelve months; of seventeen large Mexican listings, only the tequila maker Becle fell further. What has been marked down is the end of the shelf where the household's income arrives from abroad.

Where the basket's money comes from

Remittances to Mexico came to $5.64bn in October, down 1.7% year on year and the seventh consecutive monthly contraction. The composition matters: the number of transfers fell 5.4% while the average transfer rose 4.0% to $403, so fewer senders are sending more. Analysts in that account tie the decline to a weakening US labor market and political caution among migrants.

The 1% US excise on remittances that took effect on 1 January is the smaller force. It applies only where the sender funds a transfer with cash, a money order or a cashier's check; bank, wire, credit and debit funding are exempt, and 99.1% of January-to-October flows to Mexico arrived electronically.

The currency did the heavier work.

Banco de México's October board minutes record a member warning that the purchasing power of remittances, in pesos and net of inflation, has fallen more than 20% over eighteen months after a 10.2% appreciation, and tying that erosion to Mexican private consumption growth of just 0.8% in the first half.

It shows in the margin. Walmex operating income has fallen year on year for three straight quarters, by 2.2%, 1.4% and 0.8%, and third-quarter net income dropped 6.1% to MXN11.03bn as consolidated EBITDA margin narrowed 30 basis points to 10.2%. Guidance of 3.5% to 4.5% constant-currency sales growth was cut in April. "Consumer spending remains soft, and our performance is not yet where we want it to be," chief executive Cristian Barrientos Pozo said of a soft first quarter. On the 28 October call he described the answer: "We launched a huge campaign that called Precios que no se tocan with more than 4,000 items. We fixed the prices for more than 90 days."

Oxxo bills the flow its own shoppers live on

FEMSA reaches the same households through a different door. Oxxo Mexico same-store sales rose 9.2% last quarter, split 5.5% ticket and 3.5% traffic, and the convenience arm is also part of the country's cash plumbing: Oxxo pays remittances across more than 21,000 stores, and Spin by Oxxo, the largest banking correspondent in Mexico by regulator count, reached 11.7 million active users, up 17.3%, its loyalty program present in 52.1% of Oxxo Mexico transactions against 48.2% a year earlier. The ADR's 35.3% twelve-month gain came with compression, to 23.5x trailing earnings from roughly 46x; forward estimates of 25.5x sit above trailing, consensus saying the trailing figure, flattered by currency gains and discontinued-operations comparisons, comes down from here.

Banorte's funding cost stopped falling

The lender tests the other mechanism, and it failed. Bank net interest margin landed at the high end of guidance at 6.8%, with performing loans up 11% to MXN1.33tn, deposits up 13%, fees up 14% and return on equity of 24.5%. Nine-month profit rose 8.7% and the MXN62-64bn full-year target stands. Deposit margins are not being squeezed by an easing cycle because there barely is one: Banco de México cut to 6.25% on 5 November and signaled an extended hold. The soft spot is credit cost at 2.3% against 1.8% to 2.1% guidance, which management attributes mostly to loan growth. At 9.9x trailing earnings on 2.27x book, against about 8.8x a year ago, a little over half the 20.3% share gain is re-rating.

The month the shares moved without the numbers

Walmex gained 9.9% in the 30 days to 27 November, fifth-best among those seventeen listings, after trading below its moving averages from March to mid-October. No reported figure improved in between. Buen Fin brought more than 76 million transactions and the company's highest sales day on record on 17 November, yet fell short of Walmex's own initial expectations. The likelier reading is the currency: the peso closed at 19.71 to the dollar on 26 November, its weakest in twelve months, against 16.85 in early September, so each transferred dollar now converts into roughly a sixth more pesos than it did then.

The twelve-month de-rating was earned. Trailing earnings per share of MXN2.85 are slightly below the MXN2.91 of a year earlier, taking the trailing multiple to 18.7x from roughly 20.8x, and consensus has 2026 earnings growth at 1.5%. The November leg rests on relief that has not reached a results statement. The other two are different cases, and for a reason: Oxxo collects a fee on the inflow whatever its size, and Banorte's margin is set by a central bank that has stopped cutting.

Ahead of all three sits the wage. Proposals for 2027 were due on 30 November, with unions seeking 15% and employers about 6%; Walmex has guided to roughly 12% plus a workweek cut from 48 hours to 46, FEMSA to 13%.

That ruling lands on both sides of the Walmex ledger at once. It raises what the company pays its 240,779 employees and what the shopper who stopped walking into Bodega Aurrerá brings home, and only one of those two reaches January's income statement.
