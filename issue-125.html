Grid Dynamics Lifted Revenue Per Engineer a Fifth While Shedding Staff; EPAM's Stayed Flat

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Two digital-engineering firms were re-rated upward together after October and November earnings on the premise that artificial intelligence is adding to their work rather than eating it. Only one has the per-head arithmetic behind it.

Grid Dynamics turned $114.3m of September-quarter revenue out of 4,559 people, down from 4,971 a year earlier, and reported gross margin widened from 34.8% to 40.3% across the year. EPAM grew revenue 2.3% with delivery staff up 2.2% and utilisation unchanged at 76.5%, so output per delivery head sat still while price, offshore mix and a Polish research tax credit carried the margin.

Grid Dynamics' move rests on a genuine beat and raised broker targets but now carries the dearest forward multiple on the shelf at 26.1x; EPAM's 22.2% session came on a guide that landed on consensus, and at 12.4x it still trades under its own 15.4x of a year ago.

Grid Dynamics finished September with fewer engineers than it had a year earlier and more revenue to show for them. Management told investors on 29 October that revenue per employee had cleared $100,000 for the first time in the company's history.

That single measure is the test the whole digital-engineering shelf has been marked down against. Since mid-2026 these firms have been priced as though generative software tools would quietly eat the billable hours inside a fixed-fee statement of work; Morningstar cut EPAM's economic-moat rating to none and Accenture's to narrow on exactly that reasoning. Both of the names examined here have been re-rated upward since the October and November prints as if the thesis had been settled. One of them has the per-head numbers to settle it.

Fewer engineers, more revenue at San Ramon

Grid Dynamics, which builds search, data-analytics and platform-engineering software for Fortune 1000 retailers, telecom carriers and consumer-goods makers, booked $114.3m in the September quarter, up 9.7% year on year. Growth accelerated through 2026, from 3.7% to 7.0% to 9.7%, and reported gross margin widened from 34.8% to 40.3% over those same three quarters, with operating margin moving from minus 3.5% to 8.0%. Headcount fell to 4,559 from 4,971. Annualise the quarter over those staff counts and revenue per head works out near $100,300 against roughly $83,800, a gain of about a fifth.

"In a sector where headcount and revenue have historically moved in lockstep, Grid Dynamics is demonstrating that the two can and should be decoupled," chief executive Leonard Livschitz said on the 29 October call. Revenue from AI work grew 45% year on year for a third consecutive quarter and now approaches 32% of sales. Adjusted earnings of $0.18 a share beat the $0.13 consensus, and EBITDA of $22.9m cleared a $20m-topped guide of $16.5m-$17.5m. TD Cowen lifted its target from $9 to $13 and Needham from $10 to $15 the same day.

The efficiency came with a shorter client list. The top five customers supplied 45.4% of revenue against 40.1% a year earlier, and the top ten 63.3% against 58.3%, which management attributes to clients cutting their roster of suppliers in its favor. At $14.12 the shares trade on 26.1x forward earnings, the dearest of the four large names on this shelf, where Accenture sits on 16.4x, and against roughly 16.7x at the 2025 year-end.

Cash of $304.3m against a $1.13bn market value softens that: on enterprise value the shares are near 9.8x 2027 consensus EBITDA.

Where EPAM's extra margin actually came from

EPAM, the Newtown, Pennsylvania platform-engineering firm serving banks, life-sciences, media and software clients, reported $1.426bn, up 2.3%, with more than 57,350 delivery professionals, up 2.2%, and utilisation of 76.5% — unchanged from a year earlier and down from 78.3% in June. Per delivery head that is roughly $99,500 annualised against roughly $99,400: flat. The 1.8 points of non-GAAP gross-margin expansion, to 32.8%, came from pricing, offshore mix and a Poland research tax benefit that halves from about $40m this year to about $20m in 2027, and not from busier engineers.

Reported revenue growth decelerated across the year, 7.6% to 4.5% to 2.3%, and the December quarter is guided to organic constant-currency growth of 0.5% to 1.6%. The raised full-year non-GAAP earnings guide of $13.23-$13.31 brackets the $13.28 consensus; one outlet headlined the 22.2% session of 5 November as a jump on in-line results. A $500m accelerated repurchase and a fresh $1.8bn authorisation landed with the print. Asked whether double-digit growth could return, chief executive Balazs Fejes said: "Jamie, at a very high level, definitely not in 2027. I do foresee that we can actually get to double-digit growth. But over time, right?"

The shares at $164.63 are on 12.4x forward earnings and 11.4x the 2027 consensus of $14.43, below their own 15.4x at the 2025 year-end, and still 11.4% under where they traded twelve months ago. AI-native revenue reached $185m in the quarter, about 13% of sales, with the 2026 target raised to at least $650m from $600m.

One re-rating is paid for, the other is borrowed

Earnings explain most of Grid Dynamics' advance: the beat was wide, the margin trend is three quarters old, and brokers raised numbers on the print rather than after the move. They explain much less of EPAM's, where volume slowed, output per engineer did not change, and the gain arrived alongside a guide that matched what analysts already carried.

Why the two diverge is contractual. Kotak Institutional Equities reported in November that more than half of Cognizant's fixed-price portfolio had already passed through an AI-led deflation cycle: a vendor keeps a productivity gain only until the contract is re-signed. Grid Dynamics bills engineering pods into a consolidating supplier list at rising rates, so the gain shows up in its own revenue line. EPAM's larger book is repriced at renewal, which is why its improvement surfaced in gross margin and in the tax line instead.

The open question is which one survives contact with 2027. Grid Dynamics' finance chief Anil Doradla told the October call the mandate now is to scale, with "the appetite right now for larger acquisitions, which we hope to do in 2027." A per-head record built on 45% of revenue from five customers is a figure that can move in either direction with one of them.
