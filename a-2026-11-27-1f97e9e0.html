HDFC Bank Says Its Margin Bottomed; the Fix Is an October Rate Hike That Has Yet to Land

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

India's policy rate turned up in October for the first time since February 2023, and the quarter its two largest private lenders had just reported showed margins moving the wrong way. ICICI Bank's net interest margin slipped to 4.18% from 4.36% as its cost of deposits rose to 4.50%; HDFC Bank's cost of funds would not budge, because time deposits supplied most of its deposit growth.

Both banks say the repair comes from the hike itself: roughly 70% of HDFC Bank's loan book floats and reprices up by December, while term deposits reprice only at maturity. The shares moved first, on record foreign buying of Indian equities rather than on disclosed spread. Consensus has HDFC Bank's earnings growing 3.3% this fiscal year against 20.3% at ICICI, which is the whole of the gap between their multiples.

HDFC Bank told investors in October that its margin had reached bottom, and the thing it expects to lift that margin is the interest-rate increase the Reserve Bank of India had just delivered. The Monetary Policy Committee voted unanimously on 7 October to raise the policy repo rate 25 basis points to 5.50%, its first increase since February 2023, moving to a stance it called calibrated tightening and saying cuts were off the table in the near term. The September quarter that both of India's largest private lenders reported ten days later closed before any of that reached a loan book.

So the margins in hand went down and the margins in prospect went up, and November's rally in Indian bank shares was read as earnings confirmation. It was not that: HDFC Bank's net interest margin of 3.32% was the lowest point management identified, its reported net income grew 3.7% year on year — the weakest of the eight quarters on file — and its American depositary receipts gained a third in the month to 27 November.

A floating loan book against deposits that reprice at maturity

HDFC Bank, which gathers savings, current and term deposits across India and lends them as home, vehicle, personal and business credit, could not move its funding cost in the quarter. Cost of funds was flat at 4.4%, because time deposits supplied ₹1.3tr of the ₹1.57tr of deposit growth. Inherited borrowing maturities from HDFC Limited of roughly ₹2.6 lakh crore keep forcing that mix, holding the low-cost current and savings share down even as retail balances in those accounts grew 11.2% year on year.

The post-merger normalization of loans to deposits also stalled: deposit growth of ₹1.57tr was matched by loan growth of ₹1.58tr.

What changes is the asset side. "The 70% of floating rate book should be factored in by December [for the 25 bps rate hike], so directionally, yes, it should start improving from Q3 itself," chief financial officer Srinivasan Vaidyanathan told analysts on 17 October. Outgoing chief executive Sashidhar Jagdishan put it flatly on the same call: "The trajectory will be upward from the next quarter onwards." Asset quality gives them room — gross non-performing loans of 1.11%, down 13 basis points, with credit costs of 44 basis points and a 1.8% return on assets.

The shares now carry 16.5x trailing earnings and 2.17x book. Consensus fiscal-2027 earnings per share of ₹160.89 sits just 3.3% above the trailing four quarters' ₹155.73, so the forward multiple derived from it is about 15.9x — barely half a turn of relief for a year of waiting. The case rests on fiscal 2028, where consensus is 16.3% higher.

ICICI keeps 86 basis points more, and says where it leaked

ICICI Bank, a universal lender with 7,656 branches running deposits, mortgages, cards, insurance and wholesale banking, earned 4.18% on a comparable quarter against HDFC Bank's 3.32%. Its own compression had two named causes: overseas branches, which earn about 1% margin versus more than 4% domestically and grew as a share of the balance sheet after a foreign-currency deposit mobilization of $18bn, and retail term deposits, which pushed cost of deposits up to 4.50% from 4.41% in June, though still below 4.64% a year earlier.

"From Q4, I would expect margins to come back in a pretty healthy way as these factors start to reverse out, even assuming that there are no further rate hikes," chief financial officer Anindya Banerjee said on 17 October. Underneath, the bank delivered: pre-tax profit up 23.4%, profit after tax up 20.1% to ₹148.43bn, net interest income up 17.7%, and net non-performing loans down to 0.30% from 0.39% a year earlier. One item is unsettled — an extra standard-asset provision of ₹12.83bn on the agricultural book, pending a Reserve Bank review the company expects to resolve over the next two quarters.

At 19.3x trailing earnings, about 18.6x consensus for this fiscal year and 2.93x book, ICICI is the dearer of the two on every anchor, bought on consensus earnings growth of 20.3% this year against HDFC Bank's 3.3%.

What November actually bought

HDFC Bank's receipts rose 33.3% in the 30 days to 27 November and ICICI's 25.6%; across the year HDFC Bank is still down 21.6% while ICICI is up 16.3%. In Mumbai, HDFC Bank gained about 25% in November, its biggest monthly rise since May 2009. Foreign institutional investors bought for 16 straight sessions, a record roughly ₹1.29 lakh crore, with financial services the largest recipient and the rupee at an eight-month high. India was the biggest emerging-market recipient of those flows while South Korea and Taiwan saw heavy outflows — the same rotation that marked down Korean lenders whose own margins barely moved.

The disclosed results account for the move at one bank and not the other. ICICI's price is paying for earnings already reported and credit already improving; HDFC Bank's is paying for a spread its chief financial officer has dated to December and a year of earnings consensus still values at 3.3% growth. Jefferies, which raised its targets on 25 November to ₹1,880 for ICICI from ₹1,750 and ₹960 for HDFC Bank from ₹880, prefers ICICI first among Indian private lenders, and notes Indian equities at 2.1x one-year forward book against 2.2x for global financials after underperforming them by 46 percentage points since 2024. With HDFC Bank at ₹845.35 on 25 November, that ₹960 leaves about 13.6% — the month consumed most of the gap.

The December quarter is the first to contain the hike in full. If the loan book reprices on the two-quarter arithmetic management laid out, the margin turn arrives into a price that has already allowed for it; if the term-deposit book catches up first, HDFC Bank will have to find the earnings somewhere its own guidance has not promised them.

Originating hypothesis

ticker band divergence deposit funding and nim mechanics · subject: HDB, IBN, INTR

This 17-name international and emerging-market bank shelf is averaging only -3.0% over 30 days and -10.1% over twelve months — gradual, with not one member appearing in any mover list — yet underneath that quiet average the trend bands have split cleanly in two directions across the 90-, 180- and 365-day windows: every Latin American, UK, Japanese and Korean member has stepped down into mild or strong bear (AVAL, BBD, BSBR, BLX, BMA, GGAL, IFS, LYG, NWG, KB, SHG, MFG, DB), while the two Indian private banks have been repaired the other way — HDFC Bank from strongly bearish to mildly bullish across 90, 180 and 365 days, and ICICI from strongly bearish to strongly bullish over 180 and 365 days — and rather than another order-book-versus-price divergence, the question here is a mechanism question told end to end: what a deposit actually costs and what a loan actually earns at each bank when the policy rate is moving in opposite directions, since HDFC Bank is still working down a post-merger loan-to-deposit ratio by gathering low-cost CASA deposits into a falling-repo, cut-CRR environment where asset yields reprice faster than its term-deposit book, ICICI earns a structurally wider margin on a granular retail and unsecured mix funded by one of the system's cheapest deposit franchises, and Inter and Co — the one starred member here, and the one that flipped mildly bullish to strongly bearish within 30 days and strongly bullish to strongly bearish over the year — funds a fast-growing unsecured Brazilian consumer book with digital deposits priced off a Selic that has not come down, so its spread and its credit cost move against it at the same time.
