What the evidence actually shows

The four names cited in the rotation hypothesis — Cogent Communications (CCOI), InterDigital (IDCC), Core Laboratories (CLB), and Comtech Telecommunications (CMTL) — superficially resemble a cluster of mid/small-cap compounders being abandoned in favor of AI-adjacent trades. Dig into the price action and earnings records, and that narrative collapses entirely.

CCOI: A Dividend Aristocrat Thesis Reset

CCOI's 12-month collapse of roughly 67% (from ~$50.54 to ~$16.65) is dominated by a single session: November 6, 2025, when the stock fell ~35% in one day after Cogent slashed its quarterly dividend by 98% — from $0.985 to $0.02 per share. According to Kavout's analysis, the cut was precipitated by capital structure stress from the Sprint wireline acquisition, with gross debt/EBITDA at 7.4x and $750M of unsecured notes maturing in June 2027. The same source notes that CCOI had previously delivered 50+ consecutive quarters of dividend increases — this is a fundamental investment thesis reset from income vehicle to high-leverage infrastructure turnaround, not capital rotation. Underneath the Sprint legacy churn, the Cogent Classic business grew ~28% since acquisition closing, and Q1 2026 wavelength revenues surged 90.8% year-over-year — the operating business is improving even as the balance sheet creates overhang.

IDCC: A Guidance Step-Down, Not a Structural Crack

IDCC is actually UP 28.5% over 12 months — the recent ~26% pullback from a late-2025 peak reflects a specific 2026 revenue guide of $675M–$775M versus a near-record $834M in 2025, plus rising IP enforcement costs. InterDigital's Q1 2026 10-Q shows operating expenses rising 57% year-over-year, driven by $27.5M in revenue-share costs and $17.5M in IP enforcement spend tied to active litigation against Disney, Amazon, TCL, and Hisense. Short interest has risen 38.8% over 12 months to 10.7% of float, indicating the market is positioned around the guidance step-down. Yet ARR hit a record $567M (+13% YoY) in Q1 2026, the company holds $1B+ in cash, and top-3 smartphone licensees (Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi) are contracted through end of decade — the franchise is intact and the revenue dip reflects timing of catch-up payments, not structural deterioration.

CLB: A Geopolitical Supply Shock, Fully Idiosyncratic

Core Laboratories is the clearest counterexample to any sector-rotation thesis. Stocktitan reported Core Lab explicitly cut Q1 2026 guidance to $119–$123M from $124–$130M after the Middle East conflict closed client offices and disrupted hydrocarbon transportation through the Strait of Hormuz. A Yahoo Finance deep-dive on Q1 results shows EPS missed consensus by 45.5% and operating margin collapsed to 1.5% from 6.3% a year prior. Meanwhile, SLB gained +48.6% and HAL gained +87.4% over the same 12-month window — the broader oilfield services sector is thriving. CLB's drop is entirely event-specific, tied to a binary geopolitical scenario where a resolution of Hormuz flows restores demand rapidly. The balance sheet is clean: 1.2x leverage, 19 consecutive years of dividends maintained.

CMTL: Speculative Volatility, Not a Compounder Selloff

CMTL is UP 158.6% over 12 months, from $1.52 to $3.93. The 3-month pullback from ~$5.87 is a normalization of speculative excess around a restructuring thesis, not a rotation selloff. CMTL's SEC filing history confirms the company reported seven quarters of going-concern warnings and posted an FY2025 operating loss of $139M — it was never a "quality compounder." CMTL remains capital-structure-fragile: ~$228M in credit/subordinated debt plus $213M in convertible preferred liquidation preference, with covenant testing suspended through January 2027. Peer Iridium (IRDM) is up 53.7% over 12 months and +100.7% over the past month, further ruling out a satcom-sector explanation.

Why the Rotation Narrative Fails

The hypothesis predicted a "continuous gradual bleed" — the data shows discrete event-driven dislocations. CCOI's single-day 35% drop is entirely earnings/event-driven. CLB's discrete 7.4% selloff on April 30 followed a guidance cut tied to a specific geopolitical event. IDCC's move clusters around an earnings/guidance announcement. Every peer benchmark across OFS (SLB, HAL), satcom (IRDM), and fiber (Cogent Classic business) is holding flat-to-sharply higher. The "funding source rotation" framing requires a correlated, sector-independent decline — what exists instead is four separate company-specific stories that happen to share a calendar.

Valuations diverge widely: IDCC trades at 13.3x EV/EBITDA with an 8.0% FCF yield and a record ARR backlog; CLB at 8.8x EV/EBITDA with a geopolitical recovery call embedded in the thesis; CCOI at 17.8x with negative FCF yield reflecting the Sprint integration burden. The stories are distinct and require distinct analytical frameworks.
