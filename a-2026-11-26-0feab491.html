A sensor maker whose shares more than doubled by midsummer on a humanoid-robotics story has given back seven-tenths of its value, and the explanation is in the income statement. Vishay Precision Group booked all-time record Sensors orders of $51.7m in the third quarter for a 1.35 book-to-bill, and consolidated bookings exceeded shipments for an eighth straight quarter.

Net income still fell 92.9%. Currency translation took $3.4m out of operating profit year on year and the tax rate ran at 48%. That makes Vishay Precision the one business on this test-and-measurement shelf whose own accounts support its markdown: the two shelfmates sold down beside it, Cognex and Teledyne, each raised full-year guidance into the decline.

Vishay Precision Group, which makes strain gages, load cells and precision resistors for weighing systems, steel rolling mills and automotive crash-test rigs, took in more sensor orders in the third quarter than in any quarter of its history and kept $600,000 of net income. Revenue rose 6.7% to $85.1m. Operating income fell 73.3%, to $2.7m.

That pairing is the whole argument over a stock that ran from $60.22 on 1 May to a close of $149.91 on 30 June and has since lost 70.3%, finishing at $44.49 on 25 November. The surge was read as a cheap humanoid-robotics option bolted onto a company now worth $547m. The slide has been read as the same high-multiple technology unwind that marked down the whole shelf, a rotation out of the AI trade that accelerated on 25 November. The quarter supports neither reading cleanly, because demand set a record and profitability nearly vanished in the same three months.

Where nine-tenths of the profit went

Operating margin came in at 3.2% against 12.7% a year earlier, and the causes management named sit mostly below the demand line. Foreign exchange reduced third-quarter operating profit by $3.4m year on year, $8.1m year to date. The tax rate ran at 48% in the quarter against 37% in the first half, costing about $0.03 of earnings per share. Headcount additions and start-up costs at a new plant absorbed the rest.

Adjusted gross margin actually improved, to 39.3% from 38.6% in the second quarter. The pressure is currency and overhead, not price.

A record book, and $300,000 of robot sensors

The Sensors segment turned over $38.4m, up 21% year on year, on those record orders of $51.7m, a second consecutive quarter at that level. Chief executive Ziv Shoshani put AI infrastructure at 20% to 30% of the segment's revenue. Consolidated book-to-bill was 1.17, leaving backlog of $151m. Measurement Systems orders rose 19% sequentially to $22.2m, with steel-related bookings up 40% sequentially.

The humanoid line that drew the crowd produced $300,000 of sales and $200,000 of bookings, and the first customer's production ramp slipped to the fourth quarter. "We also received an investment from this customer to support our capacity expansion related to humanoid, which we view as a solid endorsement of our value proposition," Shoshani told investors on 4 November. Management guided fourth-quarter revenue to $84m–$91m and targeted $90m or better in the first quarter of 2027.

Two shelfmates raised their forecasts instead

Cognex, the largest supplier of industrial machine-vision systems, reported record revenue of $313.4m on 5 November, up 13.2%, at a 32.2% operating margin, and lifted full-year guidance to $1.14bn–$1.16bn of revenue and $1.68–$1.72 of adjusted earnings per share on stronger logistics. The shares fell 10.5% that session and 19.3% between 3 and 25 November. At $46.91 the trailing multiple is 32.6x, against 78.7x in May, and 27.4x on the $1.71 consensus for this year; brokers cut targets through the month, Barclays to $76 from $85 on 16 November.

Teledyne, which sells imaging sensors and instrumentation into defence, space and marine programmes, barely moved: flat over 90 days and 9.8% off its high. Executive chairman Robert Mehrabian said on 21 October that "orders have exceeded sales for 12 consecutive quarters and funded backlog is now $5.3 billion," of which roughly 72% converts within a year, and raised guidance for the third quarter running to $6.625bn of revenue. It trades at 28.8x trailing earnings, against 32.5x in May.

What the accounts account for

All three absorbed the same input shock, memory prices rising as makers reallocate capacity to AI data centres, and two of them priced through it. Cognex offset memory headwinds and still expanded adjusted gross margin by 360 basis points; Teledyne said it is recovering component inflation in price. The order books on this shelf did not break, which is why the markdown reads as a de-rating of the multiple rather than a capital-budget pause, and why the fall in Vishay Precision's own price-to-sales ratio accounts for nearly all of its slide since July.

The difference is what each multiple was resting on. Cognex's forward price now sits on earnings consensus raised to $1.71; Teledyne's on appropriated backlog. Vishay Precision's rested on a robotics ramp that has yet to ship meaningfully, and consensus has its full-year earnings at $0.41, down 40%, before quadrupling to $1.65 next year. Forward price-to-sales has gone from roughly 5.4x at the June peak to 1.60x.

The order book says the sockets are real and designed in. The accounts say that at a 48% tax rate and with currency running against it, a record book is worth $600,000 a quarter, and the recovery everyone is now holding has been moved into next year's numbers.
