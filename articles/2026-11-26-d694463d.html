Cybersecurity Shares Fell Together in November; Only Okta's Contracted Revenue Slowed Too

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A month-long markdown across cybersecurity software was read as the artificial-intelligence premium deflating out of security budgets. The contracted books disagree at two of the three names whose billing models differ most.

Cloudflare's current remaining performance obligations grew 37% year over year and dollar-based net retention reached 122%, the highest in about four years, while revenue growth accelerated three quarters running to 39.2%. Rubrik's subscription annual recurring revenue reached $1.66bn, up 33%, with net new ARR up 35% and full-year guidance raised. Only Okta fits the deceleration story: four straight quarters of slowing growth to 10.6%, a twelve-month subscription backlog up 14% and guided to 11-12%, net retention flat at 107%.

The catalysts were specific rather than thematic: Fortinet's product revenue decelerating despite a guidance raise, and CrowdStrike's all-stock purchase of Zscaler.

CrowdStrike agreed on 23 November to buy a rival in network security entirely with its own shares, and lost roughly $21bn of market value in the two sessions that followed. The target was Zscaler, the zero-trust network access vendor, valued at about $43.9bn at a fixed 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per Zscaler share, a 34% premium that leaves existing CrowdStrike holders with roughly 84.1% of the combined company. The issuance of some 191m new shares, close to a fifth of the existing count, mattered more to the market than a quarter that beat on revenue at $1.58bn with raised guidance.

That was the last act of a month in which the whole group was marked down. Okta, Cloudflare, Rubrik, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Fortinet and Zscaler all fell over the 30 days to 25 November, by an average of 17.1%, with Fortinet worst at -27.2% and Okta mildest at -9.0%. The fall has been read as security's AI premium deflating — as evidence that agentic software is about to eat seats and budgets. Across three companies that are paid in three genuinely different ways, the contracted revenue says that happened at exactly one of them.

The two catalysts were company-specific

Fortinet lit the fuse. Product revenue rose 37% to $765m in the September quarter, a sharp step down from 52% growth the quarter before, and the shares fell more than 20% despite the third full-year guidance raise of the year, to $8.125-8.215bn. Both events landed on top of the 5 November session, the biggest technology selloff since Covid, which repriced anything whose value sits in profits expected years out.

Cloudflare's meter ran faster than its price

Cloudflare, which bills consumption on a global edge network rather than seats, stepped down out of its strongest uptrend on 3 November, two sessions before it reported. Revenue growth had accelerated for three straight quarters, 33.5% to 35.9% to 39.2%, reaching $782.5m. Remaining performance obligations hit $2.877bn, up 34%, with the portion due inside a year up 37%. Dollar-based net retention of 122% was a four-year high, and the company passed 5,000 customers paying more than $100,000 a year, now 75% of revenue.

The beat came from variable usage: customers burned through prepaid pools of funds faster than modeled and renewed early at larger sizes. "Between July 1st and today, we added approximately 5% of the entire web to Cloudflare," chief executive Matthew Prince told investors on 5 November. "That's wild." Chief financial officer Thomas Seifert guided GAAP profitability to the fourth quarter, against an investor-day target of 2028 at the latest. On a non-GAAP basis the quarter earned $137.1m of operating income; the GAAP statement shows a $67.9m operating loss.

What the shares still carry is the level. Cloudflare trades at 47.6x forward gross profit, roughly three times Rubrik's 16.4x, with consensus revenue of $3.914bn in 2027 required to justify it. Gross margin fell 290bp to 72.4% as lower-margin developer products took more cost of revenue.

Rubrik's net-new line accelerated into a 20% fall

Rubrik, which sells multi-year data-protection and cyber-recovery subscriptions priced on data under management, fell 20.5%. Its last reported quarter showed subscription ARR of $1.66bn, up 33%, with net new subscription ARR of roughly $96m, up 35% year over year, and cloud ARR up 39% to $1.48bn as the maintenance base converts. Full-year guidance went up, to $1.88-1.885bn of ARR and $323-333m of free cash flow. The growth is organic; the Strata Identity acquisition added nothing to subscription ARR.

The case against is two lines: revenue growth has decelerated from 46.3% to 37.9% over three quarters, and gross margin compressed 250bp to 78.4%.

Okta is where the books agree with the shares

Okta sells per-user cloud identity, and per-user is the model the agentic thesis threatens. Revenue growth has slowed for four consecutive quarters to 10.6%. The twelve-month subscription backlog was $2.585bn, up 14%, guided to 11-12% growth, and net retention sits at 107%. Chief financial officer Brett Tighe told investors in August that AI-agent contribution will not be material this fiscal year; Okta discloses no machine-identity unit count at all, even as CyberArk's 2025 landscape put machine identities at roughly 82 times human ones.

What Okta did deliver is margin: quarterly operating margin went from 3.1% to 13.3% over four quarters, and full-year revenue guidance was raised to $3.216-3.226bn. At 51.4x forward earnings, against roughly 36x in mid-July when the shares were at $139.53, the multiple is paid for that margin expansion and not for growth, since consensus models 10.0% revenue growth next year.

What the reported books can and cannot explain

One of the three de-ratings has a basis in the numbers the company reported. Okta's decelerating backlog on a per-seat book is exactly the configuration that compresses an identity multiple, which is why its own guidance already embeds the slowdown and why a 9% fall barely touches a stock up 146.8% over twelve months. At Cloudflare and Rubrik the contracted lines moved the other way, and the likelier reading of their falls is duration repricing plus the dilution shock, not demand. The split has a cause in how each is paid: a consumption meter and a data-volume subscription both grow when agents proliferate, while a seat price only grows if someone buys seats.

Two of the three answer inside a week. Rubrik reports on 30 November against its own guide of $1,829-1,839m of subscription ARR, and Okta on 1 December. The company that told the market to expect less is the one whose shares have least to lose.
