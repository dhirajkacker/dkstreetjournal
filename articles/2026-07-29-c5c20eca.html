A merger death, not a sector melt-down

Shutterstock and Getty Images together account for nearly all of the Creative & Content bucket's brutal one-month move. On July 7, 2026, Getty's board terminated the two companies' merger agreement after the UK Competition and Markets Authority demanded a forced sale of Shutterstock's editorial business as a condition of approval — ending a deal that would have combined the two largest stock-image licensors. SSTK closed at $5.98 (-57.7% over 30 days, -69.8% over 12 months) and GETY at $0.408 (-56.6%/30d, -77.8%/12m).

The termination is not just sentiment — it has a hard mechanical consequence. It triggers a special mandatory redemption of Getty's $628.4M of 10.5% senior secured notes due 2030 at par plus accrued interest unless the deal is revived by October 6, and Getty's board is now engaging a financial advisor to review strategic financing alternatives against a roughly $171M equity base — a live refinancing overhang, not a discounted risk.

SSTK's core business is also decaying on its own: Q1 2026 revenue fell 17.9% year-over-year with gross margin compression, and FY2026 consensus models a further 23.3% revenue decline into a net loss, even as the stock's 2.96x trailing EV/EBITDA and roughly 52% trailing free-cash-flow yield already appear to price continued deterioration rather than mispricing.

Chegg's bounce looks unrelated to fundamentals

Chegg sits apart from the licensors. After cutting roughly 45% of its workforce in October 2025 — its second round of layoffs in five months, blaming reduced traffic and "the new realities of AI" — the company ended 2025 with $85M in cash and a stated goal of ending 2026 debt-free while pivoting toward B2B skilling. Its trend band has stayed mild-bullish through July even as Academic Services revenue fell 57% year-over-year, a divergence the desk's own analysis reads as takeout speculation on a cash stub rather than operating recovery.

The counter-examples: Figma and Adobe

Figma is the cohort's clearest growth story: Q1 2026 revenue rose 46% year-over-year with net dollar retention hitting 139%, its highest mark in over two years, alongside raised guidance — even though Anthropic's April 2026 Claude Design launch knocked the stock 6-7.7% on a single announcement.

Adobe presents the sharpest disconnect between fundamentals and its label: AI-first ARR more than tripled year-over-year past $500M, with Firefly ARR near $300M, on record $6.62B quarterly revenue, yet its trend band has held strong-bear continuously through July 28 alongside SSTK and GETY. Four of five cohort members carry bearish trend bands, but the underlying stories driving them — a collapsed merger and refinancing scramble versus genuine AI monetization at a profitable incumbent — are not the same trade.
