SiTime Says Wafer Supply, Not Demand, Will Cap Its Timing Chips Through 2027

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

SiTime's September quarter was limited by what it could build rather than by what customers would take: management said demand had exceeded its own internal forecast and that wafer tightness persists into next year, and guided the December quarter up about 12.5% sequentially anyway, to a $336m midpoint.

Revenue more than tripled to $298.7m. The timing division bought from Renesas on 1 July supplied $86.3m of that, so the remainder of the company still grew roughly 154% on those figures. The price of that deal is the newer fact: $1,350m of zero-coupon convertible notes plus 3.56m shares, which lifted the diluted count 13.4% in a single quarter.

Semtech, the name in this pair assumed to be carrying the debt, cut net leverage to 0.7x from 1.1x. Neither order book explains the autumn markdown; SiTime's funding does explain where its risk now sits.

SiTime's difficulty in the September quarter was that it could not build enough. The company, which sells silicon microelectromechanical oscillators and clock chips into data-center, automotive and industrial boards through a network of distributors, told investors on 4 November that demand had exceeded its own internal forecast and that the wafer tightness behind its parts would persist through 2027, improving in severity along the way. It guided the December quarter up about 12.5% sequentially regardless, to a midpoint of $336m.

The markdown that ran through silicon levered to artificial-intelligence hardware in November was read as a content premium deflating out of order books. SiTime's did not deflate: both the core business and the acquired timing division were guided to grow sequentially from here. What has changed over two quarters is the claim on its equity.

Demand ran ahead of the forecast, capacity behind it

Revenue of $298.7m was up 257% year on year, and the timing division acquired from Renesas on 1 July contributed $86.3m of that. Strip it out and the remainder still grew roughly 154% on those figures, a derived number but a wide margin. The core communications, enterprise and data-center line was $127.3m, up 202% year on year and 26% sequentially, and management said more than 80% of the acquired division's revenue is weighted to the same end market.

The mechanism is speed grades. "More bandwidth requires more precision timing," chief executive Rajesh Vashist told investors on 4 November, and the company's own figures put gigahertz-class coherent parts at $6 to $8 each against $1 to $2 for megahertz devices. On the constraint he was specific: "We expect that we will get closer to the supply chain ourselves by Q1, and we start to resolve the more severe issues, exiting this quarter, and getting into Q1, Q2, I think we should be in significantly better shape." Days sales outstanding rose ten days to 61 and working capital absorbed $6.6m of cash in the quarter.

What the Renesas timing book cost

SiTime bought the division for roughly $1.5bn of cash plus 3,558,691 shares, and funded the cash with $1,350.0m of zero-coupon convertible senior notes issued in May, according to its September-quarter filing. There is no cash coupon and no refinancing clock, but there is $1.35bn of principal and a conversion claim behind the stock consideration, which lifted the diluted share count 13.4% sequentially to 31.17m.

The acquisition also distorts the income statement it flatters. Purchase accounting running through cost of sales held the reported gross margin to 51.6% against 70.7% adjusted, and reported operating income to $8.3m against a 43% adjusted operating margin. The seller is one of the quartz incumbents SiTime displaces: quartz suppliers still hold about 72% of a frequency-control market put at $6.84bn, which is where the growth comes from.

Semtech, the one assumed to be carrying debt

Semtech, which sells signal-integrity silicon for optical data-center links alongside its long-range wireless and protection lines, cut net leverage to 0.7x from 1.1x in a single quarter on $55m of free cash flow, reaching its sub-1x target early. Revenue was a record $423m, up 58%, with data-center revenue of $145m up 158% year on year and guided to roughly $180m. Its price to gross profit is 12.0x trailing against 25.2x in mid-August, while trailing gross profit grew 79%.

The results account for none of the fall

Between late August and 25 November SiTime lost 35.6% and Semtech 34.8%, with the deepest leg inside a week when the Nasdaq fell about 10%, its worst since March 2020. Nothing in either guide moved that way.

What the results do not settle is the price of being right. SiTime's forward price to gross profit of 22.9x is far below the roughly 82x it carried in mid-August, but it rests on wafers arriving and on concentration that is rising with content: management said its largest account's 2027 revenue forecast has been raised two to five times, and most of the $10bn funnel it now claims is expected to convert in 2028 and 2029. Semtech's case is funded by its own cash flow. SiTime's is funded by convertible notes.

With no scheduled report from SiTime until an estimated early February, the next dated event on its calendar is a share split that triples the count of shares outstanding and does nothing about how many wafers it can get.
