Micron's 2027 Memory Is Sold Forward at Higher Prices; the Same Contracts Cap the Upside

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Memory's November selling was read as the cycle peaking. Micron's own filings say the prices it will be paid for most of next calendar year's output were agreed months ago, at levels above the ones it has just reported.

The fiscal fourth quarter brought revenue of $54.2bn, and the annual report allocates roughly $134bn of transaction price to remaining performance obligations, with more than 75% of calendar-2027 production committed under long-term customer agreements. The markdown is therefore landing on prices already accepted. The real fragility sits elsewhere: TrendForce says those same agreements' price ceilings are slowing conventional contract gains, and it expects NAND contract prices to fall 0-5% in the first quarter of 2027.

Everspin, on the same memory shelf, prices nothing off DRAM. Its fall traces to a $6m litigation provision and a shelf registration.

The only US-listed pure play among the world's three makers of high-bandwidth memory has already agreed what it will be paid for most of next calendar year's output. Micron's fiscal 2026 annual report states that over 75% of calendar-2027 production is committed under long-term Strategic Customer Agreements, and puts the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations at approximately $134bn, roughly a quarter of it expected to be recognized within twelve months.

Memory shares were sold through November as though the open question for 2027 were what a bit fetches on the spot market. At Micron that question was largely settled before the selling began. "We have completed agreements for the vast majority of our calendar 2027 HBM bit supply with significant price increases year-over-year, narrowing the gross margin gap with conventional DRAM," chairman and chief executive Sanjay Mehrotra told investors in prepared remarks on 30 September. The fragility in Micron's earnings is real. It sits where a spot-price markdown cannot reach it.

What 26 take-or-pay deals fix, and for how long

Micron has signed 26 multi-year Strategic Customer Agreements carrying take-or-pay volume commitments that cover more than 35% of revenue through 2030, three-quarters of that revenue subject to defined pricing frameworks, alongside $32bn of customer financial commitments mostly in cash deposits. The deposits are visible in the accounts: $12.75bn received during fiscal 2026, with noncurrent customer contract liabilities at $12.90bn against $142m a year earlier. A second high-bandwidth memory assembly and test line in Taiwan opened in mid-October with all of its planned 2027 output contracted. Samsung, one of the other two suppliers, has also sold out its 2027 supply, so none of the three enters the year chasing spot volume.

Against that, fiscal fourth-quarter revenue rose 379% to $54.229bn, gross margin reached 86.8% from 44.7% a year earlier, and the first quarter is guided to a record $61.5bn plus or minus $1.5bn.

The shares went the other way. Micron closed at $703.74 on 23 November, 19.8% below a month earlier and about 42% under its 25 June record. The selling was not memory's: Nvidia fell 21.4% over the same month, Seagate 17.1% and Western Digital 12.3%. Micron dropped 13.89% on 20 November, the day Barron's called memory stocks "agonizingly cheap" at 3.8 times forward earnings, then gained 19.04% on 23 November when Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang and the Nasdaq 100 rose more than 7%.

Where the contract book stops helping

Fiscal 2026 DRAM revenue rose 252%, of which roughly 180 percentage points came from average selling prices and only a mid-20% increase from bits shipped. Price, not volume, did the work, and price is the component a turn removes. Days of inventory closed the quarter at 129, nine days higher than the prior quarter. Capital spending was $27bn in fiscal 2026 and is tracking above $50bn in fiscal 2027; "We plan to increase our CAPEX in fiscal 2027 versus prior plans," the prepared remarks said.

Contracts cut in both directions. TrendForce expects conventional DRAM contract prices to rise 10-15% quarter on quarter in the fourth quarter of 2026, down from roughly 80% in the second and 40% in the third, attributing part of the deceleration to price ceilings in long-term agreements taking effect. NAND is further along: contract prices are forecast to fall 0-5% in the first quarter of 2027 and every category to enter a downcycle from the second, on 25-30% bit-supply growth and Chinese cloud providers cutting enterprise drive orders. Consensus already models the fade, decaying earnings growth from 138% in fiscal 2027 to 20% and then 3%. Micron trades at 4.0x forward earnings against 9.4x trailing, and its trailing price-to-gross-profit of 7.5x compares with 26.3x in May 2026. One unresolved item is labor: the union at Taoyuan voted 1,994 to 14 on 7 October to authorize a strike over profit-sharing, with no date set.

Everspin's 51% margin never touched DRAM

Everspin Technologies, which sells magnetoresistive memory into industrial automation, data-center storage, medical and aerospace sockets, fell 14.9% over the same month and sits 43% below its 2 October close. Nothing in that prices off DRAM. Third-quarter revenue was a record $20.648m, up 46.9%, split $16.9m of product and $3.7m of licensing, royalty and engineering revenue, the latter recognized largely against a $40m subcontract with a US defense contractor. Gross margin held near 51%. The dates that matter are its own: operating expenses rose to $18.5m from $8.8m on a $6m litigation provision relating to a proposed $11.2m export-control penalty the company is disputing, and a $100m mixed shelf registration was filed on 16 November.

The memory squeeze is a tailwind there. "To counter rising input costs, we began to institute pricing increases in the fourth quarter and expect product margins to improve sequentially," chief financial officer William Cooper said on the 28 October call, and chief executive Sanjeev Aggarwal said distributors are asking for MRAM parts to replace NOR flash as those fabs convert capacity to DRAM, an effect he called not yet material. Everspin's first profitable consensus year is 2027, at $0.24 a share, which on the 23 November close is about 45x; its forward price-to-gross-profit of 6.5x is nearly twice Micron's 3.6x.

So the results explain almost none of Micron's markdown for the next several quarters and quite a lot of what comes after them: the contracted book insulates fiscal 2027 revenue and simultaneously caps what rising spot prices can add, while bit growth, inventory days and a near-doubling of capital spending are the variables left exposed. Everspin's decline belongs to a provision, a penalty and a dilution filing, and its claim on the AI memory tier arrives later still, with Aggarwal placing samples of the persistent-memory standard at the end of 2028 and early revenue in 2029.

The deposits sitting on Micron's balance sheet are returned only if customers buy what they committed to buy. If they do not, the cash stays and becomes revenue. It is the first point in this cycle where the weak case pays Micron something either way.
