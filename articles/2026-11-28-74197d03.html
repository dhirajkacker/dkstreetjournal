El Niño Rain Lifted Klabin's Cost Per Tonne 12% as Suzano Cut Its Own and Raised Prices

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Two Brazilian eucalyptus producers reported three business days apart, and the wood in the ground separated them. Klabin's total cash cost per tonne sold rose to R$3,483 as El Niño rainfall disrupted forestry and forced purchases of standing timber, and its adjusted EBITDA margin narrowed six points to 33%. Suzano's pulp production cash cost fell 2% sequentially to R$825 a tonne while realized pulp fetched $591.

The two cost measures are not identical and their levels do not compare, but their slopes do, and the shares followed: Suzano up 8% in dollars over the past month, Klabin down 10% in reais, against an Ibovespa that moved 0.5%. The awkward part is that Klabin's price gain came from export pulp rather than from the contracted Brazilian packaging book meant to insulate it.

Klabin and Suzano both grow eucalyptus in Brazil, and both told investors this month what a tonne of it now costs them. The answers moved in opposite directions. Klabin's total cash cost per tonne sold, including scheduled maintenance downtime, rose 12% year on year to R$3,483, of which the company attributed about R$111 to extraordinary weather and geopolitical pressure. Suzano's pulp production cash cost ex-downtime fell 2% sequentially to R$825. The two numbers measure different things — one an integrated pulp, paper and conversion book, the other a pulp mill — so the level does not compare. The slope does.

Until these results the market treated both as hard-currency assets grown in Brazil. On the 26 October session when the Ibovespa fell 13.88%, Suzano's American depositary shares rose 2.1% and Klabin rose 3.2%, both on roughly four times normal volume. What the quarters showed is that only one of them is being paid for fibre on improving terms, and that Klabin's insulation from the pulp cycle works in both directions.

Rain in the plantations, not weakness in the boxes

Klabin's revenue rose 3% to R$5.57bn, yet adjusted EBITDA fell 12% to R$1.856bn and the margin dropped to 33% from 39%. The cause was physical: rainfall tied to El Niño disrupted forestry operations and forced higher wood inventories and purchases of standing timber, with chief executive Cristiano Teixeira saying the extra costs should mostly phase out by the second quarter of 2027.

Where the revenue came from matters more. Klabin's pulp line grew 6% to R$1.51bn on an 18% rise in dollar short-fibre prices, while corrugated-box revenue of R$1.7bn was flat because implemented price increases exactly offset a 2% volume decline to 245 thousand tonnes. More than 70% of the packaging area is contracted with brands including JBS, Nestlé, Unilever and Natura, and Brazilian corrugated shipments are forecast up only 1.6% to 2.0% this year. A contracted book in a 2% market defends price and delivers no growth.

It also carries R$24bn of net debt at 3.3x adjusted EBITDA, financed into a policy rate the central bank held at 13.75% on 4 November after five consecutive cuts. Below-the-line financing costs consumed most of the quarter's operating profit, and management says the end of the investment cycle should support deleveraging from 2027. On 6 August, a quarter before the cost miss, Teixeira told investors: "for the next quarters, you're going to see a significant free cash flow generation in the company." The trailing free-cash-flow yield is 2.5%, and the shares carry 6.09x trailing EV/EBITDA and 2.15x book.

Suzano announced a price and collected it

Suzano's pulp segment earned R$4.8bn of EBITDA at a 50% margin on 3.19m tonnes at $591 a tonne. At the post-election rate near R$5.60 to the dollar that realization is roughly R$3,310 a tonne against R$825 of production cash cost. Two $20-a-tonne Asian increases landed, the second effective 11 November, after consultancies counted more than 900,000 tonnes of bleached chemical pulp pulled from the market mid-quarter.

"We confirm that during October, our price increase announcements were fully implemented with no concessions," pulp vice-president Leonardo Grimaldi said on 6 November, adding that Chinese order intake showed "no decrease at all."

The counterweights are real. Chinese hardwood spot in Shandong averaged 4,613.33 RMB a tonne on 20 November, up 0.51% in five days and still near the lows even as imports rose 23% month on month. Suzano lifted full-year cash cost guidance from R$800 to R$825 a tonne because Brent ran $91 to $98 against a planning assumption of $84. Buying Kimberly-Clark's stake in the Arbex consumer business pushed net debt to R$76.3bn, US$14.7bn, and leverage to 3.7x, while Arbex's 16% margin helped compress consolidated gross margin to 23.7% from 30.4%. Suzano guides net debt below US$14bn by year-end on seasonal fourth-quarter cash flow, and chief executive João Fernandez de Abreu said on 6 November that after Arbex, "nothing else is on our agenda at the moment."

What the split actually rests on

Cerrado is no longer the variable: the 2.55m-tonne mill hit nameplate within months of its 2024 start-up and should run near 2.57m tonnes this year. What moved in Suzano's favor was other producers' downtime and a competitor's Indonesian delay, conditions it does not control, met by a cost line it does. Klabin's position is the mirror image: a price book that worked and a forest that did not.

That accounts for most of the 18-point gap between the two shares over the past month, and it is why the cheaper of the two on every currency-consistent anchor is the one that rose. Suzano trades at 5.39x trailing EV/EBITDA and a 9.1% free-cash-flow yield against Klabin's 6.09x and 2.5%. Forward earnings multiples on both are unusable, dividing dollar or five-share unit prices by reais per-share estimates.

Klabin expects the rain premium out of its cost line by the second quarter of 2027. Later the same year Arauco's Sucuriú mill in Mato Grosso do Sul is guided to start up, carrying more new short-fibre capacity in a single plant than Suzano's Cerrado added, and returning both Brazilian producers to the question neither can settle on its own: what a tonne is worth in China.
