Accenture and Cognizant Added Staff All Year, and Revenue Per Employee Rose Just 2%

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Generative AI is supposed to be deflating the billable hour at the big systems integrators. The one disclosure that would show it, revenue per head, has barely moved at Accenture or Cognizant, and both are still hiring.

Accenture closed fiscal 2026 with record new bookings of $84.54bn, a wider operating margin and about 35,000 more employees. Cognizant's reported third-quarter growth of 4.6% was almost all acquisitions and resold third-party product, leaving organic services growth under 1%.

Accenture holds the better operating record: margin expansion on a 1.2 book-to-bill. Cognizant cut its constant-currency growth guidance for the year to 4.0-4.4% and signed 7% fewer bookings in the quarter than a year earlier. Output per head, derived from each company's own filings, improved roughly 2% at both.

Accenture, the Dublin-headquartered firm selling strategy, consulting, cloud, data and multi-year managed operations to large enterprises, closed its fiscal year on 31 August with record new bookings, a wider operating margin and roughly 35,000 more people on the payroll than a year earlier. That last fact is the problem with the story the sector has been sold.

The case against the big integrators, stated plainly in the note that reinstated coverage of Cognizant at Neutral in mid-November, is that artificial intelligence is a structural rather than cyclical headwind, because it erodes billable hours and pushes delivery risk onto the vendor. The case for them is that the same tools let a firm bill the same work with fewer people. Both arguments resolve to one measurable quantity: revenue per employee. Derived from each company's reported revenue and its own disclosed headcount, it moved about 2% at each over the past year.

Accenture's margin gain was made below the delivery line

Revenue of $74.18bn was up 6.5% in fiscal 2026, operating income up 11.5%, and the operating margin reached 15.4% against 14.7%. That is real operating leverage, and it did not come from the price of the work: gross margin was flat at 32.1%. Headcount reached approximately 814,000 at 31 August against approximately 779,000 a year earlier, a 4.5% rise set against revenue growth of 6.5%, leaving output per head at roughly $91,100 from $89,400. Voluntary attrition rose to 14% from 13%, so nobody is leaving faster either. The firm booked $923m of restructuring charges in the year under a programme that reskills some people and exits others where reskilling is not viable.

The order book is the strongest part of the record: new bookings of $84.54bn for the year, a record, up 5% in dollars and 3% in local currency, with a trailing four-quarter managed-services book-to-bill of 1.2 and 141 client bookings above $100m in the fourth quarter. Managed services supplied $12.77bn of that quarter's $22.17bn, so most of what was signed is contracted run-work priced before the hours are worked.

What is no longer in the record is the AI line. Accenture retired its separate advanced-AI bookings disclosure after the first quarter of fiscal 2026, the final print being $2.2bn of bookings and about $1.1bn of revenue, on the grounds that advanced AI is now embedded in nearly everything it does. A client count replaced it: more than 400 clients initiated advanced-AI work during the year. "AI is the breakthrough innovation. Only reinvention can unlock the value," chief executive Julie Sweet said at the 14 October investor day, where the firm also said its AI and data workforce had grown from 40,000 to nearly 110,000 professionals over four years, additive to the 814,000 rather than a substitution for it.

Cognizant's 4.6% was mostly bought and resold

Cognizant, the Teaneck firm selling consulting, technology and outsourcing to financial services, healthcare, products and communications clients, grew third-quarter revenue 4.6% to $5.664bn. Acquisitions contributed roughly 2.1 percentage points of that and resale of third-party products roughly 1.9, leaving organic services growth under 1%. Headcount of 359,900 was up 2.9% year on year, putting revenue per employee near $60,800 from $59,600.

The adjusted operating margin of 16.3% was the highest in four years and a seventh consecutive quarter of year-on-year expansion. The reported margin went the other way, 15.4% against 16.0%. Guidance for constant-currency growth was cut to 4.0-4.4% from 4.0-5.5% on discretionary pressure in healthcare and consumer-facing sectors, and bookings signed in the quarter fell 7% even as the trailing-twelve-month total rose 4% to $28.7bn on a 1.3x book-to-bill. "Even at midpoint of 4.2%, we would be both on total growth basis as well as on the organic basis would be delivering a top-tier performance vis-à-vis rest of the players," chief financial officer Jatin Dalal told investors on 29 October. Deutsche Bank cut the stock to Hold on 13 November, target $66 from $75, saying its growth thesis had not played out.

One mechanism here points the right way. Fixed-price and transaction-based work reached 58% of revenue, up 1.4 points year on year and 5 points since the third quarter of 2022, the contract form under which a productivity gain stays with the vendor. "The pivot is switching from productivity to growth imperatives using AI. And the rebaselining of productivity has already happened to a large extent," chief executive Ravi Kumar said on the same call. At 10.6x forward earnings, the shares carry about a third less than the 15.3x on which the equivalent forward estimate was priced two years ago.

What the per-head numbers settle

Neither income statement carries the deflation yet, in either direction.

Six months of share gains, Accenture up 31.8% and Cognizant up 18.7%, came with no acceleration underneath them. Accenture's quarterly revenue growth ran between 5.6% and 8.3% through the year and its fiscal 2027 guidance is 3% to 6% in local currency; Cognizant decelerated from 5.8% to 4.6% across three quarters. Both then handed much of the gain back in a market-wide correction driven by a December Federal Reserve rate hike moving to roughly 95% probability, with no company disclosure behind it.

Accenture is being paid more per client without getting more out of each employee, and part of the next leg is bought: the investor-day framework has acquisitions contributing 2% to 2.5% of fiscal 2027 revenue against roughly 1.5% in fiscal 2026. Its 17.1x trailing earnings against 15.9x forward prices about 7% earnings growth, well under the 27.8x forward the shares carried two years ago. Cheap because slow, in other words, and the slowness is in the guidance.

The threat neither filing captures arrived from above. Anthropic, whose Claude models roughly 30,000 Accenture professionals are trained to deploy, launched its own consulting and professional-services division on 3 November, and OpenAI has made a similar move. The model vendors now bid for the implementation work, and the integrators' answer, for the moment, is to hire.
