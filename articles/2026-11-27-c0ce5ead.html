Plexus Got Customers to Fund the Inventory Behind $1.5bn of New Assembly Work

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A contract manufacturer whose growth barely consumes its own balance sheet has been marked down alongside the companies building artificial-intelligence hardware, and it does not build that hardware. Plexus closed its fiscal year with a record $4.965bn of revenue, up 23%, raised its operating-margin goal to 6.5–7.0% from 6.0%, and guided the December quarter above consensus.

The order book did not crack: the qualified funnel hit a record $4.9bn, up a third, with semiconductor capital equipment work up 50% for the year. The soft spot is cash, where trailing free-cash-flow yield is negative, and a December quarter guided flat sequentially as defense demand normalizes.

On gross profit, Plexus at 9.95x is still dearer than TTM Technologies at 7.41x, which has fallen to a 52-week low.

Plexus, the Neenah, Wisconsin company paid a fee to assemble other firms' medical devices, semiconductor tools and defense electronics, added roughly $1.5bn of annualized revenue in its last fiscal year and committed only $250m of its own working capital to carry it. Customers funded much of the inventory, and the cash conversion cycle finished the year at 62 days, one day shorter than a year earlier.

That is close to the opposite of the balance sheet November's selloff has been punishing. Plexus has traded below its moving averages since late October and is down 15% over 30 days, to $188.20; its worst session of the stretch was 20 November, when it fell 9% as a report of reduced 2027 volumes for Broadcom-designed accelerators hit the artificial-intelligence hardware complex. Plexus does not build those accelerators. Its data-center exposure is an emerging funnel of conservatively about $0.5bn in power and thermal management, with two announced programs. On the same shelf, TTM Technologies has fallen a further 16% since 23 November, to a 52-week low of $58.17.

Nothing in Plexus's own disclosures has changed since 29 October.

Ten cents on the assembly dollar, and a target raised anyway

Plexus keeps about a tenth of what passes through it. Gross margin was 10.2% in the September quarter and has sat within a third of a point of that all year, because the model is a fee for building someone else's product rather than a price for a box. The leverage shows up below that line: fiscal fourth-quarter operating income grew 62% on revenue growth of 35%, operating margin reached 6.1%, and the company lifted its target to 6.5–7.0% from a prior 6.0% goal.

"Revenue per employee expanded by approximately 20% over the same time period," David Abuhl, senior vice president and chief financial officer, told investors on 29 October, crediting investments in operational efficiency. Return on invested capital reached 15.8% in the quarter, above the company's own 15% threshold.

The funnel grew where the downturn was

The qualified manufacturing opportunity funnel reached a record $4.9bn, up $1.2bn or 33% year over year. Industrial revenue grew 27% for the year with semiconductor capital equipment up 50%, which management traces to share captured when that market was weak. "We took a significant amount of share particularly through the last downturn within the semi cap market," chief executive Todd Kelsey said on the October call. Aerospace and defense wins totaled $490m for the year, and healthcare grew 19%.

The counts are not uniformly up. Fourth-quarter wins of $290m across 36 programs ran marginally below the roughly $300m quarterly average implied by $1.2bn of full-year wins, and December-quarter revenue is guided flat sequentially as defense demand normalizes and semi-cap orders time out, with industrial down low single digits. Even so, the guide of $2.46–2.62 in non-GAAP earnings per share came in above the $2.34 consensus, and the shares fell 8.9% that day.

Plexus discloses wins and a funnel, never a book-to-bill ratio, so the nearest external read on whether fee-for-assembly order books have cracked is the Global Electronics Association's North American figure: 1.72 in October, with bookings more than doubling year over year.

Cash is the one place the growth costs something

Trailing free-cash-flow yield is −0.58%, and the company forecasts fiscal 2027 free cash flow above $100m on capital spending at the upper end of 2–3% of revenue. Customer funding softens the burden without erasing it: incremental working capital ran at 17% of the revenue added, inside a 15–20% target. Top-ten customer concentration rose to 56% of revenue from 49% a year earlier.

So the business explains very little of the fall and the financing explains some of it. Plexus at 9.95x trailing gross profit — the usable comparison when its 10.2% gross margin sits against TTM's 23.6% — is still dearer than the board fabricator at 7.41x and Benchmark Electronics at 6.63x, and it is up 32% over twelve months against TTM's 15% decline. The de-rating, from 24.8x trailing to 17.5x forward earnings, has taken the premium down without removing it. Analysts cut targets without cutting ratings: the Plexus consensus was a Buy at an average $276.86 in mid-November. At TTM, two directors answered the markdown with their own money, including a purchase struck at $58.3977 on 20 November.

Plexus offers no equivalent tell. What it offers is a funnel that grew by a third in a year its shares went the other way, and a December quarter that will either hold the flat line management drew in October or will not. That line, more than Broadcom's accelerator schedule, is what the price now turns on.
