Analog Devices' Record Quarter Lost to a Doubled Fab Budget for Capacity Opening in 2028

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A chipmaker reporting the best quarter in its history, with orders running above shipments, lost close to a sixth of its value in a single session. The explanation sits on the cash flow statement, not the order book.

Analog Devices earned $4.373bn of revenue in the quarter ended 31 October, up 42.2% and the fourth consecutive acceleration, with gross margin at 69.3% and channel inventory back inside its six-to-seven-week distributor model. It then guided fiscal 2027 capital spending to $1.6bn-$1.9bn against $732.9m, for American and European fab capacity that does not open until 2028.

About eight of the seventeen points it lost that day were the whole analog shelf falling together; the remainder attaches to the capital line and a gross margin guided to moderate. Monolithic Power, fabless, needs no such spending to reach $8bn of support capacity, but the 800-volt parts it will defend its rack-power socket with are only sampling.

Analog Devices reported the strongest quarter in its history on 24 November and closed the session down a sixth. The disclosure that did the damage sat on the capital line: fiscal 2027 capital expenditure guided to $1.6bn-$1.9bn against $732.9m spent in fiscal 2026, for front-end fab capacity in the United States and Europe plus back-end test and assembly, part of it eligible for credits under the CHIPS Act, and none of it opening before 2028.

The fall was widely read as the industrial analog cycle finally rolling over after a year-long recovery. The company's own account of its order book does not support that reading. Book-to-bill ran above one in almost all end markets, distributor inventory came back within the six-to-seven-week model the company runs to rather than above it, and January-quarter revenue is guided to $4.4bn plus or minus $150m, modest sequential growth in a quarter that normally declines.

What was in the quarter

Revenue of $4.373bn was up 42.2% year on year, the fourth straight quarter of accelerating growth after 30.4%, 37.2% and 39.6%. GAAP gross margin reached 69.3% and operating margin 42.7%, both at multi-year highs, and operating income grew 97.7% against that 42.2% revenue line. Full-year revenue was $15.179bn, up 37.7%, against a fiscal 2024 in which revenue fell 23.4%. Adjusted earnings of $4.06 a share beat a $3.89 consensus and revenue beat $4.32bn expected, and the shares were already down 9.8% in premarket trading.

One line inside the beat argues for caution. Industrial, half of revenue, finished up 55% year on year and 11% sequentially, but the sequential pace has slowed for three consecutive quarters, from 14.6% to 11.0% to 8.8%. Inventory rose $152m to 165 days.

Data centre revenue more than doubled year on year for a second consecutive quarter and is now 83% of the communications segment and roughly 13% of sales, reported as a standalone end market from the January quarter. Chief executive Vincent Roche told investors on 24 November that "we're not dependent on just a very concentrated infrastructure data center buildout," and said more than two-thirds of total growth came from non-AI-centric markets.

The spending, and what it costs

Fiscal 2026 produced operating cash flow of $6.2bn and free cash flow of $5.5bn, 36% of revenue. Doubling capital spending against that base is a real impairment of near-term cash, which is why press accounts tied the fall to the fiscal 2027 guide rather than the beat. Adjusted gross margin of 74.5% was guided to moderate to about 74% on fab holiday shutdowns, with management saying utilisation is not what is driving improvement.

The fabless side of the same shelf

Monolithic Power Systems, which sells high-density DC-to-DC power management chips and modules into data centre, automotive and consumer customers, carries the opposite capital posture. It cleared $1bn of quarterly revenue for the first time in the September quarter at $1.202bn, up 63.1%, with enterprise data at $506.7m, 42.1% of revenue and up 164.6% year on year. On 29 October it raised its enterprise-data growth floor to 150% from 130%, repurchased more than $450m of stock, and said it can support $6bn of annual revenue today and $8bn by the second half of 2027 with no significant capital spending. Founder-chief executive Michael Hsing said: "I see this explosive growth period in the next few years. And even this year, you see the first 9 months, okay? And we don't see anything -- any signs of the slowdown."

Two qualifications. Gross margin has been flat in a 55.1-55.4% band for eight quarters, because a fabless model shares foundry cost increases with customers instead of filling its own lines. And the 800-volt gallium-nitride parts with which it must hold its rack-power socket are only sampling, with revenue dated to late 2027 and material ramp in 2028 or later. The concentration its September-quarter filing discloses is at the channel: one distributor at 32% of revenue, a second at 16%.

How much the businesses explain

Not much of the single session. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.88% that day and 23.2% over November as doubts returned about whether debt-funded compute spending earns its cost of capital. Texas Instruments fell 11.25% and Diodes 12.17% on 24 November having reported nothing, so roughly eight of Analog Devices' seventeen points was the shelf. The incremental move attaches to the capital and margin lines, and that part is doing rational work.

Over the month to 24 November the shelf fell with a median near 13%, but unevenly: Microchip lost 23.9% and Analog Devices 20.0%, while Monolithic Power lost 2.0% and NXP Semiconductors rose 1.9%. Monolithic Power's own drawdown, 24.5% from its three-month high, arrived in the days after 19 November, well after its band turned down in early September. Both names remain higher over twelve months, Analog Devices by 31.6% and Monolithic Power by 25.1%.

What has gone is the multiple. Analog Devices trades at 30.7x trailing earnings against 57.9x in late August, and at 18.4x consensus fiscal 2027 earnings of $17.09 a share against roughly 23.0x recomputed at its 25 September close of $393.60, with no cut to the estimate in between. Monolithic Power sits near 24.0x fiscal 2027 consensus of $46.60 against about 29.3x in late September, a multiple conditional on consensus revenue growth of 51.5% next year surviving a capital-spending scare it does not control. Costs are also moving underneath both: conventional memory contract prices rose 93-98% in the March quarter and PC shipments are forecast down about 10.4% this year, which is why Analog Devices guided consumer down 10-15% in January.

Both bills come due before the revenue does. Analog Devices' new fabs open in 2028, Monolithic Power's high-voltage parts are dated to late 2027, and neither appears in the order book that each company has just described as running above one.
