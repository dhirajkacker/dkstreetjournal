Ceva and Impinj Set Unit Records Into a Markdown Only Ambarella's Numbers Justify

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Three semiconductor names grouped together as an edge-inference shelf were sold down together, and the shipment data from the two that have reported since the decline began runs the other way. Ceva's licensees shipped 623 million chips in the September quarter, matching its all-time record, and Impinj set records on endpoint chip revenue and unit volume while telling investors it could not keep up with orders.

Only Ambarella's growth is genuinely slowing, from 31.2% year over year four quarters ago to 13.2% in the July quarter and roughly 10% guided for the one it reports on 24 November. Its chief executive traced the risk to memory allocation at its customers, not to cancelled orders. Ceva is the cheapest of the three at 7.0x trailing gross profit against Impinj's 23.8x; Impinj has the best volumes and the least valuation room.

Ceva, which licenses signal-processing, artificial-intelligence and wireless connectivity designs to chipmakers and collects a royalty each time a licensee's chip ships, told investors on 9 November that its customers shipped 623 million Ceva-powered devices in the September quarter, matching its all-time quarterly record, with record cellular internet-of-things and record Wi-Fi 6 volumes inside it. Its shares are down 40% from their 26 May high.

The drawdown across this group has been read as the physical demand for edge-inference silicon turning down. The disclosures say something narrower: unit volumes are at or near records at the two companies that have reported since the decline started, and the single business that is actually decelerating blames a shortage of memory in its customers' bills of materials.

Record units, stalled revenue

Ceva's quarter is the awkward one to read. Royalty revenue of $12.7m rose 18% sequentially on about 10% more units, so revenue per device improved — Wi-Fi 6 reached 57% of its Wi-Fi volume from 24% a year earlier, lifting the royalty per chip even as total Wi-Fi units fell 6%. Group revenue still grew just 2.4%, to $29.1m, with gross margin at 85.4% against 88.0%. A multi-year connectivity licence slipped out of the quarter and was signed in early Q4, and the stock fell 13% on the print; management trimmed full-year growth to about 11% from 13-15%.

"AI licensing revenues reached a record, accounting for more than 35% of total licensing and related revenues," chief executive Amir Panush said on the 9 November call, with six of ten deals signed in the quarter AI-related. Chief financial officer Yaniv Arieli guided to $34m-$38m: "This would represent the highest quarterly revenues in CEVA's history."

The one deceleration, and it starts in DRAM allocation

Ambarella, which sells vision chips combining video compression with computer-vision inference to camera, automotive and robotics makers, is the case that fits the bear reading. Year-over-year growth has fallen through four quarters — 31.2%, 20.1%, 16.9%, 13.2% — and the September guidance of $115.0m-$124.0m implies about 10% at the midpoint. Gross margin held at 57.7% and the operating loss narrowed 63% to -$8.1m, so this is a volume story, not a pricing one.

Asked where guidance could break, chief executive Fermi Wang told investors on 3 September: "The downside is only one, it's memory." Suppliers would not commit November allocation to his customers. Ambarella neither buys nor resells memory, so there is no direct margin hit; the transmission runs through customers whose device costs rise as TrendForce projects conventional DRAM contract prices up 10-15% in the fourth quarter and NAND up 15-20%. Forward earnings now cost 68.4x against roughly 12% consensus growth, down from about 98x in July.

The name that never turned

Impinj sells radio-frequency identification endpoint chips for individual retail, logistics and food items, with no inference on the chip. Its September quarter set records on revenue, at $112.1m, and on endpoint chip revenue of $97.3m, up 24%, with record unit volume. "We met, but were unable to get ahead of, market need," chief executive Chris Diorio said on 28 October. It is the dearest of the three at 23.8x trailing gross profit, against Ambarella's 10.0x and Ceva's 7.0x, and it is the only one whose trend never turned negative — its shares are flat since late May.

Three pay structures, three outcomes

The shared markdown has a shared cause that is not demand: all three fell 9-12% on 20 November, a week in which the S&P 500 lost 8.6% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index about 17%, and all three bounced on 23 November. Underneath that, the split follows how each gets paid. Ceva earns pennies per unit after the design is done, so a record shipment quarter can sit beside flat revenue when one licence signature moves two weeks. Impinj is capacity-rationed at the back end of its own supply chain. Ambarella sells the chip into a finished device whose other components are being price-rationed in front of it.

That accounts for the Ceva and Impinj declines poorly and the Ambarella decline well. Growth settling near 11-13% on consensus is a different company from the one that grew 37% in fiscal 2026, and a lower multiple is rational work. What the prints do not support is the group reading.

Ambarella reports its October quarter after the close on 24 November, with the call at 1:30pm Pacific. It is the one data set on this shelf where the memory squeeze could show up as orders that never arrived.
