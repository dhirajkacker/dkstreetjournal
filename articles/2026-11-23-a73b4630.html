Twilio's Price for a US Text Never Moved in 2026; the Carriers Raised Theirs Three Times

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Both US messaging platforms raised guidance on the same day in October and have since lost a third and half of their market value, and the published price cards show why that is hard to square with the meter. Twilio lists a standard American text segment at $0.0083; Verizon's surcharge on top went to $0.0050 on 1 October, so roughly 38% of what the end buyer pays stops at the phone company at close to zero margin.

The pair split on earned margin, not on pass-through. Bandwidth is the heavier pass-through case, with carrier surcharges supplying 59% of its incremental revenue last quarter, yet its non-GAAP gross margin rose 300 basis points to 60.6% on an owned voice network while Twilio's slipped to 49.2%. The one unit that pays a step-change, an AI voice session at about nine times a bare voice minute, is roughly 1% of Twilio's voice interactions.

The price of sending a text message in the United States went up three times this year, and the two companies best known for sending them did not get any of the increase.

Twilio and Bandwidth both reported the September quarter on 29 October, both raised full-year guidance on every metric, and both gave preliminary 2027 outlooks above their own prior frameworks. Since then Twilio has fallen 38.1% from the $301.27 it closed at on 1 October and Bandwidth 50.0% from its 9 July high of $78.44. The markdown reads as a verdict that AI agents will route around the messaging and voice rails before the rails get paid for them. The published rate cards say something narrower: the meter is intact, it is still being repriced upward, and the increases are accruing to the phone carriers.

Who gets paid on a Verizon text

Twilio lists a standard outbound American SMS segment at $0.0083 per 160 characters, the same across long codes, toll-free numbers and short codes, with carrier fees applied automatically on top. Since 1 October the terminating carrier's cut on Verizon has been $0.0050, an 11.1% rise. T-Mobile went from $0.0030 to $0.0045 on 19 January, a 50% increase, and AT&T to $0.0035 in April; on 1 November the surcharge stepped to $0.005 on six further networks. Add the two published rates and a Verizon-terminated message costs its buyer about $0.0133, of which roughly 38% never stops at Twilio. Twilio's own rate did not move.

Substitution changes the billed unit without removing it. A conversational Rich Communication Services session opens once a thread holds at least four rich messages with two genuine replies, and Verizon's session fee is $0.009, so since October a four-message exchange is cheaper billed as a session than as four messages.

Where the two part company is earned margin

Twilio's non-GAAP gross margin was 49.2% in the quarter, down 90 basis points, and chief financial officer Aidan Viggiano said it would have been up 80 without $53m of incremental US carrier fees. Messaging grew 24% as reported and 17% organically, with about seven of those points supplied by pass-through.

On that axis Bandwidth is the heavier case. Total revenue was a record $234m, up 22%, but cloud communications, its own measure of earned business, grew 12% to $158m, and messaging surcharge revenue rose $25m, or 51% out of a $42.1m total increase: 59% of the incremental dollars. GAAP gross margin fell 200 basis points to 36.2%, which management attributed solely to the surcharge line.

Strip it out and the comparison inverts. Bandwidth's non-GAAP gross margin rose 300 basis points to 60.6%, roughly 1,140 basis points above Twilio's by subtraction, on a network it owns and interconnects in about 70 countries. "SI is raising the value of that layer," chief executive David Morken said on the 29 October call of the network itself. "A legacy carrier connection cannot do that."

The unit that pays nine times more is barely metered

Twilio's ConversationRelay bills $0.07 per minute of active AI session on top of $0.0085 for an inbound local voice minute, so about $0.0785 against $0.0085 for a bare minute. That is the largest per-unit price step either company has. It is also almost empty. "The way that we characterize voice AI in our prepared remarks is that it's about 1% of what the voice interactions are so far," chief executive Khozema Shipchandler told analysts on 29 October, calling consumer AI "a fraction of that 1%." Neither company disclosed a dollar figure for AI voice revenue.

What the results account for, and what they do not

Some of the decline has fundamental content, all of it second-derivative. Twilio's organic messaging growth slipped from 18% to 17%, dollar-based net expansion from 116% to 114%, and fourth-quarter non-GAAP operating income is guided to $300–310m against $326m in the third, on higher hosting costs, which is what coverage blamed for a 19.2% two-day fall after a 12.2% earnings beat. HSBC's downgrade to Reduce, with a $211 target, argues the market already prices a generous share of future AI-agent volume into Twilio. None of that is a volume decline, a price cut or a named customer loss, and the surcharge steps this autumn ran the other way.

The rest is a rating change imposed from outside, arriving in a week when the S&P 500 confirmed a correction on 20 November on doubts about AI spending durability, and both names bounced with the complex on 23 November, Twilio 8.3% to $186.46 and Bandwidth 14.6% to $39.25. Twilio now trades at 9.8x forward gross profit against the 13.0x on the Desk's record in August, while gross profit grew 20%. Bandwidth is at 3.8x trailing gross profit against 6.5x in May, a 42% compression while GAAP gross profit grew 14.9%, and its balance-sheet risk has gone the wrong way for the bears: all but about $7m of the 2028 convertible notes is retired and net leverage is 1.3x.

The asymmetry is in who holds the pen on price. Twilio and Bandwidth set what a message and a minute earn them and have held those rates flat; the carriers reset their cut whenever they choose, and chose four times in eleven months. Whatever an AI agent ends up paying for access to a phone number, the surcharge on the text it sends will have moved again first.
