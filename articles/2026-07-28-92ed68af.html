Clean-energy selloff is a bifurcated cohort, not a fresh rotation

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Sonnet 5 · Writing Sonnet 5 · Prompt v1.0

Trend-band data shows the clean-energy complex splitting into two distinct groups this week: a handful of names (CWEN, ORA, and the already-broken RUN/STEM/EOSE) are in genuine multi-week-to-multi-month bear regimes tied to specific fundamentals, while XIFR, BEP, ENPH, SEDG, GEV and TLN remain in bull bands despite sharp five-day pullbacks — arguing this is sector beta layered on top of two older, idiosyncratic downtrends rather than a new cohort-wide macro-driven break.

Old downtrends deepening, not starting

Sunrun, Stem and Eos Energy have sat in strong-bear trend bands since February–March 2026, well before this week's headlines. Sunrun's break traces to a roughly 28% share-price plunge around its Q4 2025 earnings report, when the company guided 2026 cash flow to $250-450M versus 2025's $377M and Jefferies downgraded the stock to Hold. The demand deterioration has continued into 2026: Sunrun's Q1 2026 metrics showed new subscriber count down 25% year-over-year, added storage capacity down 15%, and added solar capacity down 19%. RUN, STEM and EOSE are down roughly 29%, 29% and 45% over the trailing 30 days and 51%, 68% and 79% over six months, respectively — a long secular decline this week's action is extending, not initiating.

The freshest genuine break: CWEN and ORA

Clearway Energy is the cleanest new cohort signal: its trend band flipped from mild-bull to mild-bear on July 2 and deepened to strong-bear on July 20, ahead of this week's broader selloff. The catalyst looks fundamental: CWEN was added to the Zacks Strong Sell list on July 13 on a 112% negative EPS revision, with shares falling into oversold RSI territory near a 52-week low. Notably, the company has not cut guidance to match the sentiment shift — Clearway reaffirmed 2026 cash-available-for-distribution guidance of $470-510M and a 2030 CAFD target of $2.90-3.10/share as recently as its May 2026 Q1 results, suggesting an estimate-revision/sentiment move rather than a guided coverage deterioration. Ormat (ORA) shows a similar, slightly earlier band flip on July 14.

What's still holding up

Six names in the original cohort — XIFR, BEP, ENPH, SEDG, GEV and TLN — remain in mild- or strong-bull trend bands despite steep five-day declines. XIFR is roughly flat over 30 days and up 15.7%/20.1% over 90/180 days despite its -6.3% single-day headline; the company's July 28 Q2 print reaffirmed full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $1.75-1.95B and free-cash-flow-before-growth guidance of $600-700M. GE Vernova's drop looks like its own story: shares fell on a quarterly margin/cash-flow guidance miss and a Siemens Energy rival downgrade, yet its trend band never left strong-bull.

Macro legs are mostly already priced

The hypothesis's structural catalysts predate this week's move: the 45Y/48E "beginning of construction" deadline landed July 4, three weeks before the selloff; heavy AD/CVD tariffs on Southeast Asian solar cells were finalized months earlier; California's NEM 3.0 grandfathering window closed April 15; and the 10-year Treasury yield actually eased into July 28 after a modest rise over the trailing month. None of these lands as a fresh, dated trigger inside the specific window driving this week's price action.

Originating hypothesis

category early downtrend vs sector beta · cross_segment: XIFR, CWEN, BEP, RUN, STEM

The clean-energy yieldco and residential-solar complex — XIFR -6.3% on the day, RUN -7.2% and CWEN -6.3% over five sessions, BEP -3.3%, with STEM -68% and EOSE -79% over six months — may be entering an early, still-actionable cohort-wide downtrend that is distinct from this week's broad energy/infrastructure risk-off (GEV, WMB, LNG, CAT all down 6-9% in the same window), and the question is whether trend bands show these names rolling out of neutral/bull into bear on a 30-90 day basis behind validated macro (the 45Y/48E credit phase-out deadlines and Treasury start-of-construction guidance, imported-cell tariffs and safe-harbour equipment costs, higher long-end yields raising yieldco cost of capital and pressuring distribution coverage, and net-metering/rate-design changes in the big resi-solar states) — or whether this is five days of beta inside bands that are still intact.
