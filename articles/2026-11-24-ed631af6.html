Medicare Priced Axogen's Nerve Graft at $8,965 and Left Inspire's Implant Without a Code

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Three companies sitting on the same nerve-and-robotics shelf are paid in three different units, and the unit explains why they went opposite ways. Procept BioRobotics collects a single-use handpiece on every prostate treatment; Axogen is paid per nerve repair on a product the Food and Drug Administration licensed as a biologic in December 2025; Inspire Medical is paid once per sleep-apnea patient implanted.

Procept's Q3 procedures rose 22% to more than 13,400 with gross margin at 66.3% against 60.6% four quarters earlier, and Axogen raised 2026 guidance to at least 29% growth. Inspire's revenue fell 7.9% because providers lost the billing code used for roughly 10,000 Medicare placements, a $120m hole; its dedicated replacement code does not take effect until 2028. Earned per-procedure payment carries two of the three.

What one procedure pays turned out to be the whole argument in nerve and prostate devices this year. Procept BioRobotics, which places image-guided surgical robots in hospitals and sells a disposable handpiece for every Aquablation treatment of an enlarged prostate, now collects roughly $3,570 of handpiece revenue per case, up from about $3,200 a year earlier as its newer HYDROS platform displaced the older system and end-of-quarter bulk discounts were dropped.

That is one payment unit out of three on a shelf the market has been treating as a single re-rating story. Axogen is paid per nerve repair. Inspire Medical is paid once per patient, for life. Over the month to 23 November the first two rose 25% and 52%; Inspire fell 24%, and is down 36% over twelve months. The segment did not turn up together, and the reason is mechanical.

Procept's growth is coming out of installed robots, not new ones

Procept performed more than 13,400 aquablations in the third quarter, up 22% year over year, with handpiece revenue up 21% and the handpiece-to-procedure ratio at 106% against 98% the prior quarter as field inventory normalized. Revenue of $98.3m grew 18%, slower than the 37% of full-year 2025, but gross margin reached 66.3% against 60.6% in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Utilization, not capital placement, is doing the work. "In the third quarter, procedure volume at HYDROS accounts was more than 35% higher than legacy AQUABEAM accounts," chief executive Larry Wood told investors on 4 November, attributing it to surgeon migration between the two platforms. Capital pricing held at about $495,000 for a new system and $315,000 for a replacement, and management reaffirmed roughly 54,000 procedures for the year. Aquablation is still the challenger in its market: transurethral resection accounted for 53.7% of US procedures for benign prostatic hyperplasia in 2025 against Aquablation's 15.6%. The shares trade at 3.65x forward sales and 5.65x forward gross profit, against 4.09x and 6.33x trailing, and remain 74.8% below their December 2024 peak; Truist raised its target to $24 while keeping a Hold, and consensus sits essentially at spot.

Axogen's step-up was a change in what the product legally is

Axogen sells Avance, a processed human nerve allograft that bridges a severed nerve without harvesting a donor nerve from the patient. On 3 December 2025 the Food and Drug Administration approved its Biologics License Application, moving the graft from human-tissue classification to a licensed biologic. The payment followed: Medicare set 2026 facility reimbursement at $8,965 for hospital outpatient and $6,157 for ambulatory surgery centers, and commercial coverage reached about 96% of US lives after Elevance removed its nerve-gap-length criterion in August.

Third-quarter revenue of $75.8m grew 26.2%, guidance went to at least 29% growth and $290m, and breast reconstruction, now 64% of revenue, accelerated past 60% growth at roughly 11% penetration of eligible cases. Gross margin fell 180 basis points to 74.8% on costlier long-length production, the one line going the wrong way. The move itself is dated: shares jumped on the 28 October beat, with adjusted earnings of $0.21 against $0.13 expected, then climbed again after a 9 November presentation putting a $754m market behind prostate nerve reconstruction at about $7,000 per procedure, none of it in guidance. At 12.0x forward sales and 105x forward earnings, the price already discounts two more years near 30% growth.

Inspire sells the best gross margin and cannot get it billed

Inspire's hypoglossal-nerve stimulator for obstructive sleep apnea carries an 86% gross margin and is implanted once. From the first quarter of 2026 providers could no longer use the billing code previously applied to roughly 10,000 Medicare placements of Inspire V, costing about $30m of third-quarter revenue and $120m for the year. Revenue fell 7.9% and operating margin swung from positive 4.3% to minus 13.9%.

A dedicated permanent code exists on paper. "This dedicated Category I code is set to take effect on January 1, 2028, and stands to meaningfully simplify coding and reimbursement, thereby expanding access to Inspire therapy," chairman and chief executive Tim Herbert said on 2 November. The valuation process that sets the physician fee begins with a committee panel in January 2027, and management conceded the shorter Inspire V procedure could be valued lower. Chief financial officer Matt Osberg guided to "low single-digit year-over-year revenue growth for the full year 2027." The market has taken him at his word and then some: forward price-to-sales of 1.82x sits above the 1.76x trailing, which is a bet on another decline, even as the company holds $453.1m of cash, no debt and the cheapest gross-profit multiple of the three at 2.05x trailing. Barclays cut its target to $62 at underweight on 3 November and the shares have fallen every week since.

How much of this the numbers account for

For Procept and Axogen, most of it. Both moves rest on disclosed procedure counts and a raise, and in Axogen's case on a reimbursement schedule with dates attached. For Inspire the de-rating is also earned, by a coding gap that is arithmetic rather than demand, which is why its 86% margin and free cash flow sit awkwardly beside a price that assumes shrinkage through 2027. What the shelf does not have is one shared mechanism: the category average was also lifted by LivaNova and Integra LifeSciences on their own quarters, inside a November rotation out of artificial-intelligence equities into healthcare.

Axogen closes at the highest level in its record on a market it will not begin developing until 2027, and the next hard numbers for it land at a March 2027 investor day. Inspire's own fix arrives on a calendar nobody can accelerate, and the company has to fund a sales organization through more than a year of temporary billing to reach it.
