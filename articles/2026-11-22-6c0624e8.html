Coherent Keeps Three Times Fabrinet's Margin on the Same 800-Gigabit Optical Link

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Fabrinet and Coherent both grew revenue about 50% in their September quarters, and both shares have fallen by roughly a third since August. But the two are paid for entirely different work, and only one got more profitable doing it.

Fabrinet, which assembles transceivers in Thailand to its customers' designs, held gross margin at 11.8%, where it has sat for five quarters. Coherent, which makes the indium-phosphide lasers inside the modules it sells, expanded gross margin to 39.1% and pulled its long-run target up to above 45%. Coherent's operating income rose 119% on 50% more revenue; Fabrinet's gross profit grew more slowly than its sales.

The market has not discriminated between the two models, pricing both off the same hyperscaler budget. The one genuine deterioration is cash: both now run negative free cash flow while funding capacity.

Two companies ship the optics that carry traffic between artificial-intelligence accelerators, and in the quarters just reported both grew revenue by about half. Only Coherent got more profitable doing it. Fabrinet, which assembles transceivers, tunable lasers and active optical cables in Thailand to specifications its customers own, booked $1,474.5m of revenue in its fiscal first quarter and kept 11.8 cents of gross profit on each dollar. Coherent, which makes the lasers inside its own datacom modules, booked $2,370.3m and kept 39.1 cents.

That gap is the story the shares ignored. Optics has been read as one trade on hyperscaler capital budgets, and the de-ratings since August say exactly that: Fabrinet is down 32.3% over the last 90 trading sessions and Coherent 32.0%, two near-identical falls for two opposite income statements. The question of who keeps the margin on an 800-gigabit link is not a question the market has priced.

Eleven point eight cents, five quarters running

Fabrinet's gross margin has sat between 11.6% and 12.3% across five reported quarters while revenue rose from $978m to $1,474m. In the September quarter gross profit grew 49.3% against revenue's 50.7%, so the leverage at the gross line was slightly negative. Over five fiscal years the full-year figure has drifted from 12.3% to 12.0% as revenue more than doubled to $4.64bn. Chief executive Seamus Grady has framed the mechanism plainly: as the material percentage in a product goes up, the gross margin percentage typically comes down. Where customers consign the optics, Fabrinet books the conversion fee.

Put the two margin structures against module prices and the per-link split becomes arithmetic. Mature 800-gigabit modules run roughly $350 to $450, with 1.6-terabit parts clustering at $1,100 to $1,500. Applying each company's blended September-quarter margins — neither discloses per-unit economics — a $400 module carries about $47 of gross profit at Fabrinet's structure and about $157 at Coherent's.

The lasers Coherent does not have to buy

Coherent's gross margin has widened 251 basis points over five quarters to 39.1%, and operating income rose 119% on 50% more revenue. The cause is supply it owns: internal indium-phosphide output doubled in the quarter, a quarter ahead of plan. "We pulled that in, right, in only 18 months. Now raising our target to over 45%," chief financial officer Sherri Luther told investors on 4 November, of a gross-margin goal set at above 42% at an investor day roughly 18 months earlier. Chief executive Jim Anderson, on the same call: "calendar 2027, is basically booked out, and the customers are booking out calendar 2028 now." Coherent has also disclosed a five-year agreement worth approximately $20bn with an unnamed major AI company for its PhotonLink integrated-optics platform, with revenue starting in early calendar 2028 against a current run rate near $9.5bn.

What Marvell bought on 2 November

The in-housing risk stopped being theoretical. Marvell agreed to acquire Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at approximately $100bn, putting lasers, transceivers and custom accelerator silicon under one roof. Anderson's answer was ownership of the stack: "there is no other company, I believe, on the planet that has a broader and deeper set of photonic and optical technology." Grady's was scale: "Historically, when there's been consolidation in the industry, it has tended to benefit Fabrinet." Both Lumentum and Marvell are Fabrinet customers.

Fabrinet's own disclosure shows what program timing costs a conversion-fee model. Its fiscal 2026 annual report lists NVIDIA at 16.3% of revenue, down from 27.6%, alongside Cisco at 19.9%, Nokia at 10.7% and Amazon at 10.5%. On those percentages NVIDIA's dollars fell roughly 20%, from about $944m to about $757m, while group revenue grew 36% — concentration fell, and the lost dollars were the largest AI customer's.

One budget, two multiples

The operating results do not account for the fall. Fabrinet guided its December quarter to $1.6bn-$1.65bn and Coherent to $2.55bn-$2.75bn at a 40% to 42% gross margin; consensus looks for 43% revenue growth at Fabrinet and 59% at Coherent this fiscal year. What did deteriorate is cash. Fabrinet's free cash flow was -$137m in the quarter on $159m of capital spending as it lifts capacity toward roughly $10bn of revenue by January 2027; Coherent's capital spending reached $776m against $104m a year earlier, partly funded by customer prepayments, with leverage at 1.1x. Both carry negative trailing free-cash-flow yields, -1.9% and -5.0%.

So the de-rating is a budget markdown, not a verdict on module economics. On 20 November the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% and the whole chain repriced together, and over 90 sessions the silicon names fell harder than either protagonist. What the compression did do is reprice the two models to similar places from very different starting points: Fabrinet at 12.9x forward gross profit against 47.7x price-to-gross-profit in May 2026, Coherent at 8.6x forward against 25.0x then. Arista Networks, which earns a 63.6% gross margin selling the switch the modules plug into, still trades at 25.2x forward gross profit.

The transition everyone fears for the module makers is also the slowest-moving fact in the file. Co-packaged optics ships now, but large-scale deployment is three to five years out. "We don't really think it's one or the other for a long time to come," Grady said of pluggables and co-packaged parts on 2 November. Until that changes, the dollars keep splitting the way the September quarters split them, and one of these two is building the capacity while the other is building the lasers.
