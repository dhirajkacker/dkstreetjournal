Applied Optoelectronics Signed $2.2bn of Minimum Orders During the AI Optical Selloff

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The optical suppliers were marked down through November on the belief that artificial-intelligence interconnect orders had cracked. Both of the group's contracted books got bigger while that was happening.

Applied Optoelectronics disclosed a four-year supply agreement on 24 November with a hyperscale customer it already serves, including about $1.5bn of 800-gigabit transceivers in 2027. Ciena, which reports fiscal fourth-quarter results on 10 December, left its July quarter with $8.5bn of backlog, $800m more than three months earlier.

The deterioration is narrower than the selling implies, and it is specific to Applied Optoelectronics: a 28.7% gross margin on a module, below its own guide, and 96m diluted shares assumed in fourth-quarter guidance. Ciena's trailing earnings multiple compressed; its order book did not.

Applied Optoelectronics told the market on 24 November that a hyperscale data-centre customer it already supplies had committed to roughly $2.2bn of minimum purchases running from December 2026 to December 2030. It is the company's second such agreement since October, and the larger one by a wide margin. It arrived in the middle of the worst stretch the optical suppliers have had.

That timing is the story. November's selling was read as evidence that the order book behind artificial-intelligence networking had peaked; what the disclosures of the past three weeks show is contracted demand getting firmer at both of the group's order-book businesses while the shares fell. The genuine deterioration is narrower, and it sits at the per-module line and in the share count paying for the factories.

What the minimums cover

The new agreement specifies about $1.5bn of 800-gigabit transceivers in 2027 and roughly $0.7bn of laser diodes across 2028 and 2029, some intended for co-packaged optics from 2028. Set against the $2.57bn of fiscal 2027 revenue that consensus already carries, that 2027 tranche underwrites close to 58% of next year's expected sales, a figure derived from the two disclosures. The company said the same customer's forecast demand may exceed $10bn over four years; only the $2.2bn is committed.

It was signalled in advance.

"The second LTA is in the final stage. We expect to sign it within a few weeks. The size will be much, much bigger than first one, will be even 5x bigger or even more than that," founder and chief executive Thompson Lin said on the 5 November earnings call. The announcement followed nineteen days later.

The first agreement, from October, covers 1.6-terabit transceivers through May 2029, obliges the customer to take 10% of certain 1.6-terabit needs, and is expected to produce $1.0–1.5bn. The quarterly filing also records that the company agreed to certain maximum pricing levels and that "LTAs of this type are not typical for the industry and AOI has not been a party to an agreement of this type before."

The margin is set by speed grade; the capacity is paid for in shares

September-quarter revenue of $269.9m was up 127.5% year on year, the sixth consecutive record, and net income of $6.3m returned the company to profit on a reported basis. Gross margin did not come with it: 28.7% on a non-GAAP basis against a 29.0–30.5% guide, because 400-gigabit product supplied $93.4m of data-centre revenue and 800-gigabit only $47.5m. The operating line was still negative, at -$1.4m.

Funding the ramp has cost holders their share of it. Diluted shares were 87.8m in the quarter against 63.3m a year earlier, fourth-quarter guidance assumes 96m, and the company has authorised roughly $2.25bn of at-the-market programmes, including $550m launched on 17 November. Its $124.9m of 2.75% convertible notes carry a conversion price near $43.31, above the market. Capacity, meanwhile, slipped: the end-2026 target fell to more than 500,000 units a month from 650,000, with a Sugar Land expansion phase delayed on electricity and power logistics.

Everything with an AI label fell together

From 23 October to 24 November the four optical names fell 41.4% (Applied Optoelectronics), 35.6% (Lumentum), 35.2% (Adtran) and 24.5% (Ciena). Over the same sessions Marvell fell 42.4% and Nvidia 28.6%, against a 5.4% decline in the S&P 500 tracker. Applied Optoelectronics lost 19.3% on 5 November, which was attributed to fourth-quarter guidance below Wall Street projections; the other three fell that day without a disclosure of their own, as voters rejected data centres in 40 of 41 local ballot measures. On 20 November, Nvidia alone dropped 14%.

The component-pricing explanation does not survive contact with Lumentum, which makes the lasers inside the modules: its September-quarter gross margin was 51.0% against 34.0% a year earlier, on revenue up 136.5%. Adtran, the one member with no meaningful AI revenue, shows where the cost went. "Generally speaking, I mean, it's 80%-90% supply cost," chief executive Tom Stanton said on 3 November, naming optics, lasers, semiconductors and memory; its revenue grew 0.6% and it trades at 0.8x trailing gross profit.

Ciena's $8.5bn comes up for inspection

Ciena, which sells coherent optical transport systems and Blue Planet automation software to carriers and cloud operators, left its July quarter with $8.5bn of backlog, $800m more than three months earlier, most of it with 2027 request dates, and guided to more than $10bn by year-end. Revenue rose 37.0%, gross margin reached 45.4% and operating margin went from 6.0% to 18.0%. "I think it's almost entirely just driven by a function of lead times. The demand is absolutely there," chief executive Gary Smith told analysts on 3 September. The shares change hands at 20.5x forward earnings against 52.6x trailing, and at 13.0x trailing gross profit against 31.1x in early May; brokers cut targets through November while keeping their ratings, Evercore ISI to $375 from $550.

Nothing disclosed in the past three weeks shows a customer stepping back.

So the results account for almost none of the move. Revenue at both companies accelerated through the drawdown, and the single figure that genuinely worsened at Applied Optoelectronics, its 28.7% module margin against 31.0% a year earlier, is a mix outcome the long-term agreements are designed to shift toward higher speed grades. What the market repriced is the certainty of 2027, and it repriced it across Nvidia and Marvell in the same sessions. The conditional part is specific: 8.8x fiscal 2027 consensus earnings exists only if $2.57bn of revenue arrives, and arrives at a margin that services a share count half again as large as last year's. Applied Optoelectronics now says its ceiling is its own supply, not its customers': more than $450m of 1.6-terabit orders sit behind $50–70m of deliverable fourth-quarter revenue, held up by signal-processor availability.

Both of those contracted books are promises about shipments nobody has seen yet. Ciena opens its own before the market on 10 December, the first occasion since the selling began for anyone to check whether the backlog reads the way it did in September, and whether the orders behind it still carry the request dates they were booked with.
