Three Chip Toolmakers' Order Books Grew in the Month Their Shares Fell About a Fifth

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The market spent November pricing the end of the artificial-intelligence tool cycle. Every order book involved got bigger while it did so.

Applied Materials printed a record quarter and guided the next one to $11.1bn, up 58% year on year, saying clean-room space rather than customer demand caps how fast it grows. ASML's third-quarter release says planned 2027 and 2028 output will not fully meet customer demand, with some customers asking to place orders for 2029. ACM Research raised full-year guidance twice and carries a record $2.5bn backlog at its Shanghai subsidiary on orders up 147% so far this year.

Nothing in the three sets of results explains month-long declines of 18% to 21%. What the three-month split does explain is duration: the contracted lithography backlog fell least, the policy-dependent China book most.

Three of the companies that sell the machines inside a chip fab have reported since mid-October, and each one told investors its order book had grown. Applied Materials, whose deposition, etch, implant and process-control tools go into foundry, memory and advanced-packaging lines, closed its fiscal year with quarterly revenue of $10.41bn, up 53%, and guided the January quarter to $11.1bn give or take $500m.

November read this shelf as the place where artificial-intelligence capital spending runs out, and the reading was applied group-wide, to backlogged and unbacklogged books alike. What the three books describe is a supply constraint. The one switch capable of turning an equipment order off sits in Washington, and its current setting expires on 10 January.

ASML took away the number everyone watched

ASML, the Dutch maker of the lithography scanners that print circuit patterns and the only supplier of extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) machines, grew third-quarter revenue 55% to €11.66bn at a 57.4% gross margin, its third consecutive quarterly expansion. Its results release says order intake remains very strong, that backlog continues to increase across a broad mix of customers, and that some customers want to place orders for 2029 and even 2030. "Given the dynamics of this quarter, we now anticipate that our current planned levels of output in 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet the demand of our customers," chief financial officer Roger Dassen said on the 14 October call.

Investors cannot check that against an orders print, because ASML stopped disclosing quarterly net bookings in 2026, retiring the metric that had moved its shares more than any other. The company is also raising prices on its most critical high-volume products from 2027 without repricing existing backlog, which is the plainest demonstration that the backlog fixes revenue per tool years ahead. The shares now trade at 22.9x next year's consensus earnings, against 30.6x three months ago and roughly 40x in May.

Applied Materials guided up and had its targets cut on valuation

Applied's Semiconductor Systems segment turned over $8bn in the October quarter, up 64%, at a 39% operating margin, and the company logged its fourteenth straight quarter of year-on-year gross-margin expansion. It guides only one quarter ahead and publishes no backlog; the substitute it offers is a count of customer projects, more than 20 fab ramps or line expansions tracked for 2027, with more than 10 major projects added for a third consecutive quarter.

"Nobody is saying that they're not wanting the tools as fast as we can get them," chief executive Gary Dickerson told investors on 12 November. "I've been traveling quite a bit over the last couple of months, and we're constantly receiving requests from customers to accelerate our ramp and deliver earlier." Management expects industry equipment spending up more than 30% in 2027 and is targeting double its current systems output by end-2028, naming clean-room availability as the binding constraint.

The shares fell anyway, 19.7% in the month to 25 November. At least nine brokers cut price targets while keeping Buy or Overweight ratings, and Bank of America moved its target multiple from 27x to 20x 2028 earnings, citing equipment-sector multiple compression. On unchanged consensus of $19.33 for fiscal 2027, the stock is at 16.1x, against 24.7x at its 25 August close.

ACM Research's book is the one a rule can reach

ACM Research, which sells single-wafer wet-cleaning, plating and furnace tools under the Ultra C brand, grew third-quarter revenue 36.6% to $367.7m and widened its operating margin to 15.1% from 10.7%. Plating and furnace was the engine at $109.5m, up 83% and 30% of revenue. Backlog at ACM Shanghai reached RMB 17.1bn, about $2.5bn, up 88%, and on 5 November the company raised 2026 guidance to $1.24bn-$1.28bn from $1.125bn-$1.175bn.

It is also the most exposed. Substantially all of its first-half revenue came from customers in mainland China, where spending answers to domestic substitution policy; chief executive David Wang said on the same call that "China peak will be behind that [2028-2029] by a year or two." Reported GAAP earnings are not usable as an anchor here, since minority interests in the Shanghai subsidiary left diluted earnings per share at $0.02 against the $0.55 presented on the call. Against consensus, the stock sits at 13.0x next year's $3.11, versus 25.4x in late August.

The month did not sort; the quarter did

Over 30 days the decline ignored how the revenue is booked: ASML fell 18.1%, Applied 19.7% and ACM Research 21.0%, inside a selloff that took Teradyne down 31% and Lam Research 22% and included a 9.2% single-session drop in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index in early November. Stretch the window to three months and the ordering is exactly contract duration: ASML down 25%, Applied 35%, ACM Research 49%.

That is a de-rating rather than a downgrade cycle. Forward estimates were not cut behind any of the three, equipment forecasts for next year were raised into the selloff, and SEMI projects record equipment sales of $156bn in 2027. What separates the three is not demand but who holds the permission to buy. ASML's revenue for the next two years is contracted at fixed prices. Applied's turns on fab build schedules this quarter and next, with China at 25% of October-quarter revenue and guided below 25% of fiscal 2027 shipments. ACM Research's customers are the Chinese fabs that American rules govern directly.

Applied has quantified that risk itself: the Bureau of Industry and Security Affiliates Rule, which extends controls to affiliates of listed Chinese entities, would have cost it about $600m of fiscal 2026 revenue before its suspension. On 9 November the bureau extended that suspension only to 10 January 2027.

Six weeks from now, one of these three order books needs a renewal from a government to stay worth what the company says it is. The other two need clean rooms.
