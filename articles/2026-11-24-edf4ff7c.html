Albemarle and SQM Price Lithium Off a Lagging Index; the Halved Spot Hits Next Quarter

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Both producers reported third-quarter realized lithium prices near $20 a kilogram of carbonate equivalent while the Chinese spot price that sets those contracts has roughly halved since May. The index resets with a lag, so most of the markdown has yet to reach either income statement.

Deterioration has begun arriving anyway. Albemarle's gross margin fell from 33.9% in the second quarter to 19.4% in the third as higher-cost spodumene inventory met a lower price, and SQM's realized price fell 10% sequentially before it warned that the fourth quarter could be substantially lower.

What cushions the fall splits them. Albemarle's Specialties segment is guided to $350-375m of adjusted earnings for all of 2026 against $560m from Energy Storage in one quarter, and a mandatory preferred converts on 1 March 2027. SQM draws about a quarter of gross profit from iodine and plant nutrition.

Albemarle, the Charlotte specialty chemicals producer that sells lithium carbonate, hydroxide and chloride to battery and cathode makers, booked an average realized Energy Storage price of $20.02 per kilogram of lithium carbonate equivalent in the third quarter, against $10.28 a year earlier. The Chinese battery-grade carbonate price that increasingly sets that number closed at 89,550 yuan a tonne on 16 November, roughly 44% below its 24 August level.

Through September the setup read as a markdown running ahead of the numbers: both companies were posting record lithium margins against a falling screen price, and nothing in the reported results had cracked. The prints of 5 and 18 November closed that gap. Albemarle's gross margin fell to 19.4% in the third quarter from 33.9% in the second, and operating margin to 9.5% from 26.0%, as the company processed higher-priced spodumene inventory into a lower selling price. Revenue was still up 18.8% from a year earlier. The year-over-year optics and the sequential ones now point in opposite directions, and only one of them is forward-looking.

The lag cuts the other way now

Sociedad Química y Minera de Chile, which mines lithium and iodine from Atacama brine and caliche ore in northern Chile, realized about $19.6 a kilogram in the third quarter, down 10% from the second even as volumes reached roughly 73,300 tonnes of lithium products. Group revenue of $2.089bn was 78.1% above the prior year and 15.4% below the prior quarter. In the same filing the company disclosed that it had observed a significant decline in market prices in recent weeks, suggesting its fourth-quarter average realized price could be substantially lower than the third's. The shares fell 5.5% in New York to $48.75 the following session.

Neither company accepts the signal. "The fundamentals in the physical market do not support what we are seeing in the spot market now," chief commercial officer Eric Norris told Albemarle's investors on 5 November, attributing the slide to speculation about future supply. Citigroup took the opposite view on 2 November, cutting its 12-to-24-month carbonate target to $12,000 a tonne from $20,000 and forecasting surplus over the next two years, as restarts at CATL, Mineral Resources and Core Lithium brought idled capacity back. That target sits about 40% below what both companies realized last quarter. Volumes, meanwhile, keep rising into it: Albemarle guides 240,000-260,000 tonnes of carbonate equivalent for 2027, and SQM to slightly over 300,000 tonnes.

Albemarle's cushion is one segment thin

Albemarle's bromine and catalysts arm is the hedge, and it is small. "We are increasing the specialties adjusted EBITDA outlook to $350 million-$375 million," chief financial officer Neal Sheorey said on 5 November — a full-year figure against $560m of Energy Storage adjusted earnings in the third quarter alone. The self-help is largely spent, with $150m of run-rate cost improvement delivered year to date at the high end of target, and capital expenditure already cut to $300-350m from about $500m. At 7.6x forward earnings the stock looks cheap until the denominator is dated: consensus already models 2027 earnings of $7.97 a share against $10.69 this year, which puts it nearer 10x next year's number. Book value of 0.90x is the one anchor arguing the other way. Set against that, 2.3m mandatory convertible preferred shares convert automatically on 1 March 2027 into as many as 9.14 common shares each, roughly 21m shares or 17.7% of the third quarter's diluted count at the maximum ratio.

Iodine does the work lithium cannot

SQM's buffer is both larger and unrelated. Its iodine division earned $595m of gross profit on $1.11bn of trailing revenue and a 37% global share, about 21% of group gross profit, selling into x-ray contrast media that management expects to grow more than 3% in 2026 and 2027. Specialty plant nutrition adds another 5%, on much thinner margins and with its own problem: volume growth was cut to about 5% because high fertilizer prices suppressed demand. The resource question is settled further out. Codelco holds 50% plus one share of the NovaAndino Litio venture, with SQM keeping operational control through 2030 and the state copper company managing the Salar from 2031 to 2060. On 4.53x trailing enterprise value to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, SQM is the cheaper of the two against Albemarle's 6.98x, with an 18.7% trailing free-cash-flow yield.

Shares in both have fallen about 43% since 24 August and have been in a sustained downtrend since late September. The wider specialty-minerals shelf they sit in is up on the year, but that average belongs to titanium dioxide, carbon black and lime: Kronos Worldwide is 58% higher over twelve months while Albemarle is 30% lower and SQM 20% lower.

So the de-rating has a cause, and it is per-tonne rather than sentiment. What it does not yet have is the proof in print. One quarter of margin compression at each company reflects inventory cost and a 10% price reset, while the spot move is more than four times that size and arrives on a contractual delay. Albemarle carries the harder version: a thin non-lithium earnings base, dilution dated to March, and a chief executive handover to Rag Udd on 1 February. SQM's iodine franchise and brine cost position mean its 2027 consensus of $6.12 a share has further to fall before the business stops earning, but that estimate still assumes lithium far above $12,000 a tonne.

Pablo Hernandez, strategy head at SQM's Novandino division, told investors on 18 November that the current price "seems to be sitting below the necessary incentive price required to bring new supply," and so "we should expect some price increase in the future." He named no date, which is the whole trade: the companies are arguing cost-curve logic against a futures curve that only has to be right for two quarters.
