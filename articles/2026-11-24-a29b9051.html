Optical Modules Buy Triple the Timing Chips, and Four RF Suppliers Just Set Revenue Records

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A shelf of radio-frequency chipmakers was sold through November as though base-station and handset cycles still set its revenue. The prints from the first week of the month say the revenue now comes from inside optical modules, where each speed upgrade adds silicon.

SiTime's quarter was $298.7m, up 257% year on year, with its communications and data-center line up 202%; management says co-packaged optics carries three times the timing content of the electrical modules it replaces. MACOM's data-center revenue reached 44% of a record $424.8m quarter, with a 1.4-to-1 book-to-bill and only 7% of the quarter shipped from orders taken in it.

Semtech and MaxLinear posted records too. The one order book that is genuinely flat belongs to the automotive name, indie Semiconductor, which fell least.

In the first week of November two companies that sit on the same radio-frequency shelf reported the largest quarters in their histories, and the growth came from the same narrow place: the silicon inside a high-speed optical module. MACOM Technology Solutions, which makes analog, microwave and photonic parts for optical networks, radar and base stations, booked $424.8m in its September quarter, up 62.7% on the year, with data center at $188.1m, or 44% of the total. SiTime, which sells silicon MEMS timing devices against incumbent quartz crystals, reported $298.7m, up 257%.

The shelf was marked down anyway, and the reason given was that radio chips were rolling over. That reading assumes these businesses still turn with handset and base-station builds. The November disclosures describe something else: a content-per-box cycle in which the bill of materials for an optical module grows every time the industry steps up a speed grade, so revenue rises without a single extra module shipping.

What a speed grade adds to a module

SiTime put numbers on the step. Moving from electrical modules to extended pluggable optics doubles timing content; near-package optics drives twice as many oscillators; co-packaged optics drives three times the content. Coherent modules need gigahertz-class precision, where parts sell for $6 to $8 each against $1 to $2 for megahertz devices, an uplift of up to five times. "More bandwidth requires more precision timing," chief executive Rajesh Vashist told investors on 4 November.

The mix is doing what that implies. SiTime's communications, enterprise and data-center line was $127.3m, up 202% year on year. Eight-hundred-gigabit revenue grew fourfold while management conceded it had held low share at 400G, so this is share capture layered on a unit ramp. The acquired Renesas timing division contributed $86.3m from 1 July; strip it out and core revenue still grew 154%, which settles whether the quarter was bought. What is not disclosed is units or distributor inventory weeks, and the binding constraint is wafer supply, which management expects to stay tight through 2027.

SiTime holds roughly 95% of the MEMS oscillator market and competes mostly against quartz suppliers, which still account for about 72% of a frequency-control market put at $6.84bn this year. The conversion, not the cycle, is the growth.

MACOM shipped 7% of its quarter from inside it

MACOM's evidence is its order book. The company ended fiscal 2026 with record backlog, a 1.4-to-1 book-to-bill in the fourth quarter and 1.5-to-1 for the year, its heaviest bookings year. Only 7% of quarterly revenue shipped from orders received in the same quarter, which management attributed to backlog depth and extended lead times. Gross margin rose every quarter of the year, from 54.6% to 59.9%, and the January quarter is guided to 62% to 63% on revenue of $450m to $460m.

"Being able to better utilize some of our existing manufacturing facilities has been a contributor," chief financial officer John Kober said on 5 November, naming volume, utilization and new products. The soft spot is the old business: "We see some softness in PON. We see some softness in some of our broadband business," chief executive Steve Daly said on the same call. Telecom is $72.1m and grew 1% sequentially. Industrial and defence, at $164.6m, includes follow-on shipborne radar and air-to-air missile orders and a low-Earth-orbit constellation win worth about $20m beginning in early 2028.

The names that were supposed to be deteriorating

The competitors held up the same way. Semtech reported record sales of $423.0m on 23 November, up 58%, with a record 33% adjusted operating margin and data-center revenue guided up about 160% year on year on 800G and 1.6-terabit ramps. MaxLinear's September quarter grew 72%, with infrastructure revenue near $125m, up 210%, and its optical data-center outlook raised to $250m to $270m. Both fell harder over 30 days than either protagonist.

One order book in this group is genuinely flat: indie Semiconductor's automotive strategic backlog held at $6.2bn year on year, with a $39.0m operating loss on $70.2m of revenue. It was the best 30-day performer of the five, and at 4.0x trailing gross profit it carries a sixth of MACOM's rating, so the market is discriminating by business quality even while the shelf moves together.

The markdown came from outside the order books

The timing points elsewhere. The Nasdaq had its worst week since 2020 in the week to 20 November on doubts about artificial-intelligence profitability, and when Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang on 23 November, MACOM rose 16.8%, SiTime 18.8%, MaxLinear 22.6% and Semtech 17.5% in one session. These names traded as exposure to the artificial-intelligence complex, not as a radio order book.

What the drawdown did do is reset the rating against rising profits. MACOM's price to trailing gross profit stands at 23.1x, against roughly 50x on 22 May, a figure derived from that day's close and trailing gross profit, which has since grown 30%; its forward price-to-earnings ratio of 23.6x sits on consensus fiscal 2027 earnings of $9.80 a share, up 78%. Needham cut its target to $300 from $410 while keeping a Buy. SiTime remains the dearest of the five at 34.5x trailing gross profit, down from about 90x in May, and that figure is distorted upward by purchase accounting on the Renesas deal, which held reported gross margin to 51.6% against 70.7% on an adjusted basis.

So the business explains none of the three- and six-month declines at either company and the valuation reset explains all of it. What remains unresolved is supply: SiTime's wafers are short into next year, and MACOM is lifting capital spending to $100m-$110m with about 60% aimed at fab expansion. The content per module is rising faster than either company can currently build it, and that is a different problem from the one the shares were sold on.
