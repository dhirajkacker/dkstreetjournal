Akamai Is Now Paid by the Megawatt, and the Switch Is Eating Its Gross Profit

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The part of Akamai everyone expected to break is healing, and the damage has landed somewhere nobody was watching. Content delivery revenue fell 3% year on year in the September quarter against 7% in the March quarter, and group revenue growth accelerated to 6.4%.

What fell was profit. Gross margin dropped to 55.0% from 59.3%, so gross profit declined while revenue rose, and quarterly capital spending reached 41% of revenue. Akamai has contracted more than $14bn of multi-year compute capacity, 83% of it with Anthropic, and pre-funded the hardware with $2.5bn of new convertible notes on top of roughly $7.6bn already outstanding.

Cloudflare, growing 39% with net retention of 122% and capital spending at 15% of revenue, is priced at 47.6x forward gross profit against Akamai's 5.2x.

Akamai Technologies, which runs customers' web traffic, security and now rented compute across servers embedded inside internet service providers' networks, has spent a year being marked down for a problem that is getting better. Its content delivery book, the price-deflating legacy everyone watches, shrank 3% year on year in the September quarter after falling 7% in the March quarter and 6% in June. Group revenue growth accelerated to 6.4%. What deteriorated instead was the profit line: gross margin fell to 55.0% from 59.3% a year earlier, so gross profit declined 1.3% while revenue rose, and operating income dropped 34.5% to $108.7m.

The de-rating was read as the compact-disc ending of the content delivery network business. The filings describe something else: a company that has stopped being paid per delivered gigabyte and started being paid per contracted megawatt, and is paying for the switch in cash well before the revenue arrives. That distinction decides whether 13.5x forward earnings is a discount or a warning.

The delivery book is no longer the thing breaking

Akamai's own quarterly filing names the old mechanism plainly: the decline in delivery was "driven by a decrease in delivery revenue due to downward pricing of contract renewals," with prices paid by some customers having declined in recent years at renewal due to competition. That book is now $391m of a $1.122bn quarter, about 35% of revenue, and management attributes part of the shrinkage to exiting low-margin deals. Security revenue was $622m, up 9%; cloud infrastructure services were $109m, up 34%.

$22m a megawatt, bought with converts

Akamai signed more than $14bn of multi-year compute commitments during 2026, against $2.8bn as of August. The $11.6bn, seven-year Anthropic contract for central-processing-unit workloads is roughly 83% of that, and carries warrants over about 2% of Akamai's shares, with up to 3% more vesting as the commitment expands. Management puts the economics at about $22m of revenue per contracted megawatt and a 13% after-tax return.

The bill comes first. September-quarter capital spending was $464m, or 41% of revenue, against $433m of operating cash flow. Fourth-quarter capex is guided to $2.445-2.655bn, including a $1.7bn pre-purchase of memory, because builders of artificial-intelligence infrastructure are competing for the same scarce dynamic random-access memory. Akamai completed a $2.5bn convertible note offering on 12 November, on top of roughly $7.6bn of converts already outstanding against a $12.8bn equity value. No shares were repurchased in the September quarter, after $616m was spent earlier in the year at a weighted average $123.19.

"We now anticipate that our CIS revenue will more than double in 2027 to $1.1 billion to $1.3 billion," chief executive F. Thomson Leighton told investors on 5 November, with total 2027 revenue growing 17% to 22% in constant currency. Chief financial officer Ed McGowan, the same day, on renting colocation space ahead of the revenue: "We estimate that will put about 1 point or 2 of pressure on margins next year."

The market is pricing the financing, not the gigabytes

The shares have held a downtrend since early September, and the break predates both catalysts: it arrived more than two weeks before the Anthropic expansion was announced on 24 September and two months before the third-quarter report, which cost 8% in a session. The stock is down 40% over six months but only 2% over twelve. Evercore ISI's Peter Levine cut his rating to In Line on 10 November, writing that "with financing concerns adding to an already complex CIS investment cycle, we see shares remaining range-bound until 3Q/4Q27."

November's last leg hit every edge network alike. Fastly fell 42.5% over the month and Cloudflare 14.6%, in a de-rating of artificial-intelligence-exposed software that began with late-October software results.

Cloudflare still shows what the opposite pricing model does to unit economics. Its flat-rate subscription plus consumption took revenue up 39% to $782.5m, with dollar-based net retention of 122% and network capital spending at 15% of revenue. It is priced at 47.6x forward gross profit, about nine times what a dollar of Akamai's forward gross profit costs at 5.2x.

So the business explains only half the move. Revenue estimates rose through the collapse, with consensus at 19.6% growth for 2027, while earnings estimates fell: 2026 consensus earnings per share sit 6.6% below 2025. That is the signature of a debt-financed capacity build, not of a multiple-only markdown. Against Akamai's own range, 2025 gross profit of $2.481bn on 147.0m diluted shares works out to roughly $16.87 a share, so the 52-week high close of $161.14 valued it near 9.5x price-to-gross-profit, against 5.3x on trailing figures now. Consensus has 2027 earnings up just 5% on that 19.6% revenue gain, because the depreciation lands first.

The counterparty behind 83% of the contracted book now has a daily quote of its own. Anthropic traded as low as $83.10 on 20 November, ten cents above its offering price, on its second day as a public company.
