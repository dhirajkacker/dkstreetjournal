Datadog's Largest Customer Cut Its Usage; JFrog Had Sold Minimum Commitments Instead

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Consumption pricing was read as software's defense against AI thinning out seat counts. Datadog grew revenue 35.2% in the September quarter, raised full-year guidance, and then told investors its biggest customer had dialed usage down and that fourth-quarter guidance assumes that customer pays only its minimum. The shares lost more than 40% over the following 30 sessions.

JFrog and Appian, which reported the same day, held their gains through the rout. JFrog's remaining performance obligations reached $812m, up 60%, a figure management stressed excludes overages above customer minimums; Appian's cloud net annual recurring revenue expansion hit 120%, its best in more than five years, as AI adoption moved customers to a tier priced 25-35% above Standard.

Contract structure explains most of the split. It does not explain ServiceNow, which had committed revenue and lost the margin instead, or Veeva, which fell for eight sessions with no disclosure at all.

Datadog, which sells cloud monitoring, log management and security observability to developers and IT operations teams, told investors on its third-quarter call that its largest customer had reduced usage during the quarter and that fourth-quarter guidance assumes that customer spends only its minimum commitment. The quarter underneath that sentence was excellent: revenue of $1.197bn grew 35.2%, the non-GAAP operating margin reached 28%, and full-year guidance went up to roughly 32% growth. The shares lost 42.4% over the 30 sessions to November 20.

That disclosure is the hinge the November software selloff turned on. Usage-based pricing had been read as the structural answer to the fear that AI coding agents shrink per-seat software: bill the machine instead of the employee. Datadog's own management described the mechanism running in reverse, with early renewals of other AI-native customers struck at discounted rates. A meter with no floor beneath it is exposure. Where a contract sets the floor, the same AI usage arrives as revenue the customer has already promised.

The floor JFrog sold, and what it leaves out

JFrog, whose Artifactory repository stores and versions the binaries, containers and packages moving through corporate software pipelines, reported into the worst tech selloff since Covid, triggered by midterm votes curtailing AI data-centre construction. Revenue grew 30.9%, a fourth consecutive acceleration from 25.2% a year earlier. Remaining performance obligations hit a record $812m, up 60%, and management emphasized that the figure excludes usage overages above customer minimums: it is the contracted floor rather than the meter reading. Net dollar retention reached 124%, up six points, with gross retention steady at 97%.

"This deal represents 167% growth in cloud data consumption compared to the customer's prior usage commitment," chief executive Shlomi Ben Haim said on the third-quarter call, describing a $76m multi-year software-as-a-service contract with a major telecom. Free-cash-flow margin reached 37% against 21% a year earlier. The shares rose 35.3% the next day.

Price is the problem here. JFrog trades at 24.27x trailing gross profit against 24.93x in late August, so the multiple went sideways while gross profit grew 34.6% and the last three months of share price were paid for by the income statement. The earlier leg was not: price to sales has roughly doubled from about 9x in May. The competitive list has not changed either, with GitHub Packages, Amazon's ECR container registry and Azure artifact bundles competing on price inside hyperscaler-native shops.

Appian's cash went backwards in its best expansion quarter in five years

Appian, a low-code platform that generates workflows and interfaces for banks, government agencies and insurers, reported cloud subscription revenue of $136.4m, up 20%, with total revenue growth accelerating to 25.5% and cloud net annual recurring revenue expansion at 120%, the strongest in more than five years. The uplift came from customers moving to an Advanced Tier priced 25-35% above Standard; management called AI consumption monetization beginning but not yet material.

"Appian has claimed a distinctive and valuable place in the AI stack," chief executive Matthew Calkins told investors on the third-quarter call. Full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance went to $116-119m.

The cash did not follow. Operating cash flow was -$15m against +$19m a year earlier on collection timing, and cash and investments fell to $89m from $187m at the end of 2025 while the company repurchased $17m of stock. At 6.05x trailing gross profit Appian is the cheapest of these names, but that is up from 3.63x in late July, and an enterprise value of 158 times trailing EBITDA says the cash earnings have not arrived. A retrial of its trade-secret case against Pegasystems is set for January 11, 2027, with the judge allowing Appian to seek a reasonable royalty of $284.6m plus $1.36bn.

A committed floor did not save ServiceNow's margin

ServiceNow's September quarter contained everything the floor thesis wants: revenue up 20.5%, current remaining performance obligations up 21% in constant currency, a 98% renewal rate, and an AI contract-value target raised for the third time. "Today, we are raising that target again this time to $1.7 billion plus by year-end," chief executive Bill McDermott said on October 28. The shares still fell 42.8% over 30 sessions, because gross profit grew only 9.95% as gross margin dropped to 70.5% from 77.3% and operating income fell 32.7%. Committed AI revenue that costs this much to deliver is not the asset a committed subscription is. The workflow vendor now trades at 8.06x trailing gross profit, cheaper on that measure than JFrog.

Veeva fell for eight sessions without publishing anything

Veeva, which sells the clinical, regulatory and commercial systems of record used by the global life-sciences industry, is the outlier. Its most recent numbers are from August 26: revenue of $928.0m, up 17.6%, with operating margin at 29.64% against 24.83% a year earlier and full-year subscription guidance lifted to about $3.080bn. "AI is opening up the next big chapter for Veeva and life sciences," chief executive Peter Gassner said in that release. Then the stock fell eight consecutive sessions to November 20, losing 14.2% and about $7.1bn of market value, with no announcement, earnings report or filing. It trades at 42.9x trailing earnings, essentially where it stood in August at 42.3x, and 28.8x forward.

So contract structure explains most of the split and not all of it. The two vendors that rose through the rout monetize AI inside a commitment the customer has already signed, a tier upgrade at Appian and a minimum-commitment floor at JFrog, and both put accelerating revenue against it on the same day. Datadog's drawdown traces to one disclosed sentence about one account. The workflow case shows a floor is necessary and insufficient when the cost of serving it moves. Veeva's decline contains no issuer event at all: a life-sciences compounder marked down inside a market-wide session, carrying per-seat exposure of its own after it priced the new Aspen CRM at $50 a seat for customers outside life sciences.

Veeva reports third-quarter results on December 3, the first of this group to set a disclosure against the new price. The others have already told the market what their customers owe them, and the market has decided that promises are worth more than meters.

Originating hypothesis

software bull streak vs sector derating · subject: APPN, FROG, VEEV

While the large application- and infrastructure-software names rolled out of bull bands in the last 7-30 days (NOW, DDOG, TWLO and CRM into mildly bearish, CRWD into strongly bearish), three mid-cap software vendors have quietly held a strongly bullish band for 90-100+ consecutive days — Appian (APPN, 101 days, plus a strongly bearish-to-strongly bullish transition over 180 days), JFrog (FROG, 91 days) and Veeva (VEEV, 102 days) — and the question is whether these streaks rest on a real shared mechanism (multi-year committed enterprise contracts, consumption-based AI and dev-tooling expansion, disclosed AI-attributed revenue) that is still earning the band, or on multiple recovery off depressed 2025 bases inside a sector the market is otherwise de-rating.
