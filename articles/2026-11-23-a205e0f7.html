AAON Says Most of Its Data-Center Backlog Sits Beyond Reach of State Moratoriums

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Three cooling-equipment makers were marked down together in November on a political event, and only one of them has told investors how much of its order book a building ban could actually touch. AAON put the figure at under a third of its data-center backlog, with more than 80% of it held by well-capitalized customers.

All three accelerated into the selloff. Data-center equipment is now 56% of AAON's revenue and 44% of Modine's; at Trane the only sized program is roughly 2% of consensus 2026 revenue and explicitly earnings-neutral. AAON raised its full-year growth guide to 72-75% from 55-60%, Modine raised its guide and crossed $1bn of quarterly orders, Trane's commercial HVAC backlog reached $12.5bn.

What the results do explain is margin: gross margin fell at all three. What they do not explain is where the next site gets permitted.

On 4 November, as Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governorship having campaigned for an immediate moratorium on new data centers and Democrats took both houses of Congress, Modine Manufacturing — which builds precision air conditioning, chillers, air handlers and coils for data centers — fell 21.4%, and AAON fell 19.7%. AAON, a Tulsa maker of rooftop units that also builds purpose-built data-center cooling through its BasX division, had disclosed nothing; its quarter came five days later. AI data-center equipment names fell as much as 24% that session while the S&P 500 rose about 2%, on a state that accounts for roughly 25-30% of planned US capacity.

The order books were read all year as an unconstrained claim on artificial-intelligence capital spending. The election repriced them as a claim on land-use permits, and top Democrats then called for a national moratorium. Only one of the three companies has since put a measurement against that fear.

What a moratorium could actually touch

AAON told investors on 9 November that less than one-third of its BasX backlog sits in potentially regulated data-center states and that more than 80% of it is held by well-capitalized customers, and guided fourth-quarter bookings at or above the year-ago quarter. The backlog it was describing is now the company: BasX-branded revenue reached a record $397m in the September quarter, up 218% year on year, 56% of the $707.4m total, at a 28.7% gross margin. Legacy rooftop work at AAON Oklahoma grew 19%.

"We have approximately $1.4 billion of backlog. Order activity increased significantly during the quarter," chief executive Matthew Tobolski said on the call. AAON raised full-year sales growth guidance to 72-75% from 55-60%. The shares rose 19.3% on 10 November and are the only one of the three higher than a month ago.

The discount it still carries is a governance one. AAON self-identified errors in percentage-of-completion revenue and restated the first half, lifting net sales by $10.7m, and disclosed a material weakness in internal control. "Our financial processes and controls did not keep pace with that growth," Tobolski said. At 26.4x forward earnings, against 67x trailing in early May, the business is cheaper than it was when it was a quarter this size.

A bidder answered for Modine

Modine's rebuttal came from outside. Bloomberg reported on 12 November that Samsung Electronics is in talks to acquire the company, possibly within weeks, and the shares gained 28% the next day to $135.66, the biggest move in six years. Samsung completed its purchase of European HVAC maker FläktGroup in November 2025; a strategic buyer, not a financial one, is what sits under the price.

The underlying quarter was the same shape as AAON's. Data centers reached $439.7m, up 95%, 44% of revenue, and "we achieved our fourth consecutive quarter of record orders, exceeding $1 billion in quarterly order intake for the first time in our history," chief executive Neil Brinker said on 4 November. Guidance went up. The segment's adjusted EBITDA margin was 15.4%, down 470 basis points on materials, components and tariffs. At 24.5x forward earnings against 128x trailing in early May, the stock has absorbed its growth.

The smallest exposure took the deepest month

Trane Technologies, which sells chillers, airside equipment and Thermo King transport refrigeration, fell 16.3% over the 30 days to 20 November, the sharpest of the three, with the least to lose. Its one sized data-center program, Stellar modular chillers, is on track for $500m of 2026 revenue against roughly $24.1bn of consensus, and management calls it earnings-neutral this year. Commercial HVAC backlog reached $12.5bn, up more than 70%, with Americas applied bookings up 40% and 1,700 mega projects tracked, up from 1,400. "The pipeline continues to grow," chief executive David Regnery said on 29 October. Trane also cut full-year pricing to about one point from 1.5. On 2027 consensus earnings it is the dearest of the three, at 21.8x against Modine's 12.6x.

None of the three reported weaker demand. Each of them raised a number this autumn.

Converting the orders is the part the results explain

Gross margin fell at all three — Modine to 20.7%, Trane to 35.3%, AAON down 110 basis points year on year — so the cost of turning record orders into shipped equipment is real and visible. None of that is a demand event. On 20 November the complex moved with the index, with no company disclosure to explain it: the S&P 500 fell 3.7% and Nvidia 14%, in a session attributed partly to worries about the pace of data-center buildouts, while Modine fell 7.4%, Vertiv 10.1%, Trane 3.9% and AAON 2.4%.

Each side of the split has its own cause. AAON held because it raised a number and bounded its political exposure; Modine held because a strategic acquirer appeared; Trane kept a premium earned by positive free cash flow while its data-center content stays too small to carry it. The threat none of the three has answered is further up the rack: Google announced its own coolant distribution unit at OCP EMEA as operators prepare for one-megawatt racks, which is how standardization could cap vendor content per megawatt even as megawatts multiply.

The backlogs say 2027 is largely sold. The election asked a different question — whether the ground gets broken in the counties where those backlogs assume it will — and one company in this group has told anyone what its answer looks like.

Originating hypothesis

category emerging bear cooling mix economics · subject: MOD, AAON, TT

The data-center cooling shelf has rolled into an emerging bear at gradual intensity — the category averages -7.4% over 30 days and -6.3% over twelve months, a far shallower markdown than the -18% to -27% taken by analog, servers and fabless silicon on the same snapshot — and the question is mechanical rather than directional: establish what a megawatt of AI heat rejection actually pays each tier of this business, since Modine's Climate Solutions data-center line and AAON's BasX unit sell purpose-built air- and liquid-cooling systems into hyperscaler and colocation projects at project-specific margins with visible backlog, while Trane carries data centers as a small, fast-growing sliver of a largely commercial-HVAC P&L, so the same direct-to-chip transition can re-rate the pure-plays and the diversified incumbents in opposite directions.
