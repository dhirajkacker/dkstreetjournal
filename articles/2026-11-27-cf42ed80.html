EnerSys Is Relocating Battery Output to Keep a Credit Worth 38% of Operating Profit

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Six small battery and storage companies have been marked down together as though one demand signal were failing. Their November disclosures describe three unrelated businesses, and only one of them reported something broken.

EnerSys held revenue flat at $949.9m while operating income rose 25.5%, orders rose 21% and guidance was not cut, yet the shares are a third below late August, putting trailing earnings at 12.8x against roughly 20.7x then. Eos Energy nearly tripled revenue and drew $87m of a federal loan while still losing money on every system it ships. Enovix is the exception: its silicon-anode cells missed an energy-density target, the smartphone program was deferred, and the Malaysian fab built for it is left underutilized.

EnerSys, which sells reserve-power batteries into data-center and telecom backup systems and motive-power batteries for forklift fleets, told investors on 12 November that it is shifting production out of Mexico and into existing US plants to keep claiming a federal manufacturing credit that delivered $44.4m in the September quarter. That single credit equalled 38% of the quarter's operating income, and the company has stopped guiding to it separately.

The small-cap storage shelf EnerSys sits on has been marked down for a year on the reading that storage demand is failing. The November disclosures from its three largest members say something narrower: what determines which of these businesses earns anything is eligibility, not shipped kilowatt-hours. From 2026 a storage project must show that 55% of its costs come from non-prohibited foreign sources or forfeit the entire 30% investment credit, a threshold rising toward 75%. From 2027, Section 45X also requires that at least 65% of a secondary component's direct material costs trace to domestically produced primary components, integrated at the same facility. That is the rule EnerSys is rebuilding its footprint to pass.

The credit EnerSys has to re-earn

The quarter underneath the credit grew. Revenue was flat at $949.9m, operating income rose 25.5% to $115.5m, orders rose 21% and backlog 36%, with book-to-bill at 1.09. "Our quarterly backlog coverage is 1.3, which hasn't been -- I'm looking at last quarter, it was 1.1. Last year, it was 1.0," chief financial officer Andrea Funk told investors on 12 November. Energy Systems, the data-center and communications arm, grew 3% to $449m with data-center bookings up mid-teens and that backlog more than doubling; motive power fell 8% to $397m on weak material handling. Third-quarter adjusted earnings were guided to $3.05 to $3.15 a share against a $3.09 consensus.

The shares fell 33.8% from $192.96 on 25 August to $127.77 on 25 November while trailing four-quarter diluted earnings rose from $9.34 to $9.96 a share, which by derivation compresses trailing earnings from roughly 20.7x to 12.8x; the forward figure is 9.56x against an FY27 consensus of $13.37. Most of that happened before the results: EnerSys fell 8.5% on 4 November and a further 7.2% on 5 November, moving with the artificial-intelligence data-center complex after the Democratic congressional sweep while renewables rallied. The company bought back 679,000 shares for $130m during the quarter, at about $191 each.

The caveats are its own. The batteries inside Nvidia's 800-volt racks come from contract manufacturers Flex and Delta. Its lithium data-center cabinet is early: "we have over 80 opportunities in sales," chief executive Shawn O'Connell said on the call, "representing over 1,000 battery cabinets to date." Six have shipped.

Who is paying for Eos Energy's second line

Eos Energy, which builds zinc-based long-duration systems for utilities and renewable developers, is paid on project milestones and funded the same way. Third-quarter revenue was a record $82.7m, up 171%, with 2026 guidance of $300m to $325m reaffirmed. Gross profit was still minus $44.2m, a negative 53.4% margin, improving for an eighth straight quarter. Its Energy Department facility provides up to $277.5m across tranches, each conditional; $87m landed on 14 September for the second Thorn Hill line.

"The winners were never the companies with the best technology on paper," chief executive Joseph Mastrangelo said on 5 November. "They were the ones whose equipment showed up on schedule." Backlog slipped to about $765m from $807m on timing; 83% of the quarter's volume went to related party Frontier Power USA; more than half the 3.5 GWh late-stage pipeline still awaits project financing. At 1.75x trailing sales, against 5.15x in August, and 0.61x backlog, there is no earnings anchor to compress, because there are no earnings.

What Enovix actually disclosed

Enovix is the one name whose markdown came with a disclosed failure. Its AI-1 silicon-anode cells passed internal qualification on a pre-AI-2 process that did not meet the target of more than 1,000 watt-hours per liter, so the smartphone program was deferred, later density gains were reallocated to smart eyewear, 20% of headcount was cut, and the Malaysian fab was left severely underutilized. The shares fell 22.1% on 13 November and 57.3% over thirty days, the worst on the shelf, and at 6.24x trailing sales it remains the dearest of the three.

It is not funding the fab with new paper. It retired $128m of its 2030 convertible notes for about $81.4m in October and November, leaving $232m outstanding against $415m of cash and a $243m market value, which implies roughly $60m of value on the operating business before the undisclosed 2028 balance. The refinancing runs through a warrant dividend distributed 18 November, one warrant per four shares at a $3.00 exercise price, payable in cash or by surrendering 2028 notes. That strike sits 2.6 times above the $1.145 close on 25 November. The growth case is a trade rule: "there's no shortage of demand for TAA-compliant cells, non-China cells," chief revenue officer Steven Bakos said on 12 November, with a 100,000-unit drone order shipping in January 2027.

Three mechanisms wearing one label

The shelf did not move as one trade. One of the six members gained 16.8% over the thirty days in which the average was marked down, and the bounce from the 20 November low ranged from 10.5% to minus 2.1% across the group. For EnerSys the three-month decline is entirely a de-rating, with an asterisk on earnings quality: a credit supplying nearly two-fifths of operating profit, no longer separately guided, with its integration test tightening next year. For Eos the equity is a wager on reaching positive product margin before the government loan and the state procurement calendar run out; the first New York bulk-storage round selected no Eos projects. For Enovix the markdown is explained, because the company explained it.

What the three share is a dependence on rules rather than on demand, which cuts both ways: the same content thresholds that force a Pennsylvania maker to relocate its Mexican lines also make a non-Chinese cell scarce enough to sell. The question the filings leave open is who funds the half of Eos's late-stage pipeline that no one has financed yet.
