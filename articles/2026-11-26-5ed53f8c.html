Amkor and ASE Earn More Per Packaged Chip as the Market Prices Their Capital Spending

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Packaging revenue at Amkor stood still last quarter and gross profit rose more than a quarter, because the company collected more money on each package it shipped. The advanced-products line, the part supposedly riding the artificial-intelligence build, actually shrank 3.1% to $1.632bn.

ASE's assembly, test and materials segment ran a 30.6% gross margin, 62% of group revenue but 91% of group operating profit, with utilization at or near 100%. Its October monthly print had that segment up 7.9% sequentially while the whole group managed 0.6% — the electronics-manufacturing arm went backwards.

Per-package economics therefore do not explain the markdown. What does is cash: both are spending more than they generate, Amkor at a negative 2.0% trailing free-cash-flow yield on roughly $2.5bn of 2026 capital spending, ASE at negative 4.2% with net debt at 66% of equity. Amkor now trades at 12.6x trailing earnings against 44.4x in May.

Amkor Technology, which packages and tests other companies' finished chips under contract from a base in Tempe, Arizona, sold fewer advanced-packaging dollars in the September quarter than a year earlier and made considerably more money doing it. Advanced-product net sales came in at $1,631.6m, down 3.1% from $1,683.5m, while group revenue was flat at $1.991bn and gross profit rose 27.8%. The gross profit extracted per dollar of revenue went from 14.3 cents to 18.3 cents.

That is the opposite of what the shares have been saying. Amkor and ASE Technology Holding, the Kaohsiung group that is the largest listed packager outside the foundries, have been marked down since late October on a reading that advanced packaging has peaked and that outsourced assembly earns a thin, cyclical fee per unit. The filings and the October calls say per-package economics reached records in the same quarter, and that what deteriorated is the market's willingness to fund plants that bill nothing until 2027 and beyond.

What one package now pays

Amkor's gain came from price and mix per package, not from more advanced units: with advanced products down, the mainstream lead-frame and wirebond remainder implied by the filing grew about 18%, to roughly $359m. Computing revenue rose 25% sequentially to a record on a high-density fan-out central-processor program and a new hyperscaler program, with two further 2.5-dimensional networking and datacenter programs coming online in the December quarter. Communications fell 8% sequentially, which management attributed to memory constraints and component shortages hitting both Android and iOS handsets, plus the migration of system-in-package work to Vietnam. Automotive and industrial set a record, up 37% year over year.

"I think the pricing environment in general is still attractive related to being able to pass costs along and think about long-term investment and how we're managing that margin profile to make sure the return is there," chief executive Kevin Engel told investors on October 26. Amkor guided December-quarter sales to $1.9-2.0bn and gross margin to 18.0-19.0%, holding the gain rather than giving it back, and disclosed top-ten customer concentration easing to 66% of sales from 73%.

ASE's assembly, test and materials segment turned over TWD 151.5bn in the quarter, up 51%, on a 30.6% gross margin, eight points better than a year earlier. The electronics-manufacturing arm grew volumes 35% but ran an 8.3% gross margin, down from 8.9%, squeezed by component cost pass-throughs. The October monthly print separates the two cleanly: packaging and test revenue up 7.9% sequentially to NT$56.84bn, consolidated revenue up 0.6%, implying the remainder fell about a tenth month on month. "For 2027, we're expecting to remain as we last projected to double our LEAP revenue for the year," chief financial officer Joseph Tung said on October 29, referring to the leading-edge packaging line now guided above $4bn for 2026. Utilization is at or near 100%, with physical space named as the binding constraint.

The foundry-insourcing worry also runs backwards. TSMC has been expanding its outsourcing of the chip-on-wafer step to packagers, with the 2027 shortfall still estimated above 30% of demand. Substrate inflation is real — Ajinomoto raised build-up film prices about 30% from the third quarter — but it surfaced in ASE's low-margin manufacturing arm, not its packaging fee.

The bill, not the fee

Amkor fell 14.3% in the month to November 25 and ASE 27.3%, in a window when Nvidia fell 30.2%, Micron 30.3% and Teradyne 31.0% against 6.6% for the S&P 500 tracker. Two sessions carried most of it, including November 20, when the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% and erased its year.

Earnings explain none of the de-rating and cash explains most of it. Amkor trades at 12.6x trailing earnings and 12.5x forward, against the 44.4x trailing recorded in May, with 5.9x trailing earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization and consensus still looking for 13.3% revenue growth in 2027. Its trailing free-cash-flow yield is negative 2.0% on roughly $2.5bn of 2026 capital spending, with an Arizona framework of $1.8bn for 2027; roughly $3bn of customer advance payments is expected to cover that without new debt. ASE compressed from 52.6x trailing earnings to 27.8x while raising 2026 capital spending to $11.5bn from $10.5bn, at negative 4.2% free-cash-flow yield and 66% net debt-to-equity.

The split between them is contracted work. ASE's packaging subsidiary Siliconware has Nvidia funding a tripling of dedicated capacity by mid-2029; Amkor was brought in as a third 2.5-dimensional supplier at engineering-run stage, with no meaningful volume expected in 2027. That is why ASE has doubled over twelve months and Amkor sits 6.7% below where it traded a year ago, two-thirds beneath its high, even as both post record packaging margins.

Amkor's Arizona Phase 1 completes late 2027 and Phase 2 breaks ground at the same time, a combined $12bn commitment. Until those buildings hold tools that bill, the advance payments on the balance sheet are the only evidence that the capacity will be wanted.
