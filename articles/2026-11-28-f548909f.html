Tecnoglass Set a Revenue Record and a Rising Peso Took Half Its Operating Income

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Tecnoglass bills Florida construction in dollars and pays its Barranquilla cost base in pesos, and that mismatch is what separated a record quarter from its earnings. Gross margin fell to 34.1% from 42.7% a year earlier, and management put roughly two-thirds of the decline on the currency.

The shares then rose 57.8% in the month to 27 November, the last leg of it around S&P's decision to make Tecnoglass the first Colombian company in its SmallCap 600 index, leaving them at 25.4x trailing earnings against about 14.6x a year ago.

The peso reversed hard in November, and the Colombian shelf split along currency lines: Ecopetrol's earnings grew as its shares fell with oil, Grupo Cibest's lending margin keeps rising with a policy rate held at 12.25%, and Grupo Aval's securities book is being marked against it.

Tecnoglass fabricates architectural glass and aluminium window, door and curtain-wall systems in Barranquilla, including the hurricane-resistant windows that go into Florida construction, and it sends its invoices in dollars while its factory costs accrue in Colombian pesos. In the third quarter it set a revenue record and a backlog record. Operating income fell by half.

The four Colombian large caps listed in New York have been read all year as one trade on a cheap Andean market repairing. They are not one trade. In the thirty days to 27 November Tecnoglass rose 57.8% while Ecopetrol, the state-controlled integrated oil producer, fell 23.8%, Grupo Aval 22.7% and Grupo Cibest, the holding company for Bancolombia, 10.5%. What sorts them is which currency each is paid in, set against a central bank that is not yet cutting.

A dollar invoice and a peso payroll

The official peso rate went from about 3,757 per dollar at the start of 2026 to 3,062.96 on 21 August, an appreciation of roughly 23% on the year driven by carry flows into high local rates and firm export receipts. Tecnoglass's gross margin fell to 34.1% from 42.7%, and management attributed about 580 basis points of that to a 20% year-on-year peso appreciation, 250 of it a non-cash inventory revaluation after the currency moved 12% inside ninety days. Section 232 duties cost $15.5m in the quarter, with aluminium up 52%; the US tariff on aluminium products reached 50% in June 2025 and, from 6 April 2026, is assessed on the full customs value of the imported article rather than metal content alone.

Demand was not the problem. Revenue rose 19.2% to $310.6m, multifamily and commercial work hit a record $192.4m, up 31%, and backlog reached a record $1.43bn with a book-to-bill of 1.2 times, a twenty-fourth consecutive quarter above 1.0. "Well, Florida is not doing well. It's doing extremely well. The demand is unbelievable," chief executive Jose Manuel Daes told investors on 5 November. "On the commercial side, I believe that we have work for the next five years for sure." Single-family revenue grew only 4% as reported, with US single-family starts running about 5% below the prior-year pace, and Florida slipped to roughly 75% of backlog from over 85% a year earlier.

Five sessions and an index committee

The company raised full-year revenue guidance to $1.10bn-$1.14bn and guided fourth-quarter gross margin back to 38-39% on pricing, cheaper aluminium and automation. "With a full year of pricing and incremental automation savings, we remain committed to fully offsetting the tariff impact in 2027," chief financial officer Santiago Giraldo said on the same call.

None of that has been reported yet, and the price has moved as though it had. At 25.4x trailing and 24.0x forward earnings, the shares stand against roughly 14.6x on the $3.42 of diluted earnings a year ago, while consensus 2026 earnings per share have fallen 35% to $2.47 and trailing free cash flow yield is negative 2.8%. The last stretch came in five sessions, from 47.72 on 20 November to 59.35 on 27 November, bracketing S&P's 23 November announcement that Tecnoglass replaces MarineMax in the SmallCap 600 before the 1 December open, making it the first Colombian company in the index.

The currency turned, and oil took Ecopetrol with it

In November the peso fell 12% to 3,630.89 and ended the month as the weakest currency in the world on fiscal worries, local dollar shortages and falling oil. Ecopetrol's quarter was the strongest of the four: net income rose 86% to COP 4.76tn, gross margin widened to 50.9% from 33.4%, the refining margin reached a historic $45.6 a barrel and production was 747,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day at a lifting cost of $11.4. The shares fell 24.7% from their 16 October peak as Brent, which averaged about $124 in October, retreated toward the high-$80s.

That leaves 6.57x trailing earnings, 3.97x EV/EBITDA and a 14.0% trailing free cash flow yield at 1.10x book. Consensus has 2027 earnings per share down 19%, so part of the de-rating is a forecast rather than a dislocation. The state owns 88.49% of the company, and Ecopetrol's own filings flag that the government tends to maximise dividend payouts alongside its fiscal challenges. "Market confidence is not built through announcements," new chief executive Joaquin Gutierrez said on 13 November. "It's built through consistent execution, legal certainty, and measurable results."

The two banks are on opposite sides of the same curve

Banco de la República held its policy rate at 12.25% in November, with four of seven board members voting to hold and three for another quarter-point increase; Grupo Aval expects a final 25-basis-point hike in December and cuts only from the second half of 2027. Grupo Cibest guides its 2027 net interest margin up to 7.5-7.7% on higher rates, from a record 7.4% this quarter on a COP 274tn loan book up 9.7%. Its cost of risk rises with it, to a guided 1.9-2.1% from 1.8%. "Most of the increasing cost of risk that we are expecting for 2027 comes from growth," chief strategy and financial officer Mauricio Botero Wolff said on 10 November, in commercial and consumer brackets "in which we were not participating." Third-quarter net income still fell 5.0%, at a 22% return on equity the bank itself calls rate-inflated against a 20% long-run target, and the shares carry 1.80x book against roughly 1.3x a year ago.

Aval shows the same rates from the other side. Its loan margin rose 44 basis points to 4.9%, while consolidated margin fell 191 basis points to 3.6% because the margin on its investment portfolio turned negative, at -0.1% from +8.7% a quarter earlier. Return on average equity was 7.9%, and it trades at 0.80x book.

What the quarters actually account for

Ecopetrol is the only one of the four whose twelve-month advance its earnings earn outright, and its November fall belongs to crude and to a cut in next year's estimates. The banks divide on where each one's margin sits: Cibest's inside a loan book repricing upward, Aval's inside a securities portfolio the curve has moved against. Tecnoglass is the reverse of both. Its order book and its prices are improving and its reported earnings are not, which makes the month's 57.8% a wager on a guided margin recovery plus a fixed date when index funds must own it.

Tecnoglass has told investors it is weighing a change of functional currency to the US dollar, which would end the non-cash revaluations that cost it 250 basis points of margin in the quarter. It would not move the payroll out of Barranquilla.
