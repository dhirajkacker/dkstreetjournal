Inspire Medical Must Pull Its Sleep Apnea Implant From Four European Markets

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A default judgment in Europe's Unified Patent Court has done to Inspire Medical's European business roughly what the loss of a US billing code did to its domestic one, and the company is absorbing both at once. Nyxoah, whose rival leadless nerve stimulator held about 8% of the obstructive sleep apnea device market in 2024, obtained the decision on 18 November and began enforcing it on 23 November: no sales in Germany, France, the Netherlands or Belgium, plus recall of product placed on those markets since March 2021.

The damage already in the numbers is domestic. Third-quarter revenue fell 7.9% to $206.7m and full-year guidance of $850m-$860m sits below the $912m delivered in 2025. At 41.7x forward earnings, the shares are priced for the return to growth chief financial officer Matt Osberg described as low single digits in 2027 - a recovery that now has to clear a court order as well as a reimbursement hole.

A patent court in Munich has stopped Inspire Medical Systems from selling its implanted sleep-apnea stimulator in four of its European markets, and the order reaches backwards. On 18 November the Court of First Instance of the Unified Patent Court issued a default decision in favor of Nyxoah on a European patent covering hypoglossal-nerve stimulation. On 23 November Nyxoah began enforcing it: Inspire is barred from offering, selling, importing or storing infringing product in Germany, France, the Netherlands and Belgium, and must recall, withdraw and destroy product placed on those markets since 31 March 2021, disclose the sales and pay damages.

Inspire sells a one-time implant per patient for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea, with no per-case consumable behind it. That structure was read through the autumn as a story about American billing codes and nothing else: providers lost the code they had used for Inspire placements, the company sized the hole at about $120m for 2026, and the American Medical Association's CPT Editorial Panel approved a dedicated Category I code effective 1 January 2028. The court order is a separate wound, in a region that was already the weak one.

The geography was soft before the injunction

International revenue fell 9% in the third quarter on destocking in Germany - one of the four countries now covered by the enforcement. Group revenue of $206.7m was down 7.9% year on year, and the operating margin swung to -13.9% from +4.3% a year earlier. Gross margin held at 86%, up 20 basis points, which is the point about a one-shot device: the margin per sale is excellent and there is no recurring annuity to cushion a volume shock. Full-year guidance of $850m-$860m is a guided decline against $912m of 2025 revenue.

"We are increasingly confident in our ability to return to growth and deliver low single-digit year-over-year revenue growth for the full year 2027," Osberg told investors on 2 November - before the judgment was published.

What the shares are actually measured against

Inspire has closed lower in every weekly step since 3 November, down 23.7% over 30 days to $54.18 and 12.9% over 90 days. The twelve-month drawdown figure depends on where the clock starts: the stock rose 28.6% in a single afternoon on 24 November 2025 when the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services finalized sharply higher payment rates for its procedures. Measured from the close after that spike, the fall is 58%; measured from three sessions earlier, about 40%. The lever that produced the spike is the one that broke.

The trailing earnings multiple is not usable - 2025 net income of $145m against $51m of operating income. On forward earnings the shares sit at 41.7x consensus of $1.30, and forward price-to-sales of 1.84x is above the trailing 1.79x, which is what paying up for a smaller company next year looks like. Against that, $453m of cash and no debt buy time.

How much time depends on who holds the socket. Inspire had roughly 65% of implantable-device share and 40% of the overall sleep-apnea device market in 2024, against about 8% for Nyxoah's leadless Genio and 12% for LivaNova's Aura. A plaintiff with 8% share and an enforceable European injunction is a different competitor than a plaintiff with 8% share alone.

Inspire has begun calling out battery-replacement revenue as a growth vector, generated by implants placed roughly a decade ago. Genio's pitch to surgeons is that there is no second surgery to perform.
