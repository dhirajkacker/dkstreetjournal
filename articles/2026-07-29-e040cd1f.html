Online Travel's "Turn": Real for One Name, Unconfirmed for the Rest

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Sonnet 5 · Writing Sonnet 5 · Prompt v1.0

The Online Travel Agencies bucket's 8.5% monthly bounce hides a wide split: Expedia's uptrend has held three weeks, MakeMyTrip and Booking flipped bullish only in the last one to seven sessions, and Trip.com — the biggest 30-day gainer — has stayed in a strong-bear trend band continuously since early February. Guidance out of Expedia and Trip.com points to decelerating, not accelerating, growth, while Booking and MakeMyTrip's next data points don't land until August 3-4.

A bucket, not a cohort

Treating BKNG, EXPE, MMYT and TCOM as one story obscures how differently each name has actually behaved. Expedia's trend band flipped to mildly bullish on July 8 and has held for roughly three weeks — the most durable signal in the group. MakeMyTrip only turned on July 21 (about a week old), and Booking's flip is a single session, dated July 28. Trip.com, meanwhile, has sat in a strongly bearish band continuously since February 6 — nearly six months — despite posting the largest 30-day price gain of the four (+13.4%, from 39.96 to 45.31). That divergence is the core finding: the name doing the most bouncing is the one whose trend signal never actually turned.

Price action over the past month tells a similar story of dispersion rather than a unified re-rating: TCOM +13.4%, EXPE +11.5% (265.28 to 295.79), BKNG +9.3% (182.41 to 199.31), and MMYT essentially flat at -0.3%. Over the trailing year, the average across the four (BKNG -10.9%, EXPE +61.8%, MMYT -45.8%, TCOM -28.2%) works out to roughly -5.8% — consistent with the bucket's -5.4% "still bearish" label, meaning the label isn't stale so much as it's averaging one outsized winner against two deep laggards.

Fundamentals are mixed, not confirmatory

Expedia's Q1 gross bookings grew 13% year-over-year, but Q2 guidance calls for a slower 7-9% bookings growth alongside reiterated 100-125bps margin expansion — deceleration with margin discipline, not a demand reacceleration. Trip.com's own guidance is more stark: Q2 net revenue growth of just 3-8%, down sharply from 17% in Q1, with management citing softening China outbound air travel and an ongoing antitrust probe — directly undercutting a turn narrative for the stock that bounced hardest. Booking doesn't report Q2 until August 4; analysts expect EPS of $2.46 and revenue of $7.19B, with FY26 guidance for high-single to low-double-digit bookings growth — steady, not accelerating — though sell-side price targets still imply roughly 22% upside. MakeMyTrip's fiscal Q1 results land August 3, meaning its band flip currently has zero corroborating fundamental data.

The more credible tailwind for the whole group is a fear-easing catalyst rather than a growth story: OpenAI scaled back in-ChatGPT checkout ambitions in March, and Google's AI Mode travel feature routes bookings to OTA partners rather than bypassing them, though that same source flags AI-mediated bookings could carry lower commissions than Booking's current 15-20% take rate. Sector-wide, US travel spending growth is projected at a modest 2.2% for 2026, a normalization backdrop rather than a demand supercycle. Trailing valuations (BKNG 23.9x P/E, EXPE 23.2x, MMYT 148x off a thin earnings base) show no obvious re-rating discount that would signal remaining runway is unusually large.
