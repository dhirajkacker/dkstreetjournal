What the bands actually show

Across the eight tracked names — BWMX, CPNG, EYE, GLBE, HZO, LQDT, MNSO, RERE — the trend-band history does not support a coherent 30-90 day rotation into bear territory. Only EYE (mildly bullish to mildly bearish on April 2, strongly bearish by April 16) and RERE (mildly bullish to mildly bearish April 10, strongly bearish by May 11) show a genuine bull-to-bear flip inside that window. GLBE's flip happened back in February, roughly 90-plus days before its most recent data point, and its price actually round-tripped — rallying from $28.64 to $33.30 in early-to-mid April before drifting back to $28.63 — even as its band stayed pinned in strongly bearish, a sign the moving-average band lags real price action. MNSO and CPNG, meanwhile, were already sitting in bear bands as far back as early February and simply stayed there — a persistent multi-month regime, not a fresh transition. And BWMX, HZO and LQDT remain in mild or strong bull bands, up 37% to 67% year over year, directly at odds with a cohort-wide bearish framing.

The catalysts don't line up as a shared shock

Real cross-border tariff catalysts do exist. The US de minimis exemption was eliminated for China and Hong Kong in May 2025 and fully ended for all countries by February 2026, while the EU introduced a flat €3 parcel customs fee effective July 1, 2026. But these dates mostly predate or postdate the actual band-transition dates seen in the data, undercutting the idea of one shared 30-90 day trigger. GLBE's roughly 30% earlier-2026 drawdown tied to tariff uncertainty had already recovered about 11% over the trailing month by late July. CPNG's decline looks name-specific: a Q1 loss, data-breach fallout, and Korean Fair Trade Commission scrutiny drove the drop, and sell-side consensus there remains a Buy with a price target implying over 40% upside — not a market treating this as a completed value re-rating. EYE's ~19% single-day gap came on a print that reiterated full-year guidance rather than cutting it, suggesting analyst skepticism more than a fundamental break. MNSO's own quarter delivered 28.5% revenue growth that beat guidance, even as its stock sits in a strongly bearish band.

Data caveat

Seven of the eight tickers' local price history is stale as of mid-May, roughly ten weeks behind today; only CPNG has data through the current date, where it ground from a $19.15 early-July peak down to $15.44. That grind is real but can't be generalized to the rest of the basket without fresher data.

What the bands actually show

Trend-band history for the two names in this bucket tells a cleaner story than the current "turning bullish" tag suggests. VOYG flipped out of strongly bearish/mildly bearish into mildly bullish on 2026-04-24 and ran to strongly bullish by mid-May, holding that band through 2026-07-06 before sliding to mildly bullish and then mildly bearish since 2026-07-20. Price action backs this up: VOYG fell from $34.57 (2026-06-22) to $25.65 (2026-07-28), a ~26% decline, while SPCE slid from $2.95 to $2.49 over a similar window, about 15.6% — both consistent with the roughly -15% 30-day category average cited in the hypothesis, though VOYG's leg down is the sharper of the two.

The catalysts have turned into headwinds

The binary catalyst underpinning Starlab/VOYG — NASA's Commercial LEO Destinations Phase 2 award — was placed on hold on January 28, 2026 as NASA restructured its acquisition approach, and a March 2026 strategy shift now points toward private modules feeding a government-anchored hub rather than fully independent stations, with awards still pending sometime in 2026. Competing bidders are moving on their own clocks: Vast's Haven-1 has completed cleanroom integration for a 2027 launch and Axiom targets a two-module station by 2028, while Starlab's own target launch of 2029 trails both. Layered on top, Morgan Stanley downgraded VOYG to Underweight on July 15, assigning only about a 33% probability to the Starlab joint venture's DCF-implied value — a downgrade that drove a 7.3% single-day drop that lines up with the band's rollover.

SPCE's Delta-class program is progressing on schedule — structural assembly is complete and glide tests are targeted for Q3 2026 with rocket-powered flight in Q4 — but the company is funding that progress through dilution, having completed a $30.5M debt-for-equity swap and filed a $40.21M shelf while guiding to negative $87-92M quarterly free cash flow. Curated notes flag Virgin Galactic's runway as roughly three to four quarters against ~$338M cash and burn near $90-95M/quarter — a gap the recent financing only partially closes.

Not one space trade, but two divergent ones

The adjacent Direct-to-Device & Spectrum leg underscores that "space" isn't a single cohort right now. ASTS collapsed from strongly bullish to strongly bearish within days at the end of June, falling roughly 22.7% from $73.19 to $56.55 after a botched New Glenn satellite insertion and heavy insider selling. GSAT, by contrast, has held mildly bullish/strongly bullish nearly continuously and was essentially flat (-1.0%) over the same window — but that stability reflects Amazon's pending $90-cash/0.3210-share acquisition spread compressing, not sector fundamentals reasserting themselves.

Taken together, the Space Stations & Tourism "turning bullish" tag reads as a lagging snapshot of a completed round-trip rather than a live inflection, and the fundamental catalysts that drove the spring rally — CLD Phase 2 timing and Delta-class flight progress — currently sit closer to headwind than tailwind for both names.

A real producer-side transition, not a one-week wobble

Trend-band data shows the pure-play gas producer cohort actually rolled over months ago, not last week. CRK flipped to strongly bearish on April 14, EQT moved from bull to mildly bearish on May 26 and strongly bearish by June 9, RRC went mildly bearish on June 17 and strongly bearish by July 14, AR completed a clean glide from strongly bullish to strongly bearish between late April and July 1, and TOU.TO turned mildly bearish on June 24. Henry Hub futures (NG=F) themselves have oscillated in mild/strong bear bands since March, deepening back to strongly bearish on July 21 and closing at $2.679 on July 28, down roughly 8% over five sessions.

That producer downtrend has real fundamental legs. US working gas storage sat 183-185 Bcf above the five-year average through mid-July, a persistent (if not obviously widening) surplus per EIA storage data, and the EIA's outlook points to record production, led by Permian growth, capping prices. Separately, EIA cut its 2027 Henry Hub forecast 11% specifically on associated-gas supply from the Permian. An internal analysis note from early May had already flagged CRK's bear-band flip alongside a strip trading below many Haynesville breakevens, predating the current sell-off by roughly three months. EQT's own unlevered breakeven near $2.00/MMBtu, per independent research, suggests it isn't yet in curtailment territory even at current prices.

Midstream and LNG haven't confirmed the same regime

WMB and KMI cycled between mildly bullish and strongly bullish all quarter, and LNG only confirmed strongly bullish on July 14 — meaning its current pullback started from a freshly bullish trend, not a deteriorating one. Recent weakness in WMB (-6.8%) and LNG (-7.3%) lines up with a broader July 27 energy-complex risk-off day as gas hit an 11-week low alongside falling oil, plus scheduled Freeport LNG maintenance cutting feedgas. Neither looks like a fundamental turn: the 2027 winter Henry Hub strip still points above $4, US LNG exports are forecast to grow nearly 30% by 2027, and JPMorgan raised its LNG price target into the pullback per the same Cheniere coverage. Canadian producer ARX.TO has stayed in strongly bullish nearly the entire period, undercutting any claim of a uniform cohort-wide bear turn.

What's happening

The concentration problem

The rally is also less gradual than the headline number implies. A single session on July 14 saw EAF jump 16.1%, from $5.70 to $6.62 — alone accounting for roughly half the month's dollar gain. The only identifiable news that day was a ratings tweak from Weiss Ratings, which moved EAF from 'sell (e+)' to 'sell (d-)' — still a sell rating, not an obvious catalyst for a double-digit move. The second leg came after GrafTech's July 23 earnings report, which did carry real substance: Q2 sales volume rose 8% year-over-year with capacity utilization hitting 74%, the highest since 2022, and new customer commitments were pricing more than 15% above pre-announcement levels. Notably, one data source flagged the quarter as missing EPS estimates by a penny rather than beating them, underscoring how noisy the read on this print already is.

Cohort confirmation is partial at best

Even among peers whose prices rose, trend bands tell a softer story: Nucor, Steel Dynamics, and Ternium all eased from strongly bullish to mildly bullish in mid-July, and Cleveland-Cliffs' band actually flipped to mildly bearish on July 7 despite its price climbing. Balance sheet and valuation

GrafTech's Q2 press release shows $1,225M gross debt against $253M liquidity, with no maturities until December 2029, though the company drew the last $100M of its delayed-draw term loan in June, just before that facility expired. Analyst price targets average $16.67 against a $7.68 price — over 2x upside on paper — but the consensus rating is only 'Hold,' and targets have moved in both directions this year.

The balance of evidence points to a distressed bounce riding real but not-yet-confirmed sector tailwinds, not a validated band exit.

Downside room varies sharply by name

This is a dateable flow event. The macro legs don't hold

There is no easing tailwind. The 10-year yield topped 4.7% on 23 July, its highest since January 2025, and bond investors are demanding wider spreads from Alphabet, Amazon and Meta over AI capex. What's left

Runway exists — ACN sits 23% below its 200-day and 43% below its 52-week high; DOMO is -79.7% from its high. Prior desk analysis on this group conditioned everything on "wait for the trend flip." The bands say that flip has not happened.
