dLocal's Take Rate Stopped Falling and Its Margins Widened; General Atlantic Kept Selling

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

An emerging-market payments processor posted its best gross profit quarter on record this month, and its shares sit lower than before the print. dLocal held its net take rate at 0.72% of payment volume in the third quarter, flat sequentially after a year of compression, and gross margin widened to 33.2%.

The outlook trim was about currency in Brazil, Mexico and Argentina, worth roughly six percentage points of gross profit growth, with volume growth still steered toward the upper end of 60-70%. The November decline has an identifiable seller: General Atlantic sold 12.8m shares at an average $12.90.

Marqeta and Shift4 earn at different points in the same payment flow, and their twelve-month records disagree completely, so the shelf-wide story about alternative rails routing around card processors does not survive the comparison.

dLocal, the Montevideo-based processor that collects payments for streaming, ride-hailing, advertising and software merchants across emerging markets, reported on 12 November that the thing investors had been watching it lose finally stopped moving. Its net take rate, gross profit divided by payment volume, came in at 0.72% of volume, essentially unchanged from the prior quarter after a year of steady compression from 0.99%. Gross profit was a record $132.0m, up 27.9%, and gross margin widened sequentially to 33.2% from 31.8%.

The shares have fallen 16% over three months. The reading attached to that decline across the payments shelf has been disintermediation: stablecoin settlement and agent-driven checkout routing volume around the companies that process cards. dLocal's own disclosure says the opposite is happening to it. Crypto and stablecoins were described as a growing vertical with merchant wins in remittances and repatriation, and protocol-driven agentic commerce volume was characterized as still low. The company launched a product in April called Stablecoin Full, letting merchants accept and settle in stablecoins across more than 44 markets through one interface, which makes the rail a cheaper cost line and a sales argument.

The take rate held for the first time in a year

Volume was $18.3bn in the quarter, up 77% year over year and the eighth consecutive quarter above 50% growth. Revenue grew 40.9% to $398.0m, far slower, because 62% of that volume was local-to-local flows that settle inside one country and earn a thinner spread than cross-border collection. Payouts were another 25%. On the revenue line the implied take is about 2.18% of volume against roughly 2.72% a year earlier, a derived figure; the mix shift is most of it, with Brazil hit by a merchant shift toward the PIX instant-payment scheme and Boleto vouchers plus processing-cost catch-ups, and Mexico by slower volume at concentrated, lower-priced merchants.

Costs went the other way. Operating profit of $76.2m grew 37%, faster than gross profit, reaching 58% of gross profit as headcount fell 3% and operating expenses 11% sequentially. "The investment cycle is behind us, and now it's more about continued expansion of margins going forward even as we pursue growth," chief executive Pedro Arnt said on 12 November. Chief financial officer Guillermo Perez told investors the same day that the company expects "to grow gross profit above OpEx on an annual basis, but quarterly figures could be a bit more lumpy."

What trimmed the outlook was currency

dLocal reiterated volume growth toward the upper end of 60-70% and put full-year gross profit growth at around the midpoint of its 25-30% range, with operating profit at the lower end of 27.5-32.5%. The cause named was foreign exchange in Brazil, Mexico and Argentina, estimated at a six-percentage-point swing on year-on-year gross profit growth. Cash followed the margin: $99m of adjusted free cash flow in the quarter, a 146% conversion from net income, and $34m of stock repurchased.

The heaviest session was 17 November, when the shares fell 6.6% on roughly six times the prior week's average volume and crossed below their 200-day average. That day, General Atlantic's holding vehicle sold 12,813,179 Class A shares in open-market trades at an average $12.90, about $165m, its second large disposal in three months after 9.84m shares in August. The surrounding week was broadly risk-off, with the S&P 500 tracker down 8.6%.

Three businesses, three points in the flow

The companies grouped with dLocal do not earn money the same way, which is why their records diverge. Marqeta, an issuer-processor that runs card programs for platforms, keeps about 9.7 cents of gross profit per hundred dollars of processed volume, down from roughly 11.7 a year earlier; its gross profit growth has decelerated four quarters running to 6.6%, yet the stock is up 257% over twelve months and trades at 17.5x trailing earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, the dearest of the three on cash earnings. Shift4, the Allentown acquirer behind SkyTab point-of-sale terminals, reported $1,319m of gross revenue of which $671m was network fees it never retains, processed $61bn at a blended spread of 64 basis points that widened rather than compressed, and is down 41.5% over a year against 3.7x net leverage and a growth framework cut to mid-to-high single digits on restaurant and European weakness.

None of the three management teams quantified alternative rails as a threat. In the same November week the hardest-hit payment names were the stablecoin beneficiaries, Circle down 23.6% and Coinbase 20.1%, while Visa fell 5.6% and Mastercard 5.4%. On-chain AI-agent payment value has shrunk to about $1.59m a month from roughly $29m.

So the operating results account for none of dLocal's three-month decline and the guidance trim accounts for part of it: a six-point currency haircut on gross profit growth is real money, and management said compression has not bottomed even as the declines slow. The rest is a seller and a bad tape. At 15.5x forward earnings and 6.8x forward gross profit against 7.3x trailing, with a 14.0% trailing free-cash-flow yield, the market is paying for roughly 7% of gross profit growth from a business that just grew it 28% and holds 42 licences across 26 markets.

There is no scheduled disclosure from dLocal until its next annual results, estimated for late March. Whether General Atlantic has finished is not in the filings.

Originating hypothesis

category gradual derating take rate mechanics · subject: DLO, MQ, FOUR

The payment and fintech infrastructure shelf — the largest Technology segment on this universe snapshot with 18 members — is quietly rolling over at genuinely gradual intensity while still up on the year (-6.5% over 30 days against +16.2% over twelve months, a fraction of the markdowns taken by memory, EMS and enterprise software on the same snapshot), and the question is mechanical rather than directional: establish where in the payment flow each of these three businesses actually earns its money, since dLocal is a licensed cross-border collector that keeps a take rate on locally settled emerging-market volume and already moves part of it on stablecoin rails, Marqeta is an issuer-processor whose reported net revenue sits after network fees and bank-partner revenue share and is concentrated in a handful of platform customers, and Shift4 is a merchant acquirer whose gross revenue is padded with interchange it never keeps — so the same "stablecoins and agentic commerce will route around the card rails" de-rating is landing on a take-rate business, a per-transaction processor and a pass-through acquirer at once.
