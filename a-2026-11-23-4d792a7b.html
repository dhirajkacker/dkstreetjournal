Snap Is Leaning on 30m Paying Subscribers Because Its Advertising Grew Just 4%

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A distribution deal with an artificial-intelligence search company was supposed to be Snap's answer to the question hanging over every social platform. It ended quietly in the first quarter of 2026, and nothing replaced it — so what is left in the numbers is an advertising business growing 4% a year.

Snap's September quarter revenue rose 14.3% to $1.722bn, but advertising, $1.373bn of it, slowed from 9% growth in June, with brand budgets falling on consumer-packaged-goods weakness and North American daily users at 92m against 98m a year earlier. The growth came from Snapchat+ and Lens+ subscriptions, up 84%.

The market has filed Snap, Reddit and Meta under one heading. Only Reddit's deterioration is actually caused by AI answers; Snap's is brand advertising, and Meta sold 10% more ad impressions at 14% higher prices.

Snap, which sells advertising inside the Snapchat camera-messaging app, spent the past year being priced as a participant in an artificial-intelligence trade that never paid it. The $400m distribution agreement with Perplexity, announced in November 2025 and payable in cash and equity over a year as global rollout was achieved, was "amicably ended" in the first quarter of 2026, after the two companies had yet to agree a path to a broader rollout. Snap said its guidance assumed no contribution from it.

What is left is an advertising business that has stopped growing at the pace its cost base was built for, and a subscription business nobody was valuing. Shares have fallen 41.5% over twelve months. The quarter says that markdown is earned on the advertising line and owed nothing to AI.

The line that grew, and the line that didn't

September-quarter revenue rose 14.3% to $1.722bn. Advertising, $1.373bn of that, grew 4%, down from 9% in the June quarter, with brand advertising declining outright on consumer-packaged-goods weakness in the United States and the Middle East. Direct-response formats are now 77% of revenue, and inside that mix Snap is gaining: app-purchase revenue rose 54% while the cost per acquisition advertisers paid fell 24%.

The top-line growth came from somewhere else entirely. Other revenue — Snapchat+, Memories Storage and Lens+, together more than 30m paid subscribers — rose 84%.

"While advertising revenue growth remains below our ambitions, advertisers are seeing the benefits of our performance improvements," chief executive Evan Spiegel said in the prepared remarks accompanying the 4 November results. Chief commercial officer Ronan Harris told analysts the same day that brand product work was "kicking in as we move into 2027" and that the brand business should "regain ground over the course of 2027."

Users are the other half of the problem. Daily actives reached 495m, short of the 499m analysts expected, and North America fell to 92m from 98m. Effective in the first quarter of 2027 Snap switches its headline metric to global monthly actives, 977m, with a monthly-user revenue figure as the primary monetization measure, announced in the quarter the daily number missed. Citigroup kept a Neutral rating and cut its target to $6.00 from $6.75 on 6 November.

Against that, the cash improved. Gross margin reached 62.3%, up seven points; net income was $56.5m against a $103.5m loss a year earlier; free cash flow for the trailing year was $777m, up 88%. Chief financial officer Doug Hott said on the call that "approximately 93% of incremental revenue" flowed through to adjusted EBITDA. Snap also trimmed its infrastructure cost guidance to a $1.65–1.66bn range from $1.65–1.70bn while raising AI investment. There is no usable earnings multiple here, since consensus earnings per share for 2026 is still fractionally negative, so the anchors are 1.95x forward price-to-gross-profit against 2.02x trailing, and a 9.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield at a $7.85bn market value. That yield is the only argument that the de-rating overshot.

Reddit is the one AI actually pays, on the smaller side of its own ledger

Reddit, the interest-community network, is where the mechanism is real. Its licensing money is two fixed-fee contracts, roughly $60m a year from Google and near $70m from OpenAI, sitting inside an other-revenue line of $48m in the September quarter, 5.4% of $895m. The traffic those answers displace is the larger side: US logged-out daily uniques fell to 27.3m from 30.1m in a quarter, and management guided total daily uniques down a low-double-digit percentage from 130.3m. Revenue still grew 53% and operating income 89%, and the shares now trade at 15.6x forward earnings against 23.6x at the pre-print close on 29 October.

Meta sold more advertising and was marked down for the bill

Meta disclosed no advertising deterioration at all: impressions up 10%, average price per ad up 14%, revenue up 26.1% to $64.60bn. Its markdown is about financing, not demand: capital spending guided to $135–145bn and third-quarter free cash flow of minus $2.1bn. At 19.3x forward earnings against 28.6x in May, it is nonetheless up 2% over three months, which is why treating these three as one trade fails on arithmetic before it fails on logic.

So the common story is thin. Reddit's problem is the search box; Snap's is the brand budget, and a shrinking North American base that no distribution deal was going to fix. A slice of November belongs to neither: all three fell together on 20 November as the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% and erased its 2026 gain, with Snap down 11.1% that session on no company news. Snap's shares had held an uptrend from late September until mid-November, so unlike Reddit's, this decline did not anticipate the print.

Guidance for the December quarter is $1.950–1.985bn of revenue and $490–525m of adjusted EBITDA. It will be the last quarter Snap reports on the daily-user metric that just missed.

Whether the brand advertisers Harris expects back in 2027 actually come back is what decides the rest. Until they do, the fastest-growing thing Snap owns is a subscription it sells to its own users, and the business the market valued it for is the one standing still.
