This Year's Bankruptcies Are Too Small to Pay FTI Consulting's Restructuring Bills

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Litigation and restructuring advisers are paid by the hour, and the hours are not following the filings. Commercial Chapter 11 cases ran 11% ahead of last year through September, yet FTI Consulting's Corporate Finance & Restructuring utilization fell to 58% from 63% and the Restructuring line inside that segment shrank 1% year on year.

FTI still crossed $1bn of quarterly revenue for the first time on 29 October, with adjusted EBITDA at 15.2% of revenue against 13.7%. The lift came from a record $40.7m of success fees, a lower tax rate, the end of the Compass Lexecon dislocation and a diluted share count down to 27.0m from 31.8m; chief executive Steve Gunby normalized the $3.84 of adjusted earnings per share to roughly $2.90 himself.

CRA International, the purer litigation-economics exposure, has published nothing since 6 August and reports on 5 November.

Commercial bankruptcy filings in the United States ran 11% ahead of last year through September, and FTI Consulting, the advisory firm built to staff the largest of those cases, got less work out of each consultant than it did a year earlier. Utilization in its Corporate Finance & Restructuring segment fell to 58% from 63%, and the Restructuring practice inside that segment saw revenue decline 1%.

The 29 October print was read as the distress cycle finally reaching the invoices. FTI did cross $1bn of quarterly revenue for the first time, at $1.013bn, with adjusted EBITDA margin of 15.2% against 13.7%. But the hours behind that revenue tell a different story about where advisory money is coming from, and it is not the bankruptcy docket.

Why a filing count stopped predicting a fee

The year's insolvency growth is small-company insolvency. Subchapter V small-business elections were up 46% year on year as of October, and a small-business reorganization generates a fraction of the expert hours a large case does. Meanwhile the headline series has turned: Epiq counted 6,560 commercial Chapter 11 filings through September, but September alone produced 648 against 769 a year earlier, a 16% decrease. The wave crested in the first half, when filings ran 28% ahead.

The antitrust channel has the same shape. Merger notifications reached 2,555 in fiscal 2026, the largest total since 2022, but second requests — the investigations that pay for testifying economists — numbered 41 in fiscal 2025, about 2.1% of adjusted reported deals, and in July the Justice Department's Antitrust Division published a model timing agreement meant to compress those reviews. More deals, narrower investigations, fewer hours each.

What actually paid at FTI

Corporate Finance & Restructuring revenue rose 4.8% to $424.4m on a 3.2% higher average bill rate of $550, with Transactions up 13% on success fees and Transformation up 7%. Success fees hit a record $40.7m, including a Federal Reserve Bank of Boston loan-portfolio sale. That is event income, not a recurring run rate of billed hours.

The genuine repair is at Compass Lexecon, the Economic Consulting arm, whose dislocation management says had been costing roughly $22m of EBITDA a quarter for five quarters. Revenue there rose 9.3% to $189.3m and adjusted segment EBITDA swung to $14.0m from minus $4.6m, at a $639 average rate, the firm's richest hour. "That dislocation is behind us, and the power of everything else we have been doing is once again beginning to shine through," Gunby told investors on 29 October.

Forensic & Litigation, by contrast, was flat at $195.1m on 54% utilization, record rates offsetting lost volume as US enforcement pulled back. FTI took a $46m charge for cuts touching about 4% of staff, with roughly $80m of annualized savings expected. "We believe these actions further strengthen our business and position us for sustained growth," chief financial officer Angela Nam said the same day.

CRA re-rated before it reported

CRA International, the $1.23bn Boston economics and expert-testimony firm that competes with Compass Lexecon for antitrust mandates, rose 19.5% over October with nothing published since 6 August; it reports on 5 November. Its largest day, 22 October, came as Accenture rose 7.4%, Huron 6.5% and Exponent 5.2% on a session when the Nasdaq fell 2.6% — the likelier reading is rotation into de-rated labor businesses. Its last numbers were good: revenue up 12.8% to $210.8m at 77% utilization, with guidance raised to $805m–$820m on an unchanged 12.0%–13.0% margin target, and chief executive Paul Maleh describing "peak levels of new project opportunities" on 6 August.

Still, consensus has CRA earning $8.47 for the year ending January 2027, up 2.8%, against a forward multiple that went from 19.4x to 23.2x on no news. Its trailing free cash flow yield is minus 2.3% against FTI's 8.0%, and it is the dearer of the two on price to gross profit, 5.78x versus 3.89x.

FTI's results account for about an eighth of its 36% month: the forward multiple moved from 13.9x to 18.9x while the adjusted earnings guide rose 4.3% at the midpoint, to $9.50–$10.00. And the earnings that justified it are mostly not hours. Gunby normalized the quarter's $3.84 of adjusted earnings per share to roughly $2.90 after success fees and a tax rate near 22%, while buybacks took the diluted share count to 27.0m from 31.8m the prior quarter. The one durable gain is Compass Lexecon's return, which management frames as a five-year recovery to historical profitability.

What both firms are really defending is the named expert. FTI promoted 100 senior managing directors and hired more than 140 laterals over two years, which is why its rates can set records while its utilization falls. Paying up for experts works when the cases are big enough to keep them busy, and this year's cases are getting smaller.

GE Vernova's Turbine Order Boom Became a Price Story as the Gigawatts It Signed Fell 40%

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

GE Vernova's Power orders rose 178% organically last quarter to $21.8bn, and the generating capacity behind them moved the other way: 11 GW of new gas equipment contracts signed against roughly 19 GW three months earlier, by the arithmetic of the company's own disclosures.

Nothing in the shipped business got worse. Power segment margin reached 21.4% against 13.4% a year earlier, equipment pricing rose more than 20% in the quarter, and the two other makers of generating equipment for data centers reported their best quarters as well — Bloom Energy's product gross margin hit 38.4%, and Caterpillar's Power & Energy profit grew faster than its sales.

All three shares fell hard into 30 October, led by a 22 October repricing of artificial-intelligence capital spending. The single business fact consistent with that de-rating is narrow: the slot-reservation queue that feeds future firm orders shrank, while everything already booked became more profitable.

GE Vernova booked $21.8bn of Power orders in the third quarter, up 178% organically, and signed fewer gigawatts of gas turbines doing it than it had three months before. The company, which sells gas, nuclear, steam and hydro generating plant to utilities, independent power producers and increasingly data-center developers, took 11 GW of new gas equipment contracts in the quarter — 8 GW of slot reservation agreements plus 3 GW of firm orders — according to its 28 October call, against roughly 19 GW of gross intake in the second quarter on the same basis. That is a derived comparison, built from contracted capacity and shipments rather than a disclosed figure.

Turbine slots sold out to the end of the decade have been read as evidence that the order line measures demand. This quarter it measured price: equipment pricing was up more than 20% year on year, and full-year 2026 pricing is running 50% above 2025. Wood Mackenzie puts gas turbine prices 195% above 2019 levels. The shipped economics improved sharply, which the selloff ignored. The reservation queue drained, which it may not have.

What five gigawatts earned

GE Vernova shipped 5 GW of gas equipment in the quarter, 32 turbines including 13 aeroderivatives, a 60% increase in units. Power revenue of $6.1bn rose 25%, and segment EBITDA margin reached 21.4% from 13.4% a year earlier on pricing and volume. Divide those disclosures by shipments and the quarter carried about $1.22bn of Power revenue and roughly $260m of Power EBITDA per gigawatt out the door — a derived upper bound, since the segment also contains services and non-gas lines.

The annuity behind the hardware is the reason pricing holds. "We expect every gigawatt of HAs to generate $0.5 billion of high-margin services revenue over the first 20 years of its lifetime," chief executive Scott Strazik told investors on 28 October, with the installed or contracted fleet of those advanced-class units heading to at least 400 by the end of 2027. Full-year Power guidance went up for a third time, to 19–21% revenue growth and a 19–21% segment margin.

The deposits land years before the turbine

Equipment remaining performance obligations stood at $104.1bn on 30 September, with 33% expected in revenue within a year and 60% within two. Contract liabilities and deferred income rose $19.8bn over nine months. A roughly 20% cash deposit converts a reservation into a slot agreement, which is how a heavy-equipment maker guides to $16–17bn of free cash flow on $46.5–47.5bn of revenue, about 35% of sales.

The queue got shorter while the book got bigger

Against 8 GW of new reservations, GE Vernova converted 13 GW of existing ones into firm orders. The pool therefore shrank by about 5 GW, and total contracted capacity rose only 6 GW, to 122 GW from 116 GW, against double-digit quarterly gains earlier in the year. Strazik said he expects solid order and reservation growth in 2027, but not at the pace of the first half of 2026. Management said it had seen no financing disruptions and no slowdown in converting reservations to backlog. In the same release the company agreed to buy high-voltage equipment maker Trench Group for $9bn, half cash and half debt, closing late 2027 with about $1.9bn of 2028 revenue attached.

Caterpillar ships faster; Bloom stopped counting megawatts

The demand being announced is also being shipped. Caterpillar, whose Power & Energy arm sells reciprocating engines, generator sets and industrial gas turbines alongside construction machines, grew that segment's revenue 18% to $8.78bn with profit up 31% and margin of 24.4%. Company backlog hit a record $78bn, 96% higher, and 58% of it converts within twelve months — a far quicker clock than GE Vernova's equipment book. Caterpillar's shares fell on about $2.2bn of full-year tariff cost and margin guided near the bottom of its range, leaving 22.9x forward earnings against the roughly 38x on next-twelve-month earnings that David Giroux flagged in June.

Bloom Energy, which sells solid-oxide fuel cell servers converting gas or hydrogen to on-site power without combustion, lifted product revenue 179% to $1.07bn at a 38.4% product gross margin, up 250 basis points. It no longer discloses megawatts accepted each quarter, so margin per megawatt cannot be computed from what it publishes. At 64.1x forward earnings, down from about 119x in May, it remains the most demanding name of the three. Wärtsilä, whose Energy arm builds reciprocating-engine plants, lifted order intake 121% to €1,421m on a record €9,876m book and its shares fell too.

How much the results explain

Very little. Over the 30 days to 30 October, GE Vernova fell 9.7%, Caterpillar 23.2% and Bloom 26.7% while the S&P 500 tracker slipped 0.9%; the worst session was 22 October, when Meta's capital-spending commentary hit everything levered to data-center construction and the Nasdaq lost 2.6%. GE Vernova's 27.2x forward earnings is the honest anchor, since its 22.3x trailing is flattered by a $4.5bn one-off gain on Prolec GE; in May the shares traded at 32.1x trailing earnings. The de-rating is wider than any disclosed deterioration, and it is global.

One business fact does point the bears' way, and it is the one Matthew Smith's July thesis predicted: gigawatt intake slowing while dollars accelerate. Mitsubishi Power said in late October that demand is pushing deliveries out to 2031, so capacity remains the constraint. But a book of orders priced 50% above last year's, converting a third inside a year, is worth less if the queue behind it stops lengthening.

Pricing power this strong is usually the last thing to break. The reservation queue, where deposits are taken years before a turbine ships, is the first — and it got shorter this quarter while every other number got better.

Smithfield Foods, which raises hogs and sells Smithfield bacon, Nathan's Famous hot dogs and Eckrich deli meat, told investors on 27 October that its branded Packaged Meats segment should earn adjusted operating income of $1,075m–$1,150m this fiscal year. The company as a whole is guided to $900m–$1.0bn. The branded arm is expected to out-earn the business that contains it, because the farms and slaughter plants feeding it are losing money.

Four of the five big US meat and poultry names have been marked down between about 7% and 29% over the past year, Tyson Foods excepted, on a premise that cheap grain and cheap hogs would widen processing spreads. The quarters reported in June and September show something narrower. Inside a vertically integrated protein company the cheap raw material is a transfer, not a windfall, and it settles wherever the brand sits.

Smithfield's margin came out of its own barns

Packaged Meats earned about $230m in the September quarter, with margin widening to 11.6% from 10.8%, on sales down 5.2% to $2bn. Average selling price fell 5.3% and volume rose 0.2%: the segment made more money per pound because hog and pork cost fell faster than the price it charged. Upstream, Fresh Pork swung to a loss of $89m from a $10m profit as the processing spread fell 35% and volume dropped 6.3%, while Hog Production earned $34m against $89m. Group adjusted operating profit fell 55% to $145m.

The share gains downstream rest on distribution and brand. "Across our top 25 categories, industry volume declined 3.3%, while we grew volume by nearly 1%. We were the only company in our core competitive set to grow volume in this quarter, driving 0.4 points increase in volume share," Shane Smith, president and chief executive, told investors on 27 October. Smithfield gained share in all five of its largest categories, led by 2.3 points in smoked ham, and its Nathan's Famous purchase eliminates $33m of annual licence costs on rights previously held only through 2032. A new supply agreement with a joint-venture partner cuts internally produced hogs by roughly 900,000.

The shares closed 27 October at $18.33, a few cents above a 52-week low, at 1.03x book with a 12.8% trailing free-cash-flow yield and net debt at 0.5x EBITDA. The forward earnings multiple of 9.3x sits above 8.0x trailing, so consensus is still falling.

Pilgrim's had nothing downstream to catch it

Pilgrim's Pride, the Greeley, Colorado chicken and pork processor controlled by JBS, is the same geometry with the brand missing. US net revenues slipped to $2.65bn from $2.82bn in the June quarter while US adjusted EBITDA fell to $231.5m from $482.7m, a margin of 8.7% against 17.1%, which the company attributed primarily to a 27% drop in the jumbo cutout value. Consolidated operating margin has run 10.4%, 4.5%, 3.6% and 1.4% over the last four quarters.

Feed, the premise the widening-spread case rested on, moved against the processor. Corn ran roughly $4.25–$4.50 a bushel for the year but averaged $5.18 in the first week of August, and October soybean meal traded at $350–$360 a short ton in mid-September against a first-half range of $275–$325. Corn and soymeal are 60–70% of a broiler's diet.

The company is also adding pounds into the falling cutout: about 2.5% US supply growth in the second half. "What we want is to grow our branded business in the U.S. That will compensate the volatility in the big bird commodity market. Once again, we don't want to reduce our commodity operation," chief executive Fabio Sandri said on the 30 July call. Consensus has fiscal 2026 earnings at $2.27 a share against $5.26 for 2025; at $27.09 the forward multiple of 11.9x is wider than the roughly 8.4x the stock carried last October on $4.54 of delivered earnings.

Hormel divested the turkeys and kept the margin

Hormel Foods, the SPAM, Skippy and Jennie-O owner, is the counter-case. Gross margin moved 15 hundredths of a percentage point in the July quarter, against a 7.7-point loss at Pilgrim's; adjusted operating margin expanded to 9.0% and the company raised fiscal 2026 adjusted earnings guidance to $1.45–$1.51. Roughly half its segment sales decline came from divesting the whole-bird turkey business and exiting private-label snack nuts. "With our strong year-to-date performance and continued opportunities ahead, we are raising and narrowing our adjusted earnings outlook for fiscal 2026," interim chief executive Jeff Ettinger said on 27 August. The shares still fell 19.4% over three months, and at 13.3x forward earnings Hormel is marginally cheaper than Tyson's 14.1x — Tyson being the name whose beef unit is guided to a fiscal 2026 operating loss of $625m–$775m while its chicken segment is guided to $1.85bn–$1.95bn.

Arithmetic at Pilgrim's, an open question at Smithfield

Three companies, one cheap pound of meat, three outcomes decided by who owns the label on the package.

For Pilgrim's the market has done the arithmetic: a 28.5% decline over the year against consensus earnings more than halved, and a forward multiple that widened as the price fell. Smithfield is the awkward one, sitting at a 52-week low on the day it reaffirmed the segment guided to carry all of group profit; management tied the duration of the upstream loss to the fourth-quarter holiday season and spring 2027. Hormel's de-rating tests something else entirely: organic sales growth guided to 1–2%.

Pilgrim's Pride reports its third quarter after today's close, with consensus at 48 cents a share. Smithfield has shown where the money in a cheap pound of meat ends up; nobody can yet price when the cutout stops falling for the company that only sells pounds.
