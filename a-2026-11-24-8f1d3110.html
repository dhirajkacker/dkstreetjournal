Congress's Stopgap Keeps Paying AeroVironment and Kratos While Red Cat Waits for Funding

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The same markdown landed on three American drone makers this month, and only one of them had an order book that actually deteriorated. AeroVironment reported record funded backlog of $1.5bn, up 37% from a year earlier, on a 1.4 book-to-bill and reaffirmed fiscal 2027 revenue guidance of $2.125–2.225bn. Kratos booked $1.04bn in its third quarter for a 2.2:1 book-to-bill and a record $2.6bn backlog, $2.07bn of it funded.

Red Cat is the exception: it withdrew its 2026 revenue target on 16 November after missing the Army's Gauntlet II cut, with quarterly revenue of $11.77m against roughly $40.7m expected and gross margin of 0.6%.

The stopgap running the Pentagon pays programs already funded and bars new starts. That protects the two larger names and strands the one whose revenue sits in an unfunded pipeline.

The rule governing Pentagon money until 11 December pays for last year's programs and forbids starting new ones, and that clause of appropriations law separates three listed drone makers more cleanly than their business descriptions do. AeroVironment, which builds Switchblade loitering munitions and, since its BlueHalo purchase, space and directed-energy systems, is paid largely on reorders of things the Army already buys. So is Kratos, the target-drone, hypersonics and satellite-software contractor. Red Cat, which supplies reconnaissance and first-person-view drones to government buyers, needs orders that have not been funded yet.

All three were marked down as one trade. Over the thirty days to 23 November AeroVironment fell 26.4%, Kratos 26.3% and Red Cat 57.3%, while the primes sat still: General Dynamics was off 0.4%, L3Harris up 0.3%, Lockheed Martin down 5.4%. The reading embedded in that gap is that unmanned systems are a budget bet and the budget is stuck. Two of the three order books say the opposite.

The stopgap bars starts, not deliveries

The continuing resolution holds defense at the extended fiscal 2026 baseline of $838.5bn against a $1.5 trillion request, and bars any program not already funded and active in the prior year. Breaking Defense counted more than 150 fiscal 2027 initiatives blocked from starting, including $17.5bn of Golden Dome missile-defense work. The Senate Appropriations Committee finished releasing draft text for all twelve fiscal 2027 bills on 16 November, but floor action on the defense authorization is unlikely before December and the odds of a year-long Pentagon stopgap have risen. A rule like that is close to neutral for a contractor shipping against funded orders and punitive for one waiting on an award.

AeroVironment's consumables keep being reordered

AeroVironment's first fiscal 2027 quarter brought record funded backlog of $1.5bn, up 37% year on year, with $1.4bn unfunded and a 1.4 book-to-bill; Precision Strike revenue, the loitering-munition and counter-drone line, was $197m, up 8%. The Army placed a $51m delivery order for additional Switchblade 600 Block 2 systems on 26 August, and in November the company took a $405m international order for LOCUST X3 high-energy laser counter-drone systems, a BlueHalo line whose economics rest on power and thermal limits rather than magazine depth. Guidance was reaffirmed at $2.125–2.225bn of revenue and $305–325m of adjusted EBITDA on 9 September. "AV's fiscal year 2027 is off to a strong start, with record first-quarter revenue and funded backlog and landmark strategic wins," chairman and chief executive Wahid Nawabi said that day.

The damage in the accounts is older and identifiable. The Satellite Communication Augmentation Resource contract acquired with BlueHalo, grown to roughly $1.7bn, was terminated on 10 March 2026, producing a $240.7m goodwill write-down and a $243.8m quarterly net loss. That is why the trailing earnings multiple is negative and useless. On forward estimates the shares trade at 2.09x sales, against roughly 6x a year ago on the same revenue base, 27.7x forward earnings and 1.03x book.

Kratos guided below its own internal plan

Kratos grew third-quarter revenue 35.7% to $471.8m and booked $1.037bn, leaving $2.067bn of funded and $582.9m of unfunded backlog. Its initial 2027 guide of $2.10–2.20bn assumes an unchanged $1.15 trillion authorization topline and the stopgap resolved by 31 March. "Kratos' internal 2027 revenue and EBITDA financial forecast currently rolls up considerably higher than what we are initially guiding to today, with a substantial part of the 2027 forecast supported by programs of record," chief executive Eric DeMarco told investors on 5 November. Cost-plus work rose to 32% of revenue from 22–23%, which caps margin at roughly a point of expansion next year, and management does not expect positive cash flow until 2029. The competitive hole is real: the Air Force gave collaborative combat aircraft Increment 1 production to Anduril and General Atomics in June, and Kratos is competing in Increment 2. At 2.99x forward sales, against about 12x enterprise value to revenue in May, and 33.7x forward earnings, the de-rating has answered the valuation objection without touching the backlog.

Red Cat's revenue sat in the unfunded column

Red Cat withdrew its 2026 revenue target on 16 November, saying anticipated funding and awards had progressed more slowly than expected and that the Army's Gauntlet II outcome removed an expected near-term contributor. Its first-person-view entry scored top of the field on production capability but weak in low-light and subterranean flight; at Fort Carson, operators flew 1,858 sorties across 23 platforms from 19 companies, with Perennial Autonomy and Neros leading. Quarterly revenue was $11.77m against roughly $40.7m expected, gross margin 0.6% against 6.6%, on scrap, warranty and factory overhead no shipments absorbed. Nine-month operating cash burn was $156.8m, inventory went to $117.2m from $23.5m, and diluted shares reached 136.9m from 99.6m. Chief executive Jeffrey Thompson put the pipeline for the maritime unit at "approximately $2.3 billion" on the 16 November call, of which he described roughly $250m funded in the United States, and described the first customer for it: "We splashed our first boat last December and 11 months later, we have our first customer." With $242.7m of cash against a $289m market value, the floor argument is the balance sheet.

What the quarters explain and what they do not

For AeroVironment and Kratos the fall is compression of a multiple against order books that grew, and the sharpest leg was not theirs: the Nasdaq Composite fell about 10% in the week to 20 November, its worst week since March 2020, on doubts about artificial-intelligence capital spending, and small-cap defense technology went with it. Nothing the two companies disclosed in that window was financial. For Red Cat the markdown is earned, and the cause of the split is structural: replenishment of a munition already in the budget and a funded production backlog convert under a stopgap, while a pipeline of letters of request and a Taiwanese tender for 1,320 unmanned surface vessels, award expected in March 2027, does not.

AeroVironment reports its second fiscal-quarter results in early December, days from the stopgap's expiry. Funded backlog gets paid through a lapse; the companies whose revenue is still an opportunity do not.
