Comfort Systems Stretched Its Backlog to Two Years as Towns Voted to Ban Data Centers

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The electrical and mechanical contractors that build the insides of data centers reported the strongest quarters in their histories and were marked down harder than the chipmakers they serve. Comfort Systems' backlog reached $17.81bn, up 90% year over year; IES Holdings closed fiscal 2026 with record backlog and fourth-quarter operating income up 108%; Legence grew revenue 85%.

None of the three has disclosed a pushout, cancellation or repricing. What changed is local: 41 data-center ballot measures went to voters on 4 November and restrictions carried almost everywhere, while hyperscalers rose that day. The de-rating prices permitting delay into the contractor's schedule rather than the buyer's budget. Only the guided slowdown is earned — Comfort Systems put 2027 same-store growth in the mid-20% range, from the mid-40s in 2026.

The contractors that install power, cooling and piping inside other people's data centers each closed their latest quarter with more signed work on the books than they have ever held, and each has lost between a third and half its market value since August. Comfort Systems USA, which installs and maintains heating, ventilation, plumbing, piping and electrical systems for building owners and general contractors, finished September with backlog of $17.81bn, up 90% from a year earlier and its eighth consecutive sequential increase.

The markdown has been read as the buildout pausing. Nothing in the three companies' disclosure says it is. On 4 November, 41 data-center ballot measures went to voters across ten states, and voters backed restrictions or rejected pro-development measures in nearly every contest. On the same day, hyperscalers and Apple surged as the election removed investor uncertainty.

The chain split. What the votes changed is the schedule on which a signed scope can actually be built, and the market charged that delay to the installer rather than to the buyer.

Five Ohio towns banned it outright

Ohio alone passed at least 14 bans or restrictions. Outright prohibitions now cover Granville, Hubbard, Piqua, Upper Sandusky and Urbana, Ohio, and North Berwick, Maine; Peru, Wells and Eliot, Maine adopted 180-day moratoriums; El Dorado, Kansas voted a ten-year ban on "high impact" data-center development. Comfort Systems fell 13.5% in the morning session of 4 November. The group then took market beta on 20 November, when the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% and Nvidia dropped 14%.

The completion squeeze that has not shown up at Comfort Systems

Third-quarter revenue rose 53.7% to $3.766bn, with operating margin up to 16.6% from 15.5% and gross margin flat at 24.8% through all of that growth. Technology work reached 59% of nine-month revenue, from 42% a year earlier. Backlog duration has stretched to 18-24 months, against roughly nine historically, which is why the siting question matters more here than the demand question.

"I do not know of a project where 1 of these considerations has changed its trajectory," chief financial officer Bill George told investors on 23 October, addressing moratorium fears. He also said: "Our backlog's the best it is ever been." Off-site modular fabrication was 17% of year-to-date revenue, with capacity above 4m sq ft and a target of 6m by the end of 2027.

The shares carry 20.4x forward earnings against 22.5x trailing, where in May 2026 the same business traded near 46x forward at a $69.25bn market value; it is $36.47bn now, on consensus 2027 earnings growth of 31.6% and a trailing free-cash-flow yield of 6.7%.

IES borrowed to buy a steel fabricator

IES Holdings, which engineers and installs electrical and network-infrastructure systems, grew fiscal 2026 revenue 32.8% to $4.478bn, with fourth-quarter operating income up 108% and record backlog of about $4.96bn. "With record levels of backlog at September 30, 2026, we expect continued strong organic growth in fiscal 2027," chief executive Matt Simmes said in the 20 November release. The board added $350m to the repurchase authorization the same day.

It is not the cost-plus counterweight it looks like: the 10-K discloses that approximately 80.3% of revenues are earned on a fixed-price or unit-price basis. The $691m DBM Global purchase, closed 5 October, bolted a structural-steel fabricator and $2.6bn of additional backlog onto the electrical business, funded by expanding the credit facility to $700m and drawing $525m. At 11.6x trailing earnings against 35.3x in May 2026, it is the cheapest of the three, though its forward estimates rest on a single analyst.

Legence stopped being a fee business

Legence, the San Jose building-systems engineer that listed in September 2025, grew third-quarter revenue 85% to $1.311bn, 40% of it organic, with backlog and awards up 92.5% to $5.90bn and a book-to-bill of 1.2x. Its Installation & Maintenance arm grew 124% and is now 63% of revenue, from 42%, while the fee-based Engineering & Consulting leg shrank 6% to $200m. Data centers and technology are 59% of backlog, from 42%.

About 40% of its data-center revenue sits in Northern Virginia, and management says none of it has been paused. "Our pipeline is still really strong in out years, and we're not seeing a slowdown for -- on any of those inbound requests," chief operating officer Steve Hansen said on 12 November. At 16.7x trailing EV/EBITDA, roughly double IES Holdings' 8.6x, it stays the group's richest name on the thinnest gross margin, 21.0%, and sits 15.4% above its $28 IPO price, with Blackstone funds holding about 74% of voting power.

What the quarters do and do not explain

APi Group, weighted to inspection and service, is flat over three months and the only name in the shelf still trending higher; it engages projects at 50-60% construction completion to convert them into recurring accounts, so permit timing moves its revenue later and less. At EMCOR, mechanical margin fell 80bp to 12.1% as AI data-center work migrates toward guaranteed-maximum-price and cost-plus terms, which trims the markup rather than concentrating the risk.

The reported results account for none of the drawdown. The earned part is a second derivative: Comfort Systems guided 2027 same-store growth to the mid-20% range from the mid-40s, and Legence's 2027 revenue guide of $5.3-5.6bn implies 36-39% growth against the 85% just posted. That is deceleration from a doubling, and the multiples now assume it keeps going.

A backlog that burns over two years is a promise about the far side of this decade, underwritten by zoning hearings in towns most holders of these shares could not place on a map. The order books say the work was sold. The ballots decide where it can stand.

Originating hypothesis

category emerging bear backlog conversion mechanics · subject: FIX, IESC, LGN

The building-systems contractors — the electricians and mechanical trades who physically install the power and cooling inside other people's data centers — are the one tech-adjacent shelf on this universe snapshot rolling into an emerging bear at genuinely gradual intensity (the segment averages -13.1% over 30 days and -16.4% over twelve months, a fraction of the -30% markdown taken by foundry and contract manufacturing on the same snapshot), and the question is mechanical rather than directional: establish how a signed data-center electrical or mechanical scope actually turns into recognised revenue and cash at each of these three businesses, since Comfort Systems earns percentage-of-completion revenue on largely fixed-price mechanical work whose margin is set by craft-labour productivity and prefabrication, IES Holdings runs a thinner-overhead electrical and infrastructure-solutions model with much shorter order-to-revenue cycles, and newly public Legence sells engineering and energy-services work billed closer to a fee than a build — so the same "the buildout is pausing" markdown is landing on a fixed-price construction risk, a labour-arbitrage business and a services annuity at once.
