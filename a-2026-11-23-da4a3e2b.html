Cohu Books Half Its Revenue From Consumables as Hotter AI Chips Burn Through Contactors

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Three suppliers sitting underneath everyone else's advanced-packaging capacity were marked down together as if the physical consumption of test parts had turned down. Only one of them is actually paid per die. Cohu's recurring revenue, primarily consumables, was 48% of September-quarter revenue, its test-cell utilization rose to 82%, and bookings were up 126% year over year with book-to-bill above 1.

The other two earn differently. FormFactor's DRAM probe-card revenue of $84.9m was flat sequentially with mix moving to non-HBM DDR5, and its growth came from foundry and logic; Ichor runs a 15.8% gross margin on work that is roughly 70% build-to-print, with Lam Research and Applied Materials at 76% of 2025 sales.

No order cut, pushout or book-to-bill below 1 appears anywhere in the group. What changed is the rating: Cohu's price-to-gross-profit is 7.4x against 10.9x in May 2026.

Cohu, which sells back-end test handlers and the contactors and probe heads that wear out inside them, reported that recurring revenue, primarily consumables, was 48% of its September-quarter revenue. Utilization across its installed base of test cells rose to 82%, two points sequentially and 7.5 points higher than a year earlier, and it took its first interface orders with two hyperscale customers for processor final test. Consumption of test parts per chip is the one thing a consumables supplier can actually observe, and at Cohu it went up.

That matters because Cohu fell 32% in the thirty sessions to 20 November alongside FormFactor, down 34%, and Ichor Holdings, down 38% — a trio treated as a single layer underneath high-bandwidth memory and advanced packaging. The assumption inside that markdown was a demand assumption: that per-die test and sub-fab consumption had begun to roll over. Read company by company, the three are not one business, and only Cohu gets paid by the die.

The meter that moves when a chip gets hotter

Chief executive Luis Müller put the mechanism in his own words on 28 October: 2027 revenue growth of 30% to 40% is "supported more by rising power and test intensity than by processor unit volume growth as customers increasingly require the higher-performance thermal solutions Cohu is well positioned to supply." He also raised the fiscal 2026 high-performance-computing revenue outlook to roughly $120m and set a goal of delivering about three times as many Eclipse handlers in 2027 as in 2026. Gross margin reached 45.5% in the quarter against 34.1% a year earlier, and the December-quarter guide of $178m ± $7m is 46% above the prior year.

This is an inflection off a trough, not a trend: Cohu lost $74.3m in fiscal 2025 on $453.0m of revenue. On consensus 2027 earnings of $2.40 the shares sit near 15.6x, and price-to-gross-profit is 7.4x against 10.9x in May 2026.

FormFactor sells the socket, not the wear part

FormFactor's September-quarter probe-card revenue was $221.1m of $280.5m total, split $134.0m foundry and logic and $84.9m DRAM, and its 10-Q attributes the DRAM increase primarily to non-HBM DDR5 designs with foundry and logic driven by networking and server CPU demand. DRAM probe cards were flat sequentially at record levels as customers optimized wafer starts for DDR profitability. The growth engine is share, not stack height: "We shipped probe cards to the world's leading foundry for 1 single GPU design in multimillion-dollar volumes," Michael Slessor said on 28 October. Chief financial officer Aric McKinnis told investors the company's Farmers Branch plant should be capable of about $400m of annual probe-card revenue exiting 2027, against just over $1bn for all of 2026. Operating margin has gone 13.1% to 29.1% across four quarters; the shares trade at 21.6x forward earnings against 32.9x trailing.

Ichor is a tool-build derivative

Ichor assembles gas panels and chemical delivery systems for equipment makers, and Lam Research and Applied Materials were 76% of its 2025 sales. Its 15.8% gross margin against FormFactor's 54.7% is the signature: proprietary content is 30% of revenue, targeted at about 35% exiting the fourth quarter. Its order book follows its customers' build plans, and those plans grew — Applied guided January-quarter revenue to roughly $11.1bn with Semiconductor Systems up 69% on 12 November, and Lam put preliminary 2027 equipment spending above $200bn, led by DRAM. Ichor guided the December quarter to $345m–365m and chief executive Phil Barros said on 2 November that "we expect Ichor's earnings growth in 2027 will outpace revenue growth by over 2x." The only shortfalls disclosed were supply-side: about $5m from component shortages in high-end etch and deposition, about $5m from space launch delays. Price-to-gross-profit is 7.1x against 19.7x in May.

What the five sessions did

The fall was concentrated and shared. On 20 November, Ichor fell 15.2%, FormFactor 10.8% and Cohu 8.5% inside a 5.3% drop in the Nasdaq Composite as Nvidia fell 14% on a reported accounting probe; Applied Materials, Lam, Teradyne and MKS all landed within the same band. All three names remain 50% to 91% above their 20 November 2025 closes.

So the demand question answers itself differently in each case, and none of the three answers is "down". Cohu's utilization and contactor wins measure consumption directly. FormFactor's measures design wins and plant capacity, with management describing capacity rather than demand as the limiting factor; its HBM mix went sideways in the quarter, which is the one genuine soft spot in the per-die story. Ichor's measures two customers' build rates, both of which guided up after the selling began. What a professional is weighing across the three is therefore not one exposure but three: a consumables meter, a share gain, and a capex pass-through with a mix-shift margin story attached.

Seven of Cohu's ranked high-performance-computing programs, about $340m of annual systems revenue, are due to hit major qualification milestones between late November and the first quarter of 2027, on a nine-to-twelve-month path from qualification to production. Qualification is where a consumables position is won or lost, and it is slower than a five-session markdown.
