Tower Semiconductor's Customers Prepaid $249m to Reserve Wafer Capacity Through 2029

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Tower Semiconductor has been marked down almost by half since late September, and the balance that would show demand cracking went the other way: customer prepayments and deferred revenue stand near $249m against $26m at the end of 2025, with signed commitments running to 2029.

Silicon photonics, the chips that carry data as light inside artificial-intelligence racks, contributed about $240m of Tower's $526m third-quarter revenue. Gross margin widened from 26.8% in the first quarter of 2026 to 34.0% in the third, and management raised its long-term revenue model by 27%.

The business does not explain the fall; the rating does. Tower's forward multiple compressed from roughly 53x in late September to 30.4x while 2027 and 2028 estimates rose. GlobalFoundries and UMC also grew faster each quarter this year as their shares fell.

Tower Semiconductor's customers are paying the Israeli foundry before any wafer ships. The company, which makes analog-intensive mixed-signal chips for telecom, automotive, industrial and medical customers from fabs in Migdal Haemek and abroad, reported customer prepayments and deferred revenue of about $249m in its third-quarter presentation, against $26m at the end of 2025, plus $92m of long-term customer advances against $2m. Its Q2 filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission put deferred revenue and customers' advances at $176.0m at 30 June and disclosed $290m received to reserve silicon-photonics capacity covering roughly $1.3bn of 2027 revenue. The commitments now run through 2029 for both 200mm and 300mm production.

That matters because the November selloff in foundry shares was read as the market calling the top in chip-factory spending. Tower's own disclosures say the opposite about its order book. Revenue growth accelerated through 2026, from 15.5% year on year in the first quarter to 23.7% and then 33.0%, and gross margin expanded from 26.8% to 34.0% over the same three quarters. Gross profit rose 92% year on year.

Half the company is now light

Silicon photonics alone was about $240m of the $526m third quarter, roughly 46% of revenue, and Tower guided its annualized silicon-photonics run rate above $1.1bn on fourth-quarter group revenue of about $600m. Its 200mm fabs ran at 85–90% utilization and the 300mm fab ran above its 85% model. Management raised the long-term annual model to $3.6bn of revenue at a 45% gross margin, $760m higher than the February 2026 version.

"This annual net profit of $1.2 billion target is $450 million higher than the pre February 2026 model, representing 59% incremental net profit derived from the incremental $760 million revenue, reflecting our enhanced product mix," chief financial officer Oren Shirazi told investors on 2 November.

The prepayments are the mechanism behind that confidence. "Customers will never sign up to being the end of '26 now, not '27, not just '28, but 2029, unless they have faith that, number one, you can ship wafers on time at the highest quality," chief executive Russell Ellwanger said on the same call. Tower's $920m capacity program was 65% paid through the third quarter, self-funded, with every long-term agreement securing that capacity signed. On 3 November it announced a collaboration with Innolight on near-package optics for AI infrastructure.

The fall was the rating, not the estimates

Shares fell 9.6% on 20 November, rose 18.4% on 23 November and fell 8.7% again on 24 November, leaving them 46.8% below the 24 September high. That is the signature of index-level repricing: Nvidia dropped 14% on 20 November after Reuters reported an SEC review of its accounting, and futures markets put roughly 91% odds on a Federal Reserve rate increase on 9 December.

Tower now trades at 30.4x consensus earnings for this year, against about 53x at its 24 September close on the same estimate, and 17.3x 2027 consensus. Consensus revenue growth for 2027 is 39.8%. Nothing in the estimates moved down.

The same gap sits at the other two. GlobalFoundries, a specialty foundry in Malta, New York, posted its first year-on-year blended wafer price increase since 2023, up 4.8%, and chief executive Tim Breen said on 4 November the company is "absolutely booked out through 2027"; its forward multiple went from roughly 23.6x in late September to 18.3x. UMC, the Hsinchu contract foundry, lifted utilization from 85% to 92% on record shipments of 1.2m 12-inch equivalents and guides a 10% blended price rise for 2027; it trades at 7.1x trailing enterprise value to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.

What breaks in 2028

The credible risk is not mature-node commoditization. It is that everyone builds the same photonics capacity at once: Tower's Japanese fabs, GlobalFoundries Malta, STMicroelectronics and UMC's Singapore shells land together, with Samsung Foundry arriving from 2027, against demand that TSMC expects to take silicon photonics past half the optical transceiver market by 2027. Ellwanger himself flagged discounting by entrants without technical differentiation as a near-term risk. Tower has the thinnest valuation cushion of the three and the richest growth assumption inside it.

Until that capacity arrives, the distinguishing fact is whose customers pay early. A buyer who wires cash in 2026 to hold a 2029 wafer slot has made a statement that a price chart cannot revise.
