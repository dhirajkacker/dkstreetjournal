Dollar Tree, Dollar General and Ollie's Spent Their Tariff Refunds on Shelf Prices

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Three discount retailers posted their widest gross margins in years this summer, and in each case most of the gain was money the federal government gave back. The margin bridges were read as a trade-down windfall finally reaching the shelf; strip the refund out and the three businesses point in different directions.

Dollar Tree booked $383m of refunded duties, 680 of its 850 basis points of expansion, and is reinvesting about $210m in prices, marketing and stores. Ollie's whole 360 basis points of expansion was refund and more, leaving underlying margin near 40.3% against its own 40.5% target on a comparable sales decline of 1.8%. Only Dollar General's core improved: comps up 3.5% on traffic up 2.0%, a fifth straight quarter of traffic growth, with 81 of 127 basis points from the refund.

Dollar Tree's summer gross margin was the widest the chain has printed in years, and four-fifths of the improvement was a refund of duties the Supreme Court decided the government never had the authority to collect. After February's ruling that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act did not support the tariffs levied under it, roughly $166bn of duties began returning to importers of record, with interest, through a Customs and Border Protection refund portal. Dollar General and Ollie's Bargain Outlet booked their shares inside cost of goods sold in the same quarter.

The bridges were read as proof that the trade-down shopper is finally paying off at the margin line. All three companies are instead handing the refund to that shopper as lower shelf prices, which makes the expansion a one-year event; and underneath it, only one of the three businesses got better.

The refund, by how much of the margin it bought

At Dollar Tree, which sells a fixed-price assortment of food, household basics and seasonal general merchandise, 680 of 850 basis points of gross-margin expansion in the quarter ended 1 August was net tariff impact, taking gross margin to 42.9%. At Ollie's, the closeout chain that buys brand-name overstock and liquidation lots, the refund was worth 380 basis points of a 360 basis-point expansion: all of it, and more. At Dollar General, the rural small-box chain, 81 of 127 basis points.

None of the three banked it. "We will not hesitate to invest beyond this level to strengthen our price leadership position," Eric van der Valk, Ollie's president and chief executive, told investors on 2 September. Dollar Tree is putting about $210m of its $383m back into cost of goods, marketing and store operations.

Dollar Tree's price is printed before the cost is known

Dollar Tree publishes its price in advance, so landed cost, freight and duty land in gross margin rather than in revenue. The levers are the item behind the price and the share of the assortment lifted above the $1.25 anchor: multi-price merchandise reached 17% of total sales, up about 400 basis points year over year, across roughly 6,600 stores. "Rather than passing those costs on to the customer, we've taken advantage of the fact that we're getting that lower tariff rate in and that tariff rate is absorbing inflation," chief financial officer Stewart Glendinning said on 27 August.

That absorption has a term. Management guided third-quarter gross margin flat and fourth-quarter margin down year over year on fuel, freight and merchandise inflation, with third-quarter adjusted earnings of $0.80 to $0.95 a share including a $0.50 reinvestment headwind. Investors priced the guide: the shares fell through late September and are 16.3% below the 25 August high of $133.74. At 15.0x forward earnings against 14.5x trailing, the forward figure sits higher because consensus of $7.90 is below the $8.19 of trailing earnings that contains the refund quarter's $1.31.

Ollie's buying was never the problem

Ollie's margin is a buying outcome, and the buying was fine: TJX, Ross and Burlington all describe closeout supply as highly favorable. Demand was the problem. Comparable sales fell 1.8% on flat traffic and a smaller basket, with unseasonable weather in lawn and garden and air conditioners costing more than 100 basis points of comp; acquired Big Lots boxes are comping low-to-mid single digits negative in their second year. Full-year sales guidance was cut to $2.928–2.941bn from $2.98–3.00bn, comps to flat-to-+0.5%.

The stock then rose 17.6% in thirty days, alongside TJX at 9.9% and Burlington at 13.7%, while Five Below fell 13.9%. The likelier reading is a bid for closeout and off-price retailing generally; consensus earnings of $4.61 sit at the midpoint of the cut guide, with no upward revision behind the move. At 18.5x forward Ollie's is the dearest of the three, and 19.0x trailing against 36.7x a year ago is a de-rating interrupted.

Dollar General earns it on shrink and trucks

With consumables at 82% of net sales, Dollar General has no price point to defend and makes its money on shrink, markdowns and distribution cost. "This marks the fifth consecutive quarter of growth in customer traffic," chief executive Todd Vasos said on 27 August, on comps of 3.5% with traffic up 2.0%. Operating income rose 29.2% on sales up 5.2%, full-year earnings guidance went to $7.80–8.00 from $7.20–7.45, and up to $700m of buybacks was pulled into the second half. The shares are up 28.9% over twelve months while the trailing multiple fell to 16.5x from roughly 18.3x.

What the pump price decides

The refund accounts for four-fifths of Dollar Tree's margin gain, roughly two-thirds of Dollar General's and all of Ollie's. What it does not explain is why the traffic went one way. Gasoline at $4.359 a gallon, up 39.4% in a year, taxes a destination trip and spares a fill-in one: Ollie's blames longer drive times in Midwest, Texas and Western markets, while Dollar General's shopper is a few miles away and its remodel program adds comp mechanically. There is also a claim on the windfall itself, since retailers that passed the duties to shoppers now face consumer class actions arguing the refunds belong to those shoppers.

All three report again in December. By then the refund is spent money, and what is left on the shelf is a price each company has promised to hold without it.

The Advice Fees Meta's AI Threatens Are a Tenth of Schwab's Revenue; Cash Spread Is Half

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Investors spent two September sessions selling brokerages on the theory that a consumer AI assistant eats into paid human advice. At Charles Schwab, advice is the small line: managed investing solutions brought in $707m of $7.07bn of net revenues in the June quarter, while the spread on swept client cash and margin lending brought in $3.4bn.

Six days before that selloff the Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75-4.00%, which mechanically widens the spread Schwab is paid on; the company already guides fourth-quarter net interest margin to 3.25-3.30% from 3.00%. Schwab still fell 9.9% over thirty sessions, roughly three times Interactive Brokers' decline.

Robinhood and Nu Holdings share Schwab's sector label and almost none of its economics: Robinhood's new earnings leg is prediction markets, and Nu's funding cost is struck against a Brazilian benchmark that is falling.

Meta launched a consumer artificial-intelligence assistant called Muse in late September, and among the shares that fell hardest were those of a company whose largest earnings engine is holding other people's idle cash. Charles Schwab, the retail brokerage and custodian for independent advisers that also owns banks, dropped 6.1% on 22 September to close at $100.35, on 16.9m shares against a typical 5m to 9m, its heaviest-volume down day of the quarter. LPL Financial fell 7% and Raymond James 3.5% on the same reasoning; the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.45% that day.

The trade priced a belief — that an assistant managing a household's money erodes demand for paid advice. Schwab's own revenue decomposition says that is the thinnest part of the franchise. Managed investing solutions produced $707m of the record $7.07bn of net revenues reported for the June quarter, about a tenth. Net interest revenue was $3.4bn, 47% of the total and up 19% on the year.

The line that got sold is not the line that pays

That $3.4bn is a banking spread wearing a brokerage's clothes. Uninvested client balances are swept off the brokerage statement into Schwab's own banks, reinvested, and paid back to the client at a rate Schwab sets. Transactional sweep cash ended June at $485.7bn, roughly 3.7% of $13.08trn in client assets, with on-balance-sheet bank deposits of $249.7bn and a further $70.6bn parked at third-party banks, where fees rose 35% on improved net yield. Margin loans add a second spread, at $165.1bn of client balances.

Six days before Muse, the Federal Reserve raised its target range 25bp to 3.75-4.00% in a unanimous vote, its first increase since 2023. A higher funds rate lifts the reinvestment yield on swept cash while the client rate stays administered. Net interest margin had already widened 12 basis points in the quarter to 3.00%, with management guiding fourth-quarter margin to 3.25-3.30%.

One caution the pitch usually omits: the cheap part of that repair is done. Higher-cost bank supplemental funding fell $44.8bn during 2025 to $5.1bn, so further expansion has to come from securities repricing and the policy rate. Asset gathering, meanwhile, accelerated: $120bn of core net new assets in the quarter, then a record August gather of $64.8bn, up 46% year on year.

Schwab lost 9.9% over the thirty sessions to 9 October, to $96.70. Interactive Brokers lost 3.8% in the same stretch and an S&P 500 index fund rose 2.2%, so the de-rating was Schwab's own.

Robinhood is paid rebates — and so is Schwab

Robinhood Markets, the 2,900-employee US platform for stocks, options, gold and crypto, is the supposed mirror: order-flow rebates instead of commissions. The mirror is cloudy. Schwab booked $528m of commissions and $624m of order-flow revenue in the June quarter, and that order-flow line alone exceeded Robinhood's entire $342m options business.

Robinhood's net revenues rose 32% to $1.31bn with net deposits of a record $21.7bn, while crypto revenue fell 38% to $100m and event contracts — regulated betting on outcomes — rose more than tenfold to $156m. Its cash spread is the laggard: net interest revenues grew only 9% to $389m, held back by lower short-term rates and weaker securities lending. Where the two firms differ is deliberate. Schwab chief executive Rick Wurster told the 21 July call the firm would support financial-event and economic-data products but has "no interest" in sports gambling or non-financial betting.

Robinhood fell 6.7% over the same thirty sessions, as bitcoin broke below $81,000 on 8 October and crypto liquidations approached $1bn. At 49.6x forward earnings against 47.2x trailing, consensus has FY26 earnings up 7.7% after 2025's 114%.

Nu's benchmark sits in Brasília

Nu Holdings, the São Paulo digital bank with 139m customers, shares the sector label and none of the plumbing. Its cost of deposits was 88% of the Brazilian interbank CDI rate, three percentage points better than a year earlier, funding an unsecured card and lending book: net interest income of $3.7bn and margin up 180 basis points to 22.9%. Copom cut the Selic to 13.75% on 16 September, the same week the Fed hiked. The blemish is credit — 90-day-plus non-performing loans rose 35 basis points to 6.9%. Nu rose 6.8% over the thirty sessions, trades at 18.5x forward against 21.8x trailing on a 33% return on equity, and reported-dollar revenue growth of 49.9% against 39% excluding currency, so roughly ten points of the headline was the real.

Which anchor you pick decides the answer

The business results account for none of Schwab's fall and arguably argue the other way. What they cannot settle is value, because Schwab's two anchors disagree. On earnings it is 14.9x forward against 17.5x trailing, the gap implying consensus FY26 earnings per share of $6.49, a third above FY25. On bank capital it is near 6.9x tangible book value of $14.10 a share, against a ten-year median of 5.29 — a premium, not a discount. Sell-side opinion split accordingly: Piper Sandler cut its target to $100 on 2 October while Bank of America raised its to $95 and stayed at Underperform.

The three names did not move together because they are not paid alike: Schwab on an administered deposit rate the Fed just made more valuable, Robinhood on volatility and product launches, Nu on a discount to a falling Brazilian benchmark.

Schwab reports third-quarter results before the open on 15 October, with consensus looking for $1.67 a share. The number that matters is the margin on cash, which is the one line Meta's assistant cannot touch.

Prepaid Tuition at TAL and New Oriental Is Growing Slower Than the Revenue It Funds

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Chinese tutoring companies collect the fee before the class is taught, which makes the prepaid balance the one line in their accounts that leads the income statement. At TAL Education, New Oriental and Gaotu Techedu that balance is now growing more slowly than reported revenue — TAL's deferred tuition rose 25.9% in the year to 31 May against revenue up 31.9% — and guidance follows the book, with New Oriental pointing to 14–18% revenue growth this fiscal year.

The shares went the other way. All three were in downtrends in July and in uptrends by late September, Gaotu most of all, with no earnings release inside the thirty days to 9 October. TAL's results carry the move best: an 18.1% operating margin on capacity leased a year early. Gaotu's carry it worst, with selling expense alone absorbing 54.7% of revenue.

China's tutoring companies are paid before they teach. Parents settle the term at enrollment, the money sits as a liability, and it turns into revenue class hour by class hour as the course is delivered. That makes the prepaid balance the only figure in these accounts that moves ahead of the income statement, and at TAL Education, the Beijing provider of after-school small-group classes under the Xueersi brands, it grew 25.9% in the year to 31 May, to $1.22bn, while the revenue it funds grew 31.9%.

The past quarter has been read as a recovery still gathering force: the three listed operators of this business left July downtrends behind and rose about 31% equal-weighted over three months. Their collections say the fastest point is behind them. New Oriental's deferred revenue rose 14.8% against 23.2% revenue growth; Gaotu's 18.9% against 20.2%. At each one the reported line is draining a book that was filled at a quicker rate, and both companies that gave guidance pointed at the high teens.

The regulator decides how far the money can run ahead

A framework issued by 13 departments including the Ministry of Education bars non-academic tutoring institutions from collecting a lump sum covering more than three months, 60 class hours or RMB5,000, about $716, and requires offline classes to finish by 20:30. So the forward book cannot be a year deep. Derived from the filings, TAL's balance equals 1.61 times the $758.4m it recognized in the May quarter, New Oriental's 1.46 times and Gaotu's 1.56 times: the same cushion of roughly a quarter and a half at three companies of very different size. The cash timing inside the year is violent. TAL took in $478.2m of operating cash in the May quarter, 63% of that quarter's revenue, after using $215.0m in the February quarter.

TAL filled capacity it had already leased

TAL's operating margin reached 18.1% in the May quarter against 2.5% a year earlier, on a gross margin of 57.8%, the best in five quarters. Centers are leased and staffed before seats are sold — more than 600 across 44 cities — so that margin is a utilization reading. "Our first quarter performance in fiscal year 2027 reflects solid execution across our businesses," president and chief financial officer Alex Peng said on 30 July. Two qualifications: revenue rose 32% in dollars but only 25% in renminbi, and the learning-content slab of books, apps and devices reached 35.3% of fiscal 2026 revenue from 31.8%. Yet group gross margin widened to 55.4% from 53.3%, so the hardware mix is not visibly diluting the service margin. The shares trade at 9.1x forward earnings, with price-to-gross-profit compressing to 3.73x forward from 4.37x trailing.

New Oriental's grocery arm is taking the margin

New Oriental's revenue growth accelerated through fiscal 2026 — 6.1%, 14.9%, 19.8%, then 23.2% — and non-GAAP operating margin reached 7.5%, up 470 basis points. Here the mix dilution is real: East Buy, the consolidated livestreaming and private-label goods business, grew 34.4% to $806.9m, 14.3% of sales, while group gross margin slipped to 54.6% from 55.4%. Overseas test preparation, the product the 2021 academic ban left untouched, grew only 6%; adults and university students grew 30%; non-academic tutoring booked 1,072,000 enrollments. "In this fiscal quarter, we executed cautious capacity expansion while carefully balancing revenue growth and operational efficiency," chief executive Chenggang Zhou said on 29 July. At 19.6x trailing earnings against 13.3x forward, the spread is what is being paid for that 14–18% guide, alongside a commitment to return no less than half of prior-year net income.

Gaotu buys its students at a price its classroom cannot cover

Gaotu's selling expenses were CNY913.2m in the June quarter, 54.7% of revenue, against a 66.5% gross margin: acquisition consumes about four-fifths of each gross-profit yuan, which is how that gross margin still ends at minus 9.0% at the operating line. Revenue growth has run 30.7%, 21.4%, 12.5% and 19.9% over four quarters, with the September quarter guided to 16.4–17.7%. The first half of 2026 carries a CNY101.2m net loss against a consensus full-year profit of CNY14.4m. "Footprint growth itself is not the main KPI," chief operating officer Bin Luo said on 27 August, naming profitable sites as the goal. Gaotu moved most of the three, up 49.5% over three months, and is still down 14.3% over twelve.

Thirty days with nothing in them

No filing falls inside the window: TAL last reported on 30 July, New Oriental on 29 July, Gaotu on 27 August. A Central Committee and State Council document on vocational education lifted all three in one session, TAL more than 4% and Gaotu about 1%. The likelier reading of the re-rating is sector and policy rather than anything that happened in a classroom. What the results do support is operating leverage at TAL, where a year-old capacity decision is now being absorbed, and the margin step at New Oriental. What they do not support is growth still accelerating anywhere in the group: New Oriental, the fastest accelerator on paper, had already eased back to a milder uptrend on 5 October.

Beijing has not softened. In January regulators fined an unregistered education firm RMB67m, explicitly to kill rumors of an academic-tutoring comeback. What these three sell is what the rules permit, collected three months at a time, and three months is all the warning their balance sheets are allowed to give.
