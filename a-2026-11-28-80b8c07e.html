Petrobras Is Buying Renewable Data Center Space in São Paulo, Not Selling Power to It

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Brazil's dominant natural-gas supplier owns the thermal plants, pipelines and processing capacity that firm power for a São Paulo data centre would have to come from, and its gas division's earnings just jumped. The buyer is the national grid operator.

Petrobras's Gas and Low Carbon Energy segment earned US$521m of adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter, up 156.7% year on year, almost all of it capacity payments from a March reserve auction that began paying on 1 August. That is 2.8% of group adjusted EBITDA. The five-year plan approved on 23 November cut the gas and low carbon allocation to US$8bn from US$9bn and funds existing thermal and processing assets for reliability.

The only disclosed Petrobras data-centre contract has Petrobras as the customer, required by its own terms to run on certified renewable energy.

Petrobras's largest disclosed connection to Brazil's artificial-intelligence buildout is a bill it pays. Elea Data Centers won a 17-year, R$2.3bn award to build a 30 MVA liquid-cooled site at São Bernardo do Campo in São Paulo to host the state-controlled oil company's exploration and reservoir supercomputers, and Petrobras wrote into the contract that the facility run entirely on certified renewable energy with full water reuse. Elea completed the first phase by May 2026.

The asset base invites the opposite trade. Petrobras explores for and produces crude, refines and sells fuel, and separately supplies and trades natural gas and electricity, operating the thermoelectric fleet, the gas-processing plants and the regasification terminals from which any firm, dispatchable megawatt in Brazil must ultimately be sourced. Its gas earnings did step up sharply this year, which invites a reading in which a power annuity is quietly forming underneath the barrels.

The counterparty settles it.

The buyer of the new power revenue is the grid

The Gas and Low Carbon Energy segment's adjusted EBITDA reached US$521m in the third quarter of 2026, up 156.7% year on year, lifted by fixed revenue from Brazil's Electricity Capacity Reserve Auction contract that took effect in August and by the start-up of the Rota 3 pipeline and the Itaboraí processing unit in August and September. Petrobras contracted nine thermal plants totalling roughly 2.6 GW of firm capacity for 2026 to 2031, with estimated fixed revenue of R$44bn.

Those payments are an availability fee owed by the national system. The March auctions contracted 18.97 GW nationally with R$38.9bn of guaranteed annual fixed revenue, Petrobras taking about 14% of the award and independent developers, among them Eneva with its US$3.2bn thermal capex programme, the rest. At US$521m the segment is 2.8% of the group's US$18.394bn of quarterly adjusted EBITDA.

The data-centre socket is being filled elsewhere. Renewable energy companies accounted for 65% of requests to connect data centres to the main grid, and 86.8% of Brazil's 2025 electricity came from renewable sources. Grid operator ONS awarded all 228 MW of transmission capacity it released in São Paulo to three data-centre projects in October 2026.

The five-year plan pays to keep the thermal fleet running

The board approved a 2027–2031 plan on 23 November carrying US$122bn of capex, of which US$87bn, or 71.3%, goes to exploration and production and US$8bn to gas and low carbon, down from US$9bn in the prior plan. Inside that, only US$2bn sits in the implementation portfolio for gas and power, aimed at "maintaining the reliability and availability of thermal power, gas processing and regasification terminal assets."

What is being built is barrels. Production hit a record 2.823m barrels of oil equivalent a day, refining ran at 99% utilisation, and operating cash flow guidance went to US$45–50bn from US$35bn, with capex guidance up to US$21–22bn from US$16–17.4bn. "We went from an expected Brent of $63 to something of around $90–$100 on average per year," chief financial officer Fernando Melgarejo said on the 11 November earnings call. "The resulting increase in cash generation enabled us to step up investment execution."

What the quarter cannot explain

Revenue rose 40.4% year on year and net income 28.4%, net debt fell to US$57.8bn, and the 45%-of-free-cash-flow dividend formula held, with R$15.3bn of interim dividends approved on 10 November. The common ADR still fell 35.6% between 23 October and 27 November, 14.0% of it on 26 October, the first session after Lula's runoff win. The real lost about 10% against the dollar and the Ibovespa fell 26.8% in dollar terms from early October. Chief executive Magda Chambriard called the drop a "speculative move".

The results account for almost none of the decline; currency translation and state-intervention risk cover most of it, and the shares are still up 33% over twelve months. The 3.5x forward earnings on the common, against 4.3x trailing, rests on consensus earnings per share that peak in 2026 at US$4.80 and then decline 13.5% in 2027, with the Búzios field's cost-recovery ceiling cutting Petrobras's working interest by roughly 85,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day each year from the fourth quarter. The gas annuity underneath is contracted, real and small, and the plan does not grow it.

One tell sits between the two listed lines. The voting common's premium over the dividend-preferred widened through the selloff, from 11.2% on 23 October to 12.3% on 27 November, so the line carrying the payout preference fell slightly harder than the line the federal government controls.

Petrobras paid R$251bn of taxes in the first nine months of the year to its own majority owner. The market's worry is the size of the cheque, not who signs it.
