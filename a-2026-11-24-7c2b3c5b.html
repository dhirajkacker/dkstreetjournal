Entegris's Per-Wafer Materials Growth Went From 6% to 20% in Three Quarters

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Chip equipment and materials stocks were marked down together this month as though a single tool-buying cycle governed all of them. Entegris, which supplies filtration, chemical-mechanical polishing slurries and specialty chemicals consumed inside working fabs, told investors on 29 October that roughly 70% of its revenue is paid per wafer and per process layer, and that line accelerated from about 6% growth in the first quarter to 20% in the third, roughly twice the wafer-start growth it expects for the year. Gross margin reached 49.7%.

The de-rating landed on three different ways of getting paid for the same wafers. ASML's system prices were agreed before shipment and management says existing backlog does not reprice. Axcelis is the one with genuinely soft trailing numbers, gross margin at 41.0% against 46.1% in early 2025, even as its bookings hit their best level since 2023.

Entegris crossed a billion dollars of quarterly sales for the first time on 29 October, and almost none of it came from chipmakers buying machines. The Billerica, Massachusetts supplier of filtration, slurries and advanced materials handling reported net sales of $1,006m against a consensus near $924m, up 25% from a year earlier and 14% sequentially, with non-GAAP earnings of $1.23 a share.

That matters because the November selling in semiconductor capital equipment treated the whole shelf as one order cycle. Across a nineteen-name group of equipment and materials suppliers, the thirty days to 23 November averaged a loss of about 8%, with fifteen of the nineteen lower and the worst damage in back-end test and components, Teradyne down 24% and FormFactor down 17%. Entegris was one of four that did not fall. Its business is not paid when a tool is ordered; it is paid when a wafer runs.

Entegris bills the fab every wafer it runs

Management puts roughly 30% of revenue as capital-expenditure driven, which leaves about 70% tied to units and layers. "Unit-driven revenue accelerated from approximately 6% in the first quarter to 20% in the third quarter, that is roughly twice the rate of underlying wafer start growth we expect for the year," chief executive Dave Reeder told investors on 29 October. The gap is layer count and tighter contamination control per wafer, not more equipment: more process steps mean more filtration passes and more slurry.

The margin moved with it. Gross margin reached 49.7% in the September quarter from 46.9% in the first, and Reeder credited execution rather than price: "We've improved adjusted gross margin by 530 basis points year-over-year, independent of any pricing action." Backlog rose 35% sequentially and doubled year on year, book-to-bill ran above 1.0, and polishing-slurry growth stepped up to the upper teens from low single digits. The company also won extreme-ultraviolet filtration content at the 2-nanometer node.

The shares are cheaper per dollar of gross profit than they were six months ago: 13.1x trailing gross profit, against 16.4x in May and 14.5x in mid-August, while trailing gross profit grew 42.4%.

ASML's prices were agreed before the tools ship

ASML, the sole supplier of extreme-ultraviolet lithography, is paid out of a book written years in advance. September-quarter revenue rose 55.2% to €11.662bn with gross margin of 57.4%, all EUV output through 2027 is sold, and non-current customer advances, cash for tools due more than a year out, jumped to €9.30bn from €3.57bn in one quarter. "Given the dynamics of this quarter, we now anticipate that our current planned levels of output in 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet the demand of our customers," chief financial officer Roger Dassen said on 14 October. Service and upgrades on installed tools added €3.3bn, about 28% of the quarter. ASML trades at 22.8x trailing gross profit against 31.4x in early May.

Axcelis, which sells ion implanters, is the one where the trailing line actually deteriorated. Full-year 2025 revenue fell 17.6% to $839.0m, and gross margin was 41.0% in the September quarter against 46.1% in the first quarter of 2025, a miss management attributed to sharp Korean won appreciation. Yet bookings of $222m produced a 1.5x book-to-bill, its best since late 2023, and China held at 46% of revenue, flat on the prior quarter. At 8.1x trailing gross profit against 10.9x in early May, it is the cheapest of the three and sits 48% below its 52-week high.

Beta did most of the work

To 23 November, ASML was down 12.0% over thirty days and Axcelis 5.7%, while Entegris was up 5.6%. Measured to the 20 November trough three days earlier, the same window reads -22.4%, -20.1% and -12.9%. On 23 November the three rose 9.3%, 14.1% and 11.3% as the market stepped back into AI names, roughly two to three times the 4.7% gain in the S&P 500 tracker, having fallen with it when the index dropped 3.6% on 20 November.

So the month explains itself as a valuation event amplified by beta, not an order event. No pushout, cancellation or sub-1.0 book-to-bill appears in any of the three disclosures. The split between them is in how each would feel a real slowdown: ASML's agreed slots and prepayments mean its 2027 revenue is largely contracted and, management says, does not reprice; Entegris collects on wafers already running in cleanrooms already built; Axcelis has to sell a machine, which is why its trailing revenue fell while its forward book inflected.

The constraint Axcelis chief executive Russell Low named on 5 November cuts both ways for the consumables case. He is watching "very, very carefully the availability of kind of greenfield clean room space because clearly, you know, today, our customers are often 4 wall constrained." Fabs with no room to add tools still add layers, and Entegris gets paid for every one of them. But Reeder's own caution was that units from genuinely new fabs land in 2028 and 2029, which is a long time to be paid by the layer.
