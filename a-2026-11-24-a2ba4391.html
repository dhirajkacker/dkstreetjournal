Gentex Keeps Selling More Electronics Per Car While the Market Prices It With Robotaxis

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A mirror maker shipped far fewer mirrors last quarter and barely lost any car revenue, because the content it fits on each vehicle it still supplies went up. That is the whole defense, and it is now colliding with a component bill repricing faster than Gentex can pass it through.

Gentex shipped 9.61m auto-dimming mirrors in the September quarter, down 14.5% from a year earlier, while automotive revenue fell only 3.7% to $552.5m. Gross margin still slipped 100 basis points to 33.4%, and management flagged $10-15m of additional fourth-quarter costs it does not expect to recover until the first half of 2027.

The stock is priced on the same shelf as Mobileye and Hesai, where the autonomy story has been marked down hard. Gentex is not in that trade: its revenue follows build rates and content wins, and it trades at 9.8x forward earnings against roughly 16.6x two years ago while consensus earnings rose.

Gentex, which builds the electrochromic auto-dimming rearview mirrors, driver-monitoring cameras and full display mirrors that carmakers fit from Zeeland, Michigan, shipped 9.612m mirror units in the September quarter. That is 14.5% fewer than a year earlier, and its automotive revenue fell 3.7%, to $552.5m. Dividing one into the other gives roughly $57.5 of automotive revenue per mirror shipped, against about $51.0 a year ago — a derived figure, and the entire explanation for why a seventh of the unit base went missing without the revenue following.

That gap is the business. Gentex is classified among autonomous-driving and vehicle-electronics suppliers, and over the twelve months to 23 November its shares fell 12.8% while mainstream parts makers in the same table rose — Magna 27.3%, BorgWarner 16.3%. The markdown has been read as autonomy being de-rated. Gentex's revenue is not set by autonomy at all: it is set by how many cars get built and how much equipment the company fits inside each one.

The content wins are real; the component bill is repricing faster

North American automotive revenue rose about 3% against a 5% decline in regional light-vehicle production, and European revenue rose 13% excluding a lost Volkswagen program. Driver and in-cabin monitoring and full display mirror take rates did that work. "This acceleration in growth is being driven by new technology, higher content per vehicle, and a more diversified customer base," chief executive Steven Downing told investors on 23 October, explaining a reaffirmed 2027 revenue range of $2.8-2.9bn — 6-9% growth against primary markets the company expects to shrink about 1%.

The cost side moved the other way. Gross margin fell 100 basis points to 33.4% on precious-metal inflation and weaker operating leverage, and 2026 revenue guidance came down to about $2.65bn after a roughly $35m hit from a Hyundai/Kia union strike that landed mid-launch. Then there is memory, already the story of this quarter across electronics hardware.

"Anywhere from 8x to 10x price increases on a per unit basis. So I mean, literally, a component that cost $4 or $5 this earlier this year is now $40," Downing said on the same call, describing DDR memory and raw printed-circuit-board supply. Guidance carries $10-15m of incremental fourth-quarter cost with recovery not expected until the first half of 2027.

The two names beside it are not having the same quarter

Mobileye, which sells EyeQ vision chips and higher-content assisted-driving stacks, shipped 9.5m EyeQs, up 3.3%, at a $49.10 average system price against $51.70 a year earlier; gross profit still rose 7.4% on revenue down 2.4%, and full-year guidance went up. The shares fell 57% over twelve months, to 0.48x book. Hesai, the Shanghai lidar maker, shipped 729,673 units, up 65.3%, for 34.3% more revenue — about 19% less revenue per unit — with gross margin at 41.0% against 42.1% and fourth-quarter guidance implying growth of roughly 10-15%.

So one shelf holds three different problems. Mobileye's economics improved while its price halved. Hesai is buying platform share with price and watching operating income fall as units compound. Gentex is the only one whose units and margin both genuinely deteriorated, and the only one whose fix is already contracted: content per car, won years ago, landing now.

Its de-rating is a multiple. Forward earnings are capitalized at 9.8x against roughly 16.6x two years ago, while consensus earnings over that span rose; the stock carries a 7.0x enterprise value to EBITDA and an 11.2% trailing free-cash-flow yield. Gentex repurchased 5.7m shares in the quarter at an average $23.44, about a fifth above the 23 November price of $19.54.

The mirror count will likely keep falling as build rates drift lower, which means content per car has to keep outrunning it. The awkward part is that the cheapest items on the bill of materials are the ones repricing — and Downing said the fourth-generation full display mirror is being designed to need no DDR memory at all.
