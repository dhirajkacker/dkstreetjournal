Comfort Systems Says Its Limit Is How Many Electricians It Can Get to the Job Site

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The contractors that install the pipe, conduit and switchgear inside AI data centers have lost between a fifth and a third of their market value since early October, and nothing in their filings turned. Comfort Systems' September quarter grew revenue 53.7% with gross margin flat at 24.8%, on a record $17.81bn backlog that was 97.6% same-store.

EMCOR's one visible margin give-back runs opposite to the labor-squeeze story: US Mechanical gave up 110 basis points to 12.5% because it took on more cost-plus and guaranteed-maximum-price data-center work, trading markup for protection against wage inflation.

Of the three, only IES Holdings has cheapness it earned, with consensus putting next year's EBITDA margin at 13.0% against 16.5% after it bolted on $1.5bn of structural-steel revenue. Comfort Systems at 25.4x forward earnings, against roughly 46x in May, is the price of money at its customers, not a change in its own book.

Comfort Systems USA, which installs heating, cooling, piping, electrical and fire-protection systems in commercial and industrial buildings and fabricates modules off site, closed its September quarter with an order book nearly double a year earlier and a gross margin that did not move. Then its president told investors what decides how much of that book becomes revenue: the number of electricians and pipefitters the company can get onto a job site.

The selloff across mechanical and electrical contracting since early October has been read as the AI build-out cracking at the trade that actually swings the conduit, the first place a capital-spending pause would show. The filings of all three contractors say demand is intact, with records in backlog, margin and guidance at each. What repriced is the cost of capital at the companies signing the work.

Labor is the governor, and the wage premium has not come in

"The governor is not the man... the governor's really just labor. The ability to be able to get the labor to the work," Comfort Systems president Trent McKenna said on the 23 October call. Chief financial officer Bill George, who is retiring after 30 years, put the order book plainly: "Our backlog's the best it is ever been."

Backlog reached $17.81bn at 30 September against $9.38bn a year earlier, an eighth consecutive sequential increase, and $17.39bn of it was same-store, so 97.6% of the record was won organically rather than acquired. Set the $3.75bn sequential gain against the $3.766bn of revenue recognized and the implied book-to-bill is roughly 2.0x. Technology work, the data-center line, reached 59% of nine-month revenue from 42%. Operating margin widened to 16.58% from 15.46%.

The price of hands has not come in either. IBEW Local 26 journeymen in Northern Virginia's data-center corridor are at $62.00 an hour plus a benefit package worth $28.66, construction labor costs in primary North American markets have been rising 8% to 12% a year, and journeyman pipefitters under local United Association agreements run $68 to $72 all-in.

Comfort Systems absorbed none of it at the gross line, which is what a 17% year-to-date modular share and capacity going from more than 4M to a targeted 6M sq ft buys.

EMCOR paid 110 basis points to stop carrying wage inflation

EMCOR, which designs and maintains power distribution, low-voltage networks, process piping and HVAC systems in the US and UK, posted the only visible margin give-back among the three, and it runs backwards to a labor squeeze. US Electrical Construction margin rose 210 basis points to 13.9%; US Mechanical fell 110 basis points to 12.5% on a higher mix of construction-manager roles and guaranteed-maximum-price and cost-plus contracts carrying lower markups, including more complex data-center projects. The company sold markup to make the owner carry material and wage inflation.

Its contracted book supports the trade. Remaining performance obligations reached $17.14bn, up 43.9%, with the largest gains in network and communications, and guidance went to $32.00–33.25 a share. An AI data center carries a mechanical content multiplier of "1.5 to 2" against a conventional facility, chief executive Anthony Guzzi told investors on 30 July. On an unchanged 44,000 headcount, the guidance midpoint implies about $460,000 of revenue per employee against roughly $386,000 in FY2025, a derived 19% gain in output per head.

IES bought revenue that earns less per worker

IES Holdings, which engineers and installs electrical and technology systems for commercial, data-center, industrial and residential customers, grew June-quarter revenue 39.6% to $1.243bn at a record 27.41% gross margin, with non-GAAP backlog near $4.5bn, up 91% since fiscal year-end. On 5 October it closed the largest acquisition in its history, paying about $691m for DBM Global, a structural-steel fabricator with roughly $1.5bn of trailing revenue and about 4,000 employees. Consensus has FY2027 revenue up 48.1% while EBITDA margin falls to 12.99% from 16.53%, leaving earnings per share ahead only 16.8%, and that forecast rests on one analyst.

What actually got repriced

The decline began before the numbers did: Comfort Systems fell 5.89% on 7 October after a Zacks Research downgrade, two weeks ahead of any third-quarter figures, then 9.3% the session after the 22 October report, leaving it 29.1% below its 6 October close.

Meanwhile the buyers' funding tightened. Meta's third-quarter capital spending of $34.91bn exceeded its cash from operations and free cash flow swung to minus $2.12bn from positive $10.63bn, with chief financial officer Susan Li calling capital planning "highly dynamic" and no 2027 figure given, even as the company said its plans are "geared towards maximizing 2026 and 2027 capacity." Further down the buyer list, CoreWeave's unsecured bonds yield close to 16%. Comfort Systems has stretched backlog duration to 18–24 months from roughly nine historically, and nearly half of about 140 US data-center projects slated to open in 2026 have slipped or been canceled on power and parts. A longer book is more exposed to a slip in timing, which discounts the backlog without touching the bid margin inside it.

The spread among the three says the repricing sits with the buyer, not the trade. Over the 30 days to 27 October the most diversified name fell least, EMCOR by 12.6%, while Comfort Systems, the purest data-center exposure at 59% technology revenue, fell 22.3% and IES fell 29.8% on its own steel deal. Comfort Systems now trades at 25.4x forward earnings and 14.1x trailing gross profit, against roughly 46x forward and 27.2x in May; EMCOR sits at 20.8x trailing against 31.4x then. Only IES, cheapest at 16.8x forward, has disclosed margin dilution to justify the gap. Price-target cuts at Oppenheimer, KeyCorp and DA Davidson all still sit far above the market price, which is what multiple compression without an earnings revision looks like.

EMCOR reports third-quarter results before the open on 29 October, with consensus at $8.37 a share. The line that will settle the argument is the order book: a backlog that keeps lengthening while its owners' borrowing costs climb is a different asset than it was in May.

Gartner's new global technology budget forecast lifted the consulting and systems-integration shelf twice in a week, and the number that moved the shares is not the number that pays these companies. Worldwide information-technology spending is forecast to grow 15% in 2027 to $7.35 trillion, published 21 October. Inside it, the services line grows 4.9%, to $1,633bn. The headline rate belongs to data-centre systems, up 55.5%, and software, up 14.6%, on memory-driven hardware inflation that Gartner expects to persist through next year.

The month has been read as proof that the AI de-rating of 2026 was a mistake, and that discretionary consulting budgets are coming back. What the disclosures show is narrower: one of the three large integrators published numbers that turned, the other two did not, and a dollar added to a hardware budget by memory prices buys no billable hour.

Accenture is the one that earned it

Accenture, which sells strategy, consulting and multi-year managed services to large enterprises and governments, reported fourth-quarter new bookings of $22.17bn, up 5% in local currency, a book-to-bill of 1.2 — after a 2% bookings decline and a 1.0 ratio the prior quarter. The composition matters more than the total: managed-services signings hit a record $12.8bn at a 1.4 book-to-bill, while consulting signings of $9.40bn came in at exactly 1.0. The order book was filled by long-duration outsourcing.

It guided fiscal 2027 to 3–6% revenue growth in local currency, with 2 to 2.5 points of that from acquisitions, above estimates. The shares rose 15.9% on 1 October.

"We continue to believe the opportunities related to AI are greater than the impact of AI-related efficiencies in our business," chair and chief executive Julie Sweet told investors on the fiscal fourth-quarter call that day.

The pricing disclosure is less generous. Accenture ended the year with 814,391 staff on 5% headcount growth against 6.5% revenue growth, which works out to roughly $91,100 of revenue per employee, up about 1.4%. On the same call the company said revenue per person would rise again in fiscal 2027 but "at a lower rate in part due to AI", and that in the fourth quarter it "saw lower pricing in many areas of our business." At about 18.0x trailing earnings the stock is cheaper than at four of its last five fiscal year-ends, but 28% above where it closed fiscal 2026 seven weeks earlier, and the step to 16.6x forward implies only the 3–6% earnings growth guided.

Infosys guided the other way on the day the shelf fell

Infosys, India's second-largest information-technology services firm, cut its fiscal-2027 constant-currency growth guidance to 1–2% from 1.5–3% on 23 October, the second downgrade of the year, holding its 20–22% operating-margin range. Order intake went the opposite way: large-deal signings of $5.2bn against $3.6bn in the June quarter, 62% net-new, including one mega-deal above $2bn. Its AI-related revenue grew 29% in the same quarter the forecast came down. Headcount of 332,542 at the end of September is 551 higher than a year earlier, so per-employee revenue of roughly $61,890 is up about 1.2% — won back from attrition, not from price. Infosys is the only one of the three whose forward price-to-earnings of 15.8x sits above its trailing 15.6x: dollar earnings are expected to go slightly backwards, which is what 1–2% constant-currency growth produces.

Cognizant's move came with no numbers at all

Cognizant, the Teaneck-based outsourcer with a largely offshore delivery base, has published nothing since 29 July. Its last print showed trailing-twelve-month bookings up 5% to $29.1bn while bookings signed in the June quarter alone fell 6%, and headcount down 900 in the quarter to 356,700. Annualizing June-quarter revenue over that staff gives roughly $61,500 per employee against $60,030 for 2025 — the best per-head gain of the three, about 2.4%, on a workforce that shrank. The shares rose 19% over thirty days anyway. Barron's, reviewing the week to 16 October, found nearly half the gain came on a single session as money rotated into services while AI and chip stocks sold off, and concluded it "shows up in the multiple." At 15.0x trailing the stock is still below the 18.2x it carried at the end of 2025.

What the month accounts for

Accenture's own bookings, margin and guidance account for Accenture's re-rating. Nothing in the other two companies' filings accounts for theirs, and the research forecast credited for the second leg sits inside Accenture's guide and above Infosys's. The per-head price of the work improved by one to two and a half percent across all three — stabilization, and still below wage inflation, with Accenture's roughly $91,100 seat billing about 1.5 times the offshore seat at the other two. The likelier reading of the rest is rotation into cheap services names rather than a return of discretionary budgets, and the whole shelf fell between 2.3% and 5.8% in the single session of 23 October, with only Infosys reporting.

Meanwhile the bidders for the implementation work have changed. OpenAI launched a deployment company with more than $4bn of initial investment to embed AI engineers inside client organizations, after Anthropic raised $1.5bn for a rival enterprise services business; on 23 October Anthropic was reported to be preparing a European consultancy with Hg, with hubs in London and Berlin.

Cognizant reports on 29 October. It will be the first company in this group to add new information since the rally began, and the only question its own June numbers leave open is whether a quarter of bookings decline was one quarter.

Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, which sells gene-silencing injections for transthyretin amyloidosis, a disease in which a misfolded liver protein stiffens the heart and nerves, told investors in late July that it would collect less this year than it had promised. The cause was not a lost price or a lost prescription. The company had worked through the patients who were easiest to find.

Amvuttra cleared $1.01bn in the June quarter, up 106% from a year earlier and the first time the drug had passed a billion dollars in three months, and it still landed 4% below what analysts expected. Full-year product revenue guidance came down to $4.7–5.1bn from $4.9–5.3bn, the transthyretin franchise absorbing the entire $200m reduction at the midpoint. The first year of the cardiomyopathy launch had been read as proof that an injected silencer would take the heart indication outright. What the cut describes instead is a pool fed by two taps, and the bigger one has closed.

The stock of patients ran out; the flow is slower

Before Amvuttra was cleared for cardiomyopathy, patients who had progressed on oral stabilizers, pills that hold the transthyretin protein together rather than switching off its production, accumulated untreated. They were treated in a rush, and once that queue was drained the second-line add rate reverted to the underlying diagnosis rate. Growth now depends on newly diagnosed patients, where the prescribing order runs against an injection: BridgeBio's stabilizer Attruby booked $222m of US sales in the same June quarter, beating consensus by 6%, and its chief executive Neil Kumar told investors the market is shaping up as "a stabilizer-first market".

Alnylam's answer is that it is winning those starts anyway: 80% of new Amvuttra initiations are now first-line. "Look, clearly, we're not pleased to be lowering guidance," chief executive Yvonne Greenstreet said on the July 30 call. "I think it's really important to emphasize that we believe that the fundamentals driving our opportunity are really strong, particularly market growth and our first-line momentum."

The shares closed at $227.28 on October 22 against $464.90 a year earlier, with 28.3% of that lost in the single session after the guidance cut. The multiple has compressed further than the estimates: 35.0x consensus 2026 earnings of $6.49, against roughly 71.6x on the same estimate a year ago, and 23.6x on 2027's $9.64. Reported revenue grew for a third straight quarter, to $1,290.9m. What fell was the rate at which the patient count is climbing.

Ionis cut its own realized price by design

Ionis Pharmaceuticals, which develops antisense RNA drugs and now sells several of them itself, dropped Tryngolza's annual wholesale cost to $40,000 from $595,000 effective April 1, 2026, ahead of a US decision in severe hypertriglyceridemia, to line up with 2027 payer contracting. US net sales promptly fell to $5m in the June quarter from $32m across the half, even as demand in its approved ultra-rare indication grew. Dawnzera, the other self-commercialized launch, booked $26m, up 63% sequentially, while Spinraza royalties of $53m still dwarf both.

The salesforce is not yet paid for. Operating income swung from $139.8m a year ago to minus $102.3m in the June quarter, and company guidance of $875–900m sits below the $925m consensus still in the models. Then on July 9 the CARDIO-TTRansform trial of eplontersen, run with AstraZeneca, missed its primary endpoint in the same cardiomyopathy market, removing the asset meant to replace partnered royalties; the stock fell 23.9% that day. Cash and short-term investments stand at $2.1bn, from $2.7bn at year-end. At 7.89x forward sales against 8.35x trailing, the de-rating has not made it cheap for a business consensus has losing $574m this year.

Arrowhead's top line is a milestone schedule

Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals' approved RNA-interference drug Redemplo generated about $2.4m in the June quarter against $245.3m of operating expense, roughly 1% of a quarter's spend, with prescriptions "greater than doubling" sequentially, in chief executive Christopher Anzalone's August 4 remarks. About 90% of nine-month revenue of $413m came from non-recurring milestones. That is why its forward price-to-sales of 25.0x is double the trailing 12.3x: consensus has fiscal-2027 revenue falling 28.9% to $330.1m as milestones roll off. Some $1.6bn of cash buys about nine quarters at the current loss rate.

Eleven names, and no single shock

A segment-wide repricing on funding cost or drug-pricing policy does not fit the dispersion. Over thirty days the S&P 500 tracker fell 1.2% while Pfizer rose 8.9% and Moderna 11.0%; within the shelf, Prime Medicine rose and uniQure fell 49% after 48-month data showed its Huntington's benefit slip to 44% from 75% and the US regulator recommended a sham-controlled trial. The 100% Section 232 tariff on patented drug imports, which large pharma is largely exempt from and small biotechs are not, and the most-favored-nation benchmark tying US net prices to eight high-income countries, aggravate every one of these models. Neither explains why three companies broke on three separate days.

Two of the three declines are earned. Ionis lost the trial that justified the cost base it is carrying, and Arrowhead's reported revenue is a payment schedule rather than a franchise. Alnylam's revenue line, by contrast, is still compounding at the rate consensus assumed before the cut, and the thing that deteriorated was the slope of patient additions in the one indication that matters. Judging it needs a quarter in which first-line starts carry the number without a backlog underneath them.

That quarter is the one being reported before the US market opens on October 29.

Green coffee turned back up through October, and the chain that has spelled out how long beans take to reach its income statement is the same one that has guided its comparable sales lower. The tariff that never arrived

Green coffee was exempted from the US reciprocal tariff retroactive to 13 November 2025, and the Trade Representative's final notice spared coffee from the 25% Section 301 Brazil duty that took effect on 22 July 2026, because Brazil supplies close to a third of US green coffee imports and there is no quick substitute. What is left is physical scarcity. December arabica settled at $3.157 a pound on 19 October with stocks in ICE warehouses near a 27-year low.

A $2.2m shop against a $1.4m build

Dutch Bros' June quarter was strong on every line that matters to a unit-growth story: revenue up 32.5% to $550.9m, system same-shop sales up 5.8% on transactions up 1.7%, and guidance raised to a 5%-6% comparable range. Gross margin had already eased 145 basis points to 27.4%. Chief financial officer Josh Guenser has called high coffee prices "the biggest headwind" the company faces, and the reason it has not reached its 30% shop-margin goal, while arguing costs should normalize. That was said on the February call, before October's rally.

Vicor's October quarter answered a question the design-win announcements could not: how much of the 800-volt data-center power transition is billable today. The Andover, Massachusetts maker of modular DC-DC converters and custom power systems reported $197.1m of revenue for its third quarter, and $66.6m of that was royalties on a patent it is asserting against twenty respondents at the US International Trade Commission.

The belief the shares carried into October was that the architecture pays its component suppliers now. NVIDIA's own schedule puts the content later: the 800-volt rack is an optional configuration for Vera Rubin, with broader adoption expected at Rubin Ultra in the second half of 2027, the generation whose Kyber racks draw 600kW. Vicor and Navitas Semiconductor, the two specialists built for that conversion chain, each lost about a third of their market value over the thirty days to 22 October. Each has found a different way to cross the gap to 2027, and only one of them is being paid to wait.

The docket became a revenue line

On 9 October the trade commission instituted an investigation of vertical power delivery modules and computing systems on Vicor's complaint over US Patent No. 10,903,734, naming Delta Electronics, Infineon Technologies, Monolithic Power Systems, Flex, Celestica, Quanta and Hon Hai among the respondents, and seeking exclusion and cease-and-desist orders. The accused products are the systems that power AI accelerators, graphics processors and tensor processing units, which means importers of unlicensed designs need a license from Vicor. Vicor's royalty rates escalate across stages tied to how far that investigation has progressed; it has signed a first vertical-power-delivery license with a leading AI original-equipment manufacturer at stage-two rates, and that customer placed a further significant order at the start of the fourth quarter.

Royalties of $66.6m were up from $30.4m in the prior quarter. Strip them out of both periods and the implied product revenue still rose, from about $113.0m to about $130.5m, a derived increase of roughly 15.5%. Advanced Products reached $139m, up 47.5% sequentially and 70.4% of the total. One-year backlog was $406m at quarter-end, up 7% sequentially with book-to-bill above 1, and gross margin expanded from 58.0% to 68.3%. Licensing added to hardware growth here; it did not replace it.

"So as of yesterday, the backlog is actually closer to $400 million," Patrizio Vinciarelli, Vicor's chief executive, told investors on the 20 October call, marking the figure down slightly from the quarter-end number. The mechanism he described is physical. "Every generation of AI processor is stepping up its current requirements...it is now at the thousand amp level and on its way to thousands of amps," Vinciarelli said on the same call. Vicor's capacity to answer that is dated: equipment deployment at its second fab begins in the second quarter of 2027, funded from $513m of cash without a raise.

The fourth quarter steps down

Guidance is where the composition bites. Product revenue is guided to roughly 10% sequential growth and royalties to a minimum of $43m, which works out to about $186m in total, some 5% below the third quarter. The quarter that beat consensus on both lines, $1.33 against $1.29 of expected earnings per share and $197.1m against $183.7m, is not the quarter being annualized.

The shares rose 7.1% on the print to $204.14. The 13.1% break came two sessions later, on a day the whole AI-power complex fell on data-center financing-cost concerns, with GE Vernova and Bloom Energy each off more than 11% and Vertiv down 8.7%, after Lam Research's chief financial officer Doug Bettinger said supply-chain constraints had held back the December-quarter guide. Over the same thirty days onsemi rose 3.1% and Monolithic Power fell 7.9%, so the de-rating concentrated in the names whose content is dated furthest out.

Navitas has the roadmap and no gross profit

Navitas, a 190-person designer of gallium-nitride and silicon-carbide power devices, is the same timing problem without the licensing bridge. Second-quarter revenue was $10.53m, down 27.3% year on year as it exits mobile and consumer chargers, with negative gross profit in each of the first two quarters of 2026. It guided the third quarter to $13.5m, plus or minus $0.5m, on more than 50% year-on-year growth in high-power markets, and expects AI data centers plus grid and energy infrastructure to exceed one-third of sales by the end of 2026, implying roughly $4m to $5m a quarter. Management describes the 800-volt shift as three staged inflections running from the second half of 2026 through "late 2027 and early 2028". It closed the $232.8m acquisition of Claros on 6 October to reach the final conversion stage inside the processor, and at October's GTC Berlin conference Infineon announced its own gallium-nitride collaboration with NVIDIA for 800-volt racks covering the same 800-volt-to-6-volt sockets.

How much of the fall the numbers explain

For Navitas, most of it. The company trades at 46.5x forward revenue with no gross profit to value, and consensus puts fiscal 2026 revenue at $47.69m, only 4.7% above 2025, with the step up to $72.71m deferred to 2027. The calendar in the de-rating is the company's own.

For Vicor, little of it. The quarter beat, the backlog grew, the margin expanded, and the fall arrived two days later with the sector. What the results do not settle is the quality of a third of the revenue: a royalty stream whose rate rises with the progress of a trade case, against a fourth-quarter total the company itself guides lower. At 45.9x consensus 2026 earnings and 28.7x 2027, the shares have come down from roughly 68x forward in May, when they traded between $273 and $304.

The difference between the two is not the roadmap, which they share, but what each can charge before it arrives. Vicor can bill a patent portfolio at 68% gross margin while its fab waits for 2027 equipment. Navitas reports third-quarter results on 3 November, and has to sell parts.
