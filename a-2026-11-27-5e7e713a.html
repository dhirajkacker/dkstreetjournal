A TSMC Wafer Earns Almost Eight Times the Gross Profit of a GlobalFoundries Wafer

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

Divide revenue by wafers shipped and the three big pure-play foundries stop looking like one industry. TSMC keeps about $7,000 of gross profit on each twelve-inch-equivalent wafer; GlobalFoundries keeps $882; United Microelectronics keeps roughly $655 on a blended selling price it discloses at about $1,900.

That ladder decides who pays for the next fab. TSMC's September-quarter operating cash flow covered its capital spending more than twice over and it has told customers prices rise 3-6% from January. GlobalFoundries expects roughly a third of about $4bn of 2027 net capital spending to come from customer prepayments, plus about $760m of US government grants. UMC is funding $2.0bn this year and a newly approved $3.2bn itself, and its finance chief says depreciation will press on gross margin for two more years.

None of the three cut capacity plans or had forward estimates cut into November's markdown.

TSMC collected roughly $10,500 of revenue on each twelve-inch-equivalent wafer it shipped in the September quarter. GlobalFoundries, which builds microcontrollers, radio-frequency modems and power-management chips on mature and specialty nodes out of Malta, New York, collected $2,935 on each 300mm-equivalent wafer. United Microelectronics, the Hsinchu pure-play that fabricates other companies' designs at 28nm and above, collected about $1,900 — its own disclosed blended average selling price.

November marked all three down almost as a single trade, on the belief that spending on artificial-intelligence capacity had peaked and that the companies owning the fabs would wear it. Their own quarters record the opposite sequence: revenue growth accelerated at all three through 2026, gross margins widened at all three, December quarters were guided higher and none of them trimmed capital spending. What separates them is not the demand they describe but who is paying for the capacity that serves it.

The ladder from $1,900 to $10,500 a wafer

TSMC's gap is mix. Nodes at 7nm and below were 79% of third-quarter wafer revenue, and high-performance computing rose to 68% of the total. Shipments grew 9.6% from a year earlier while revenue in New Taiwan dollars grew 50.9%, which works out to roughly 38% more revenue per wafer — the quarter was priced rather than shipped. At a 66.4% gross margin, about $7,000 of each wafer's revenue stayed as gross profit.

GlobalFoundries kept $882 a wafer on a 30.1% gross margin against 24.8% a year earlier, shipping 647,000 wafers with selling prices up 4.8%. "This marked the first quarter of year-over-year blended pricing improvement since 2023," chief financial officer Sam Franklin told investors on November 4. UMC kept about $655 a wafer, on a record 1.21m wafers and utilization of 92% against 85% in the June quarter; its blended price was flat quarter to quarter, with 22nm reaching 18.3% of wafer sales.

Cash flow, prepayments, balance sheet

TSMC pays for itself. Third-quarter operating cash flow of NT$919.8bn covered NT$443.2bn of capital expenditure 2.1 times, with full-year capex reiterated at $60-64bn and 2026-2028 spending described as significantly higher than the past three years. It is also raising price: customers have been told wafer prices go up 3-6% from January 2027, with 2nm and 3nm-class nodes taking the most. "The gap between demand and supply are still quite big, and that is why we put all our strengths to increase the capacity," chairman and chief executive C.C. Wei said on October 15.

GlobalFoundries shares the bill. "We now expect net CapEx investments of approximately $4 billion into our manufacturing footprint in 2027, while continuing to deliver our free cash generation targets," Franklin said; about a third of that is to be supported by customer prepayments for incremental capacity, alongside roughly $760m of CHIPS Act grant money and a 35% investment tax credit. Third-quarter free cash flow of $136m already included about $150m of prepayments and grants. Chief executive Tim Breen put the book behind it plainly on the same call: "We are absolutely booked out through 2027, increasingly booked out through 2028."

UMC carries its own. The $2.0bn 2026 cash capex budget sits on its balance sheet, as does a newly board-approved $3.2bn for silicon photonics, deep-trench capacitors and thin-film lithium niobate, with first installations late in 2028. "For gross margins for next two years at least, depreciation, along with the rising raw material cost, is going to put pressures on our overall financial gross margin," chief financial officer Chitung Liu said on October 28. Chief executive Jason Wang's answer is price: a blended increase of about 10% year over year beginning in the first quarter of 2027.

The markdown nobody's estimates followed

Through the thirty days to November 25, TSMC fell 19.3%, GlobalFoundries 19.6% and UMC 31.2%. Three-quarters of TSMC's decline landed in two sessions, November 5 and November 20, the latter the day Nvidia dropped about 14% as the artificial-intelligence trade broke down over returns on the data-center build-out. Nvidia itself was down 30.9% over the same window. The de-rating belonged to the whole complex.

What it left is three different price levels. TSMC trades at 17.3 times trailing gross profit, against 21.9x in August, and about 14.7 times FY2027 consensus earnings. GlobalFoundries is at 9.7 times trailing gross profit against 13.8x in August and 1.67x book, having touched a 52-week low near $34.05 on November 25 as brokers cut targets — Needham to $55 from $80, Wedbush to $45 from $72 — while consensus FY2027 earnings of $2.55 a share still imply 28% growth. UMC's headline 13.0 times trailing earnings is the least reliable number of the three: a NT$30.05bn investment gain in the June quarter, an equity-portfolio mark, accounts for roughly NT$11.9 of its NT$33.95 trailing earnings per share, which puts the core figure nearer 19-20x.

So the compression sits in the multiple, and the operating record under it is intact: TSMC's October revenue rose 59.7% year on year and UMC's 18.3%. The difference the per-wafer arithmetic exposes is protection rather than growth. TSMC sets price on capacity it funds from its own cash flow. GlobalFoundries' volume sits under long-term agreements carrying take-or-pay reservations and prepayments, including a five-year, $2bn silicon-interposer agreement with TSMC that ramps in 2028, so a capacity pause elsewhere lands partly on its customers' balance sheets.

UMC has no such cushion. Its relief arrives with the January price list. The depreciation on the fabs it is building out of its own cash arrives whether the demand does or not.
