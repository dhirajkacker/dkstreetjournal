Playtika's Record Margin Rests on an Apple Link-Out Fee the Supreme Court Has Not Set

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The cheapest part of running a mobile casino game right now is the part that is still in litigation. Both Playtika and DoubleDown Interactive have moved their best payers onto web shops reached by links out of their apps, where Apple's commission is currently zero because no replacement rate has been approved, and Apple has asked for 27%.

That fee holiday, not demand, is what produced the records. Playtika's direct-to-consumer revenue hit $275.1m in the September quarter, 40.8% of the total, and its adjusted EBITDA margin reached 37.4% while revenue was flat and average daily active users fell 13.4%. DoubleDown is further along at 58% of social-casino revenue and a 42.7% margin, on revenue down 1.6%.

Only one set of shareholders keeps the exposure: DoubleDown's minority is being cashed out at $16.25 per American depositary share in December.

Epic Games asked the Supreme Court on 13 November to uphold the contempt order that stops Apple charging a commission on purchases made through links out of its apps, and Apple is arguing for a 27% fee on those same purchases, according to the filing reported that day. Two social casino publishers have spent two years herding their highest-spending players through exactly those links, and the commission they pay there is, for the moment, nothing.

Playtika's September quarter was read as proof that the margin story in social casino games works. It does work. What it runs on is a rate nobody has set: the Ninth Circuit held in December 2025 that Apple can charge some commission on link-out purchases and sent the question back for amendment, and the Supreme Court declined to pause the contempt order on 6 May 2026. Until a replacement number is approved, US developers link out free.

The margin is real; the volume behind it is not

Playtika, the Herzliya publisher of Slotomania, Bingo Blitz and Disney Solitaire, put $275.1m of revenue through its own web shops in the third quarter, up 31.4% year on year and 40.8% of the company total. Adjusted EBITDA margin hit a record 37.4% on $252.1m. "When we discussed DTC a year ago, we set a goal of reaching 40% on a run rate basis within two years," chief financial officer Tae Lee told investors on 5 November. "We reached it well ahead of that timeline."

Revenue was flat, down 0.1% to $673.8m. Average daily active users fell 13.4% to 7.1 million and average daily paying users 5.9% to 333,000, while revenue per daily active user rose to $1.03 from $0.89. Part of the margin is simply spending withheld: sales and marketing was $167.8m, roughly a quarter of revenue, after a 33.6% sequential cut, and a $10.8m Israeli research tax credit helped too. Management guides to more than $400m of free cash flow this year against a market value of $884m.

The company further down the same road is leaving

DoubleDown Interactive, the Seoul publisher of DoubleDown Casino and the Duelz and Los Vegas gambling brands, has pushed 58% of social-casino revenue onto direct channels, from 21% a year earlier, and its adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 42.7% as revenue fell 1.6% to $94.3m. "It is definitely true that we have fewer players today than we've had in the past," chief financial officer Joseph A. Sigrist said on 11 November. The backdrop is contraction: Eilers & Krejcik estimate the social casino market shrinking 5.6% this year to $6.4bn.

Its 67% owner DoubleU Games is buying out the minority for $16.25 per American depositary share in cash, up from the $11.25 it proposed in April. The offer expires 11 December with settlement expected 21 December and carries a non-waivable condition that a majority of unaffiliated holders tender; the shares sit 5.8% under the price. At 6.9x trailing earnings with $545.3m of net cash against a $759m market value, the asset is cheap, and the public is being removed from it.

What the shelf's gain actually was

The Seoul-controlled name is up 75% over twelve months, almost all of it on the April proposal and the November agreement. Playtika is down 38% over the same span, trended lower from early September until mid-November, and has given back 15% from its 10 November high, including an 8% fall on 20 November. One tender offer and one post-earnings bounce account for the category's move; neither company's operating trend does.

The cleared risk is the state one: Oklahoma's ban on redeemable dual-currency sweepstakes took effect 1 November and, like California's, expressly exempts free-to-play social casino games, which is what both run. The uncleared risk sits in Playtika's own filings: a $550m revolver maturing March 2027 and a $1.9bn term loan maturing March 2028, with its controlling shareholder seeking Chinese National Development and Reform Commission approval to raise the replacement debt. At 11.8x forward earnings against consensus of $0.665 a share for 2027, the gap is priced as a financing question.

DoubleDown's holders find out in December whether they are paid. Playtika's find out, at some later date and from a court, what their best revenue line costs.
