Three Ways to Get Paid for AI Rack Power, and Only Vicor's Royalties Face a Court Test

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

A federal appeals panel is deciding whether boilerplate in Foxconn purchase orders already licensed the patent behind Vicor's fastest-growing revenue line, and the answer sets the size of a stream the company does not have to manufacture.

Three suppliers of rack power to artificial-intelligence data centers were marked down together in November after all three reported accelerating revenue. They earn money in different ways. Vicor booked $66.6m of royalties inside $197.1m of September-quarter revenue, and its own fourth-quarter guidance implies total revenue falls to roughly $186m. Advanced Energy's semiconductor-equipment half grew 64% year on year to a record, with December-quarter gross margin guided to 42–42.5%. Littelfuse's organic growth accelerated from 9% to 22% across 2026.

Only Vicor has a disclosure-level reason for its de-rating: the court, and a royalty-driven sequential decline. At 30.2x forward earnings it is still roughly double the other two.

A federal appeals panel spent 3 November on the fine print of purchase orders for power converters, and what it decides sets how much revenue Vicor can keep billing without shipping anything. The question in Vicor v. ITC is whether clauses buried in purchase orders from Foxconn affiliates already granted them a licence to the patent that underpins the company's royalty income. The International Trade Commission found a licence had been formed, on the strength of that boilerplate plus one Vicor salesperson's email about shipping dates. Judge Dyk called the clause ridiculously broad at argument and floated a narrower reading limiting it to the items actually sold, an outcome that would shrink the royalty base rather than erase it.

That matters because the power-conversion layer was sold as one trade this autumn and is three businesses. Vicor, which makes brick-format DC-DC converters and licenses its vertical-power-delivery topology; Advanced Energy Industries, which sells plasma power supplies to chipmakers and power shelves to hyperscalers; and Littelfuse, whose Electronics segment ships fuses and circuit protection into data-center racks, all reported accelerating revenue and record orders in the month before the markdown. How each gets paid decides where the markdown has a reason attached.

A royalty line with a court attached

Vicor booked $66.6m of royalty revenue in the September quarter, up 119% sequentially, inside total revenue of $197.1m. That is a third of the top line arriving without a shipment. The hardware behind it is growing too: implied product revenue of about $130.5m was up roughly 16% sequentially, and Advanced Products reached $139m. "So as of yesterday, the backlog is actually closer to $400 million," chief executive Patrizio Vinciarelli told investors on 20 October, updating a $46m quarter-end figure.

The fourth-quarter guide is the awkward part. Product revenue was guided up 10%-plus sequentially against a royalty floor of $43m assuming no new licences, which works out to roughly $186m in total, a sequential decline of 5–6% caused by the licence line rather than by demand. The enforcement machine that produces those licences is active: the Commission instituted Investigation 337-TA-1526 on 9 October, naming 20 respondents including Delta, Infineon, Monolithic Power Systems, Flex and Foxconn, and an earlier case ended in February 2025 with a limited exclusion order barring unlicensed imports of infringing modules. Monolithic Power, which took the Nvidia H100 socket from Vicor, is a respondent in the new case and reported no demand deterioration of its own.

Advanced Energy's cyclical half turned out to be the fast half

The fear about Advanced Energy was that semiconductor equipment, on its own capex clock, would swamp the data-center ramp. It did the opposite: semiconductor revenue grew 64% year on year to a record $322m, outrunning data center's record $256m, inside total revenue of $696m. December-quarter gross margin is guided to 42–42.5%, up from 42.1% non-GAAP in the third quarter. "Our Q4 guidance puts us on a run rate to exceed our 2030 target model, 3 years ahead of our original plan," chief financial officer Paul Oldham said on 2 November.

The company repurchased about $30m of stock in October at roughly $241 a share, some 27% above the 20 November close. Brokers cut targets anyway while citing the strong semiconductor and data-center results, with the shares at a 52-week low. At 15.3x forward earnings against 25.6x trailing, the market is paying for the step-up already in guidance and nothing beyond it.

Littelfuse grows without ever naming the number

Littelfuse is the mildest faller and the hardest to size. Organic growth accelerated from 9% in the first quarter to 14% and then 22%, with the Electronics segment up 27% organically and its adjusted EBITDA margin at 33.1%. "Organic growth contributed 22%. The Basler acquisition contributed approximately 6%. And foreign exchange was neutral," chief financial officer Abhishek Khandelwal said on 28 October, which settles the question of whether the growth was bought. Data-center design wins ran at twice the prior-year level, more of them in higher-voltage systems.

What the company has never disclosed is dollar content per server. Management says the vast majority of data-center revenue still sits in low-voltage systems and that growth comes either way on the voltage outcome, which is a claim about direction and not about magnitude. The trailing earnings multiple is unusable after a 2025 net loss of $71.7m; at 19.4x forward earnings and a 5.8% trailing free-cash-flow yield, it is the only one of the three generating meaningful cash while it grows.

One session did most of it

The falls were wildly unequal: over the 30 days to 20 November, Vicor dropped 39.6%, Advanced Energy 22.4% and Littelfuse 7.8%. The single session of 20 November took Vicor down 11.6%, Advanced Energy 10.4% and Littelfuse 6.8%, the day Nvidia fell 14% and the Nasdaq Composite 5.28%, with no company news at any of the three.

So the reported numbers account for almost none of the move, and the split in what is left has a cause. Vicor's compression, from roughly 68x forward earnings in May 2026 to 30.2x, corrects a price that assumed near-perfect execution, and it is the only one of the three whose next quarter declines and whose largest growth line sits in front of a court. Advanced Energy and Littelfuse carry no equivalent disclosure: both guided margins up, and at 7.5x and 7.7x forward gross profit they are priced at roughly half Vicor's 14.8x. The macro reason for a de-rating is a financing question, not a cancelled order; S&P Global Ratings expects all six largest hyperscalers to post negative free operating cash flow in 2026 and 2027 as cash goes out ahead of revenue.

The architecture everyone is being priced against has not shipped yet. Nvidia's 800-volt direct-current racks enter production with the Kyber platform starting in 2027, and none of these three appears on its published silicon or power-system partner rosters. Vicor begins deploying equipment in its second fab in the second quarter of 2027, funded from $513m of cash. The judges will have answered the licence question first.

Originating hypothesis

category emerging bear power delivery revenue model · subject: VICR, AEIS, LFUS

The power-conversion layer — the components that step hyperscaler rack voltage down to the chip — is the one tech-hardware shelf on this universe snapshot rolling over at gradual rather than violent intensity (Power Conversion & Control averages -14.4% over 30 days against +28.6% over twelve months, a markdown a fraction of the -31% to -35% taken by memory and enterprise servers on the same snapshot), and the question is mechanical rather than directional: establish how each of these businesses actually gets paid as racks move from 48-volt to 800-volt DC architecture, since Vicor increasingly earns licence and royalty income on a patented power-delivery architecture it does not have to manufacture, Advanced Energy books data-center power shelves alongside a semiconductor-equipment business running on a different capex cycle, and Littelfuse sells low-ticket circuit protection whose content per rack rises whichever architecture wins — so one de-rating is being applied to a royalty stream, a capex-cycle business and a content-per-unit business at once.
