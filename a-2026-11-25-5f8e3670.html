Vistra and NRG Sold Their 2027 Power Forward Before Texas Froze Data-Center Hookups

Hypothesis Opus 5 · Research Opus 5 · Writing Opus 5 · Prompt v1.7

The two power companies most exposed to Texas have lost a third and nearly half their market value over twelve months, and neither has cut a number. Vistra had hedged roughly 98% of expected 2027 generation as of 30 October; NRG is 75% hedged in the Texas grid and 90% in the mid-Atlantic grid for 2027, with minimal coverage beyond.

Both initiated 2027 guidance above 2026 in the week after the 4 November selloff that followed Texas electing a governor pledged to keep data-center approvals frozen. The business does not explain the move: 2027 margin is already sold, so what has repriced is 2028 and later.

NRG's de-rating does more rational work. Its post-2027 book is open and $3.2bn of new plant capex sits inside the state's permitting pause, while Vistra's 2027 cash flow is contracted.

Texas turned its data-center boom into a permitting question this autumn, and the consequence landed on the two power companies with the most electricity to sell in the state. On 4 November, the session after Texas elected Democrat Gina Hinojosa governor on a pledge to keep data-center approvals frozen until the Legislature passes consumer protections, Vistra closed down 13.8% and NRG down 14.6%. KeyBanc's Sophie Karp cut Vistra to Sector Weight, citing policy uncertainty for suppliers to large industrial and data-center customers.

Then both companies reported. Vistra, a Texas generator with about 38,700 MW of gas, nuclear, coal, solar and battery capacity that also sells power retail to roughly 4.3 million customers, filed on 6 November; NRG, the Houston retailer-generator behind the Reliant, Direct Energy and Green Mountain brands, followed on 9 November. Each initiated 2027 guidance above 2026. The freeze was read as a cut to merchant power earnings for 2027. It cannot reach 2027 at all.

A 474-gigawatt queue and a pause on hookups

On 3 August Governor Greg Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission and grid operator ERCOT to audit data centers in the interconnection process, and ERCOT paused energization of new large loads pending that audit, which covers roughly 474 GW of pending requests against an ERCOT peak of 91.3 GW. The audit filing is targeted for 10 December. Forward Texas power prices already embed about 6 GW a year of new grid-connected data centers, per Jefferies, so a slower queue drags them down; the Energy Information Administration cut its 2027 Texas load-growth forecast from 13% to 5% after the pause. BNEF has warned the audit could delay 49.8 GW of data-center load.

Two hedge books that close the year in question

As of 30 October, Vistra had hedged approximately 98% of expected 2027 generation and about 78% of 2028. NRG told investors it is 75% hedged in ERCOT and 90% hedged in the mid-Atlantic PJM market for 2027, with minimal coverage beyond, and that legacy LS Power hedges cap its PJM upside next year. No Texas price, recovered or crushed, reaches those volumes.

That is why the guidance went up rather than down. Vistra initiated 2027 adjusted EBITDA of $7.7-8.5bn against a narrowed 2026 range of $7.1-7.5bn. NRG guided to $5.75bn adjusted EBITDA and $9.05 adjusted earnings per share, with $460m of organic growth absorbing $185m of net wholesale price pressure and $100m of extra emissions-compliance cost. NRG also reaffirmed 2026 guidance with Texas power prices running roughly 40% below its original planning assumptions.

The delivered quarters were strong on both sides. Vistra's revenue rose 27.7% to $6.35bn and adjusted EBITDA hit a record $2.378bn, of which generation supplied $2.280bn and retail $98m. Reported net income of $1,140m included a $559m unrealized hedge gain on positions settling in later years, so the accounting flatters delivered margin. NRG's revenue rose 24.0% to $9.47bn and adjusted earnings reached $4.68 a share.

NRG is building a plant inside the pause

Every announced artificial-intelligence megawatt lands after the hedged window. NRG's first Bring Your Own Power project, a 1.2 GW combined-cycle plant under a 15-year contract with an investment-grade hyperscaler, costs about $3.2bn and is targeted for commercial operation in late 2029; its GE Vernova pipeline of 5.4 GW is dated 2029 to 2032. "My conviction in this project has only strengthened... Since August, we successfully addressed the remaining items. The project is on track, and I fully expect approved agreements and an announcement by year-end," chief executive Robert Gaudette said on 9 November. On the same day NRG agreed to sell six gas plants totalling 4.9 GW to LS Power for $4bn cash, at what Gaudette called "8.0x expected 2027 adjusted EBITDA"; the new 2027 guidance does not reflect that sale.

Vistra's growth sits equally late: coal-to-gas conversions and the Permian Peaker from 2028, the Amazon Web Services ramp at Comanche Peak through 2032, nuclear uprates from 2031, none of it in baseline guidance. "We have line of sight to approximately 70% growth in adjusted free cash flow before growth per share from 2027 to 2030," chief financial officer Kristopher Moldovan said on 6 November. Capacity payments, meanwhile, are collared by regulation: PJM's 2028/2029 auction cleared at the $325 per MW-day cap, down from $333.44, while landing 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement.

The markdown is not in the estimates

Vistra trades at 14.5x forward earnings and 16.1x trailing, against roughly 25x trailing in August. On 2027 consensus of $9.91 a share it sits at 12.2x, against 16.7x on the same estimate at the start of January. NRG is at 10.2x forward versus 16.0x trailing, and its 2027 consensus of $9.69 runs above its own guided $9.05. Constellation Energy, the merchant generator with the most contracted book, is at 19.7x forward.

The moves have not been orderly. Vistra fell 9.1% on 22 October and 7.2% on 20 November; NRG fell 6.5% that session, rose 11.3% on 23 November and fell 7.0% on 24 November. Regulated utilities did not participate: Public Service Enterprise Group gained 4.0% over thirty days and Dominion 3.6%, while Talen Energy, whose nuclear fleet sits in PJM, rose 6.0%. This is a Texas policy repricing rather than a rates story.

So none of the move is explained by results, and none by estimates, which rose. What has been repriced is the first unhedged year. On Vistra's side that is 2028, where management is deliberately more open in ERCOT pending load hook-ups and whose 2030 outlook assumes no Texas price recovery. On NRG's side it is everything after 2027, against a plan that assumes $52-53 per MWh around-the-clock power and a $3.2bn plant whose customer load needs state approval to arrive. The same discount is doing different work on each: insurance against a timing gap at Vistra, and against an open book plus committed capex at NRG.

The audit filing due on 10 December will say how much of the Texas queue is real. Both companies will collect their 2027 margin either way, which is precisely why the argument has moved to the years they have not yet sold.
